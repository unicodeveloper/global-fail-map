# The Diovan trials

## Scope and Geographic Distribution

Between **2002 and 2014**, a major clinical research data fabrication scandal unfolded across Japanese universities involving **five major medical institutions** conducting valsartan (Diovan) trials. While the trials were distributed across five prefectures—**Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine (Kyoto), Jikei University School of Medicine (Tokyo), Chiba University (Chiba), Shiga University of Medical Science (Shiga), and Nagoya University (Aichi)**—regulatory enforcement occurred in **Tokyo** through Tokyo prosecutors and the Japanese Ministry of Health, and **Tokyo-based Jikei University School of Medicine** was a primary site [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan) [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841).

## What Was Attempted (2002 onwards)

Five major Japanese universities launched large-scale clinical research programs—officially termed "investigator-initiated trials"—comparing valsartan (Diovan) against competing antihypertensive drugs [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841). These trials aimed to demonstrate that Diovan offered benefits beyond simple blood pressure reduction: specifically, prevention of **strokes, angina, and heart failure** [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841).

**Economic significance:** By the time of scandal exposure, Diovan had achieved extraordinary commercial success. Annual global sales exceeded **\$100 billion US dollars**, making it one of Novartis's blockbuster medications [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841). In Japan specifically, Diovan became a **"huge hit"** based on the published findings from these trials [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841).

**Key publications generated from these trials:**
- The **Jikei Heart Study**, published in *The Lancet* in 2007, examined Diovan's effects in high-risk hypertensive patients [[29]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/250920689_Tampered_Data_Cast_Shadow_on_Drug_Trial)
- Multiple articles published in *European Heart Journal*, *International Journal of Cardiology*, and other peer-reviewed journals from Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine research [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal)
- The **Kyoto Heart Study** comprised six articles published across multiple journals [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal)
- The **SMART (Shiga Microalbuminuria Reduction Trial)** and related analyses examined valsartan's effects on albuminuria in diabetic patients [[26]](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/02/novartis-diovan-scandal-claims-two-more-papers/)

## Hidden Novartis Involvement and Financial Connections

Novartis maintained a facade of non-involvement. Initially, the company claimed that **Nobuo Shirahashi**—a Novartis employee—held only "an adjunct position" at Kyoto University and "merely gave advice on what type of statistical analysis to use" [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan). This characterization proved misleading.

**Financial connection:** In **March 2014**, *The Mainichi* newspaper revealed that Novartis had invested **¥100 million** (approximately £595,000 at 2014 rates) directly in Kyoto University's research [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan). When pressed, Novartis later admitted that "one of our former employees [presumably Shirahashi] had varying levels of involvement in five investigator initiated valsartan trials in Japan" [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan).

**Undisclosed conflicts:** Researchers leading the studies—particularly at Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine and Jikei University School of Medicine—failed to disclose their financial relationships or consulting arrangements with Novartis in published papers [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal).

## The Central Figure: Nobuo Shirahashi

**Identity:** Nobuo Shirahashi, age 63, was a former Novartis statistical analyst [[28]](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(14)61015-1/fulltext). He participated in research collaboration with Kyoto University "in the early 2000s" while employed by Novartis [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan).

**Role in fabrication:** Shirahashi was identified as responsible for **"5 statistical analyses during the trials"** [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841). His method involved **"passing on falsified data to researchers,"** not conducting the fraud in isolation [[28]](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(14)61015-1/fulltext). The data was manipulated to exaggerate Diovan's benefits—specifically, to support claims of secondary benefits beyond blood pressure reduction (stroke prevention, angina reduction, heart failure prevention) [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841) [[24]](https://www.aaps.ca/blog/novartis-faces-criminal-charges-in-japan-diovan-scandal).

**Arrest and prosecution:**
- Detained for questioning several weeks before **July 2014** [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan)
- Re-arrested on or shortly before **July 3, 2014** for "further questioning" as additional evidence emerged [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan)
- Faced criminal charges under Japanese law for manipulating data used to promote Diovan [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan)
- Maximum penalties: **two years imprisonment, ¥2 million fine** (approximately £11,500), or both [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan)

## Detection: Yoshiki Yui's Letter to The Lancet (April 2012)

The scandal's exposure began not with internal Novartis review but with external scientific scrutiny. In **April 2012**, Japanese professor **Yoshiki Yui** wrote a letter to *The Lancet* raising concerns about data quality in Diovan research papers [[23]](https://www.classlawgroup.com/diovan-investigation-novartis-blood-pressure-drug-data-altered). This communication triggered the cascade of retractions and investigations that followed.

## Retractions and Journal Response (2012-2014)

The scale of retraction was unprecedented:

- **December 2012:** Japanese Circulation Society retracted **two papers** by cardiologist **Hiroaki Matsubara** (Kyoto Prefectural University), citing "serious errors in data analysis." One paper from 2012 claimed Diovan helped diabetics avoid heart disease; another from 2011 claimed benefits for high-risk hypertensive patients [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan).

- **2012-2013:** *European Heart Journal* retracted the major **Kyoto Heart Study** [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan) [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal).

- **September 2013:** *The Lancet* retracted the **Jikei Heart Study** (originally published 2007), following investigation finding "evidence of data manipulation" and a researcher's failure to disclose Novartis affiliation [[26]](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/02/novartis-diovan-scandal-claims-two-more-papers/).

- **April 2014:** *Diabetes Care* retracted an article on valsartan effects in diabetes. Investigation by **Shiga University of Medical Science** found critical data discrepancies: **10.1% of albumin-to-creatinine ratio (ACR) data** reported in the article did not match original patient records [[26]](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/02/novartis-diovan-scandal-claims-two-more-papers/). In the valsartan group specifically, **12.5% of ACR data** differed from patient records; in the amlodipine comparison group, **8.1% differed** [[26]](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/02/novartis-diovan-scandal-claims-two-more-papers/). Critically, "ACR values reported for the valsartan group were smaller, and those for the amlodipine group were larger, than values reported in patient records; these differences 'worked to the advantage of valsartan.'" Shiga University concluded the article was "inappropriate for [a] scientific paper" [[26]](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/02/novartis-diovan-scandal-claims-two-more-papers/).

- **April 2014:** *Hypertension Research* (Nature Publishing Group) issued a retraction notice for "Impact of Renin-Angiotensin System Inhibition on Microalbuminuria in Type 2 Diabetes: A Post Hoc Analysis of the Shiga Microalbuminuria Reduction Trial (SMART)" [[26]](https://retractionwatch.com/2014/04/02/novartis-diovan-scandal-claims-two-more-papers/).

- **Multiple journals** (International Journal of Cardiology, American Heart Association journals) retracted additional Diovan studies [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan) [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal).

**Total documented retractions:** At least **six articles** on the Kyoto Heart Study alone, plus **five retractions** by the American Heart Association, and multiple additional retractions across cardiology and diabetes journals [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal) [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan).

## Regulatory Response: Civil to Criminal Charges

**September 2013:** Novartis issued a public apology to the Japanese public, acknowledging data fabrication allegations [[23]](https://www.classlawgroup.com/diovan-investigation-novartis-blood-pressure-drug-data-altered).

**October 2013:** Novartis released an investigation report (dated July 29, 2013) claiming **"no evidence of willful manipulation or falsification of data in these studies"**—a conclusion widely criticized as insufficient [[25]](https://www.yakugai.gr.jp/en/topics/topic.php?id=841). The company acknowledged that a Novartis employee had been involved in the studies but stopped short of accepting responsibility for the falsification itself.

**January 2014:** Japan's **Ministry of Health, Labor and Welfare** invoked pharmaceutical law banning false or misleading advertising, asserting that there was **no proof of Diovan's preventative powers** and that the data used to support such claims had been manipulated [[24]](https://www.aaps.ca/blog/novartis-faces-criminal-charges-in-japan-diovan-scandal).

**July 2014:** **Tokyo prosecutors** announced criminal charges:
- **Against Novartis AG (parent company):** Failing to oversee the work of employee Nobuo Shirahashi. Maximum penalty: ¥2 million fine [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan).
- **Against Nobuo Shirahashi:** Manipulating data used to promote Diovan. Maximum penalty: **two years imprisonment, ¥2 million fine, or both** [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan).

## Clinical and Market Impact

**Hospital response:** At least **eight hospitals in Japan** ceased prescribing Diovan to patients in the immediate aftermath of the scandal's disclosure [[23]](https://www.classlawgroup.com/diovan-investigation-novartis-blood-pressure-drug-data-altered).

**What did NOT happen:** Despite the severity of the fraud, no causal finding of safety problems or lack of efficacy for blood pressure reduction was established [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal). The scandal centered on fabricated evidence of *secondary benefits* (stroke prevention, angina reduction), not on the drug's primary indication for blood pressure reduction. The sources establish that Diovan is not proven unsafe or ineffective—only that claims of expanded benefits were based on false data [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal). A separate issue emerged: in 2013, Japanese regulators required Novartis to add warnings about five potentially life-threatening skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, erythema multiforme, pemphigus, pemphigoid), though this appears unrelated to the data fabrication scandal [[27]](https://www.peoplespharmacy.com/articles/diovan-valsartan-sparks-a-new-scandal).

## What Survived the Scandal

**Novartis:** The company continued global operations. Criminal charges and fines, while significant, did not result in market exclusion or dissolution. The company paid the criminal fine and absorbed reputational damage but remained licensed to sell pharmaceuticals in Japan and internationally.

**Valsartan as a drug:** The active pharmaceutical ingredient valsartan survived the scandal. The underlying drug mechanism and blood pressure-lowering efficacy were not disproven—only the fraudulently inflated claims about secondary benefits were retracted. Generic valsartan and other ARBs (angiotensin II receptor blockers) continue to be prescribed worldwide.

**Academic institutions:** Kyoto Prefectural University of Medicine, Jikei University School of Medicine, Shiga University, Chiba University, and Nagoya University continued operations. None were closed or dissolved. However, their reputational standing in international medicine was damaged, and institutions faced ongoing internal investigations into conflicts of interest and researcher conduct [[22]](https://pharmafile.com/news/189387/novartis-faces-data-fraud-charges-japan).

## Systemic Context and Structural Failures

The scandal exposed broader vulnerabilities in Japanese clinical research governance. **Medwatcher Japan** and other observers identified structural problems:

1. **"Cozy ties between pharmaceutical companies and university researchers"** creating conflicts of interest [[1]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.345.6192.17)
2. **Post-marketing studies regulated more loosely** than pre-market safety and efficacy trials [[1]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.345.6192.17)
3. **Inadequate oversight** of industry-sponsored research at universities [[1]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.345.6192.17)
4. **Shortage of trained clinical research managers** and biostatisticians [[1]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.345.6192.17)

In **2012**, member firms of the **Japan Pharmaceutical Manufacturers Association** gave university scientists **\$4.8 billion**, nearly double the budget of the education ministry's grants for individual researchers, creating potential dependency relationships [[1]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.345.6192.17).

The Japanese government responded by forming a clinical research advisory panel in **2014** to produce regulatory recommendations. **Prime Minister Shinzo Abe's administration** planned an NIH-equivalent agency (planned for **April 2015**) to coordinate public and private research translation efforts, with training funding for clinical research managers [[1]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.345.6192.17).
