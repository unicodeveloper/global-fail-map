# The Ryugyong Hotel

The most visible failure in Pyongyang is the Ryugyong Hotel, a 105-story pyramid-shaped structure that dominates the city's skyline as what the Guinness World Records recognizes as the world's tallest unoccupied building [[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryugyong_Hotel). At 330 meters (1,083 feet) in height, the hotel stands as a monument to postponed completion, with construction beginning in August 1987 and remaining fundamentally unfinished as of September 2026—a period spanning 39 years [[1]](https://www.mirror.co.uk/travel/asia-middle-east/abandoned-16billion-hotel-doom-north-31266911) [[4]](https://www.travelbook-magazine.com/places/the-gigantic-north-korea-hotel-that-has-been-abandoned-for-over-30-years).

## Construction Timeline and Funding Collapse

Construction commenced in 1987, heavily subsidized by the Soviet Union, with the explicit goal of showcasing North Korean engineering supremacy to the world [[5]](https://listoften.com/expensive-abandoned-megaprojects-world/). The structural frame was completed by 1989, and the building reached its full height by 1992, at which point approximately \$750 million had been spent [[2]](https://www.thedailybeast.com/north-koreas-best-building-is-empty-the-mystery-of-the-ryugyong-hotel/) [[6]](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/ryugyong-hotel). However, the building's completion became impossible after the Soviet Union's collapse in 1991 and the subsequent withdrawal of Soviet economic support [[5]](https://listoften.com/expensive-abandoned-megaprojects-world/) [[6]](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/ryugyong-hotel).

The 1990s brought compounding crises. The 1994–1998 famine devastated the North Korean economy [[50]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_North_Korean_famine), forcing the government to redirect resources to survival rather than prestige construction. The Ryugyong Hotel languished with an completed exterior shell but entirely unfinished interior, standing vacant for decades [[4]](https://www.travelbook-magazine.com/places/the-gigantic-north-korea-hotel-that-has-been-abandoned-for-over-30-years).

In a bid to resurrect the project, Orascom Telecom—the Egyptian mobile operator that won a contract to build 3G networks in North Korea—invested approximately \$180 million in the early 2000s to complete the building's exterior cladding and install LED displays [[17]](https://www.lemondededemain.fr/en-dressant-105-etages-de-beton-au-dessus-de-pyongyang-des-1987-les-nord-coreens-ont-litteralement-change-la-silhouette-de-leur-capitale/) [[18]](https://dailygeekshow.com/a-pyongyang-cet-hotel-de-105-etages-attend-toujours-son-premier-client-39-ans-apres-son-lancement). These cosmetic improvements masked a fundamental reality: the interior remained entirely unfinished, with no functional rooms, plumbing, electrical systems, or other essential hotel infrastructure [[10]](https://beijingpost.com/gnnrzr-north-korea-s-ghost-hotel-that-never-hosted-a-tourist) [[17]](https://www.lemondededemain.fr/en-dressant-105-etages-de-beton-au-dessus-de-pyongyang-des-1987-les-nord-coreens-ont-litteralement-change-la-silhouette-de-leur-capitale/).

## Structural and Financial Obstacles

Beyond funding shortages, the Ryugyong Hotel faced persistent structural problems that deterred completion. The building exhibits crooked or misaligned elevator shafts, raising concerns about safety and long-term structural integrity [[2]](https://www.thedailybeast.com/north-koreas-best-building-is-empty-the-mystery-of-the-ryugyong-hotel/). These defects, combined with the sheer scale of the unfinished interior (the hotel was designed for approximately 3,000 rooms), created a financial and technical liability rather than an asset [[7]](https://www.cnn.com/style/article/ryugyong-hotel-architecture-origins/).

Market analysis revealed a deeper problem: North Korea's tourism capacity could not justify a 3,000-room luxury hotel. The country receives approximately 4,000 Western visitors and 20,000 Chinese visitors annually [[2]](https://www.thedailybeast.com/north-koreas-best-building-is-empty-the-mystery-of-the-ryugyong-hotel/) [[7]](https://www.cnn.com/style/article/ryugyong-hotel-architecture-origins/)—an insufficient market to sustain such a massive facility. This structural mismatch between ambition and actual demand has persisted throughout the project's history.

## Attempts at Completion and Foreign Withdrawal

In 2008, French cement company Lafarge took control of Orascom's cement division and became one of the largest foreign investors in North Korea [[47]](https://www.lemonde.fr/asie-pacifique/article/2008/12/26/en-coree-du-nord-lafarge-s-est-engage-a-achever-l-hotel-fantome-de-pyongyang_1135442_3216.html), signaling renewed interest in Ryugyong's completion. However, progress stalled. By 2011, only exterior cladding had been finished; by 2013, the Kempinski luxury hotel chain announced it would not operate the facility despite being contracted to do so [[11]](https://www.youngpioneertours.com/development-of-ryugyong-hotel-suspended/), citing insurmountable operational challenges [[14]](https://www.rfa.org/english/news/korea/ryugyong-hotel-pyongyang-casino-gambling-north-korea-construction-07262024180700.html).

In 2016, Orascom lost operational control of its North Korean operations entirely due to international sanctions and an inability to repatriate profits [[53]](https://developingtelecoms.com/telecom-business/operator-news/6232-koryolink-no-longer-under-orascom-s-control.html) [[54]](https://lirneasia.net/2016/01/orascom-writes-off-koryolink-which-makes-good-profits-which-cannot-be-taken-out/). The company had attempted to operate both the Koryolink 3G network and broader commercial ventures in North Korea, but the escalating sanctions regime made the investment untenable.

## Recent Developments and Propaganda Use

By 2024, the North Korean government, facing continued inability to finance completion, announced it would seek external investors to finish the hotel and operate its casino [[14]](https://www.rfa.org/english/news/korea/ryugyong-hotel-pyongyang-casino-gambling-north-korea-construction-07262024180700.html). The structure, however, had been repurposed: the exterior LED display system was activated to broadcast state propaganda and messages, transforming the unfinished building into a billboard for regime messaging [[17]](https://www.lemondededemain.fr/en-dressant-105-etages-de-beton-au-dessus-de-pyongyang-des-1987-les-nord-coreens-ont-litteralement-change-la-silhouette-de-leur-capitale/) [[20]](https://www.admagazine.fr/article/pire-batiment-gratte-ciel-coree-nord).

As of February 2026, the hotel remains architecturally unfinished. The status cited by the Beijing Post (dated September 9, 2026) confirms that the building is seeking investors and remains nonfunctional [[10]](https://beijingpost.com/gnnrzr-north-korea-s-ghost-hotel-that-never-hosted-a-tourist). The sources reviewed provide no evidence of renewed interior construction, physical progress on unfinished spaces, or movement toward operation as an actual hotel. The Ryugyong remains what it has been since 1992: an exterior shell serving propaganda purposes, with zero operational hotel functions.

## What Survived

The building's structural shell, reinforced concrete core, and exterior cladding represent the tangible inheritance of this failed vision [[19]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Ryugyong_Hotel). The LED display system functions and continues to broadcast state messages. The Ryugyong has become an iconic landmark and dark tourist attraction, visited by limited international delegations and documented in countless photographs. Its emptiness has become a symbol of North Korean overreach and economic constraint.

What did not survive: an operational luxury hotel, foreign investment confidence in Pyongyang tourism development, or the original vision of a showcase facility demonstrating socialist progress to the world.
