## Better cholesterol numbers, worse outcomes

Torcetrapib was designed to change blood lipids in a promising direction. In Pfizer's ILLUMINATE trial, it did that, but patients assigned to the drug combination experienced more deaths and cardiovascular events. On December 2, 2006, Pfizer announced that it was ending all torcetrapib clinical development after the independent monitoring board recommended stopping ILLUMINATE. [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628) [[2]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800306000334/ex991202.htm)

The study is registered as **NCT00134264**. Its documented status is terminated. That describes the study's lifecycle; the primary publication supplies the evidence for why this particular termination represented a harmful clinical outcome. The word alone would not establish that conclusion for a different trial. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00134264) [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628)

## The hypothesis

Statins such as atorvastatin lower LDL cholesterol and reduce cardiovascular risk. Torcetrapib inhibited cholesteryl ester transfer protein, or CETP, and was expected to add benefit by raising HDL cholesterol while also lowering LDL. ILLUMINATE tested the combination of torcetrapib and atorvastatin against atorvastatin alone in people at high cardiovascular risk. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00134264) [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628)

The distinction between a biological measurement and a patient outcome is the centre of the case. A treatment can produce a large, measurable change in the intended biomarker without improving how long people live or how often they experience serious illness. The trial was necessary precisely because favourable lipid changes could not answer that clinical question on their own. [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628)

## A large randomized test

ILLUMINATE randomized 15,067 participants. The published analysis found substantial increases in HDL and reductions in LDL in the torcetrapib group, together with increased blood pressure and other biochemical changes. Despite the apparently favourable lipid results, the clinical balance was worse. [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628)

The trial was multicentre and international. Its registry lists the sponsor and study locations, but the New York pin in this atlas represents Pfizer's headquarters at the time of the decision, not a specific hospital or an assertion that all participants were treated there. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00134264) [[2]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800306000334/ex991202.htm)

## The stop decision

Pfizer's December 2 announcement said the monitoring board had recommended stopping the trial because of an imbalance in mortality and cardiovascular events. The company suspended torcetrapib development across its programme. This was not merely a slow-recruitment closure or a funding decision. [[2]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800306000334/ex991202.htm)

The later New England Journal of Medicine paper confirmed increased mortality and morbidity. It also left an important question open: the precise mechanism of harm was not settled by the result. Evidence of off-target effects did not eliminate every other possible explanation. [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628)

That uncertainty should not weaken the observed safety finding or be erased by a neat retrospective story. The trial supplied strong evidence that this treatment regimen produced an unfavourable result. It did not, by itself, prove the fate of every other molecule acting on CETP.

## What the trial left behind

The primary outcomes were published in 2007, and the registry records subsequent analyses associated with the trial. Stored data and samples made the terminated study a continuing source of scientific information rather than a vanished experiment. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00134264) [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628)

Other CETP-inhibitor programmes continued. Contemporary scientific discussion distinguished possible torcetrapib-specific effects from the broader therapeutic hypothesis. That is why this pin belongs to a named molecule and a named trial, not to a map entry declaring an entire biological target permanently disproved. [[4]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejme078029) [[5]](https://www.reuters.com/article/world/merck-announces-its-investigational-cetp-inhibitor-mk-0859-produced-positive-e-idUSIN20071004163052MRK/)

## Location and confidence

The location role is sponsor headquarters in New York. Confidence is high in the trial identifier, participant total, December 2, 2006 development halt and adverse clinical outcome, which are supported by the registry, the SEC-filed company statement and the primary trial publication. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00134264) [[1]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/nejmoa0706628) [[2]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800306000334/ex991202.htm)

The interpretation is narrower than a slogan about failure: surrogate markers need validation against outcomes that matter to patients. ILLUMINATE's lasting contribution includes the warning, the data and the unanswered mechanistic questions, not just the abandoned commercial drug.
