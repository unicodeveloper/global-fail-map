# Builder.ai

## Builder.ai: The Collapsed AI Startup (2025)

**Company Background and Valuation**

Builder.ai was founded in 2016 by Sachin Dev Duggal as a London-based software development platform claiming to use artificial intelligence to automate application development [[12]](https://siliconcanals.com/uks-builder-ai-collapses/) [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/). The company raised a total of \$445 million in funding from major institutional investors including Microsoft, the Qatar Investment Authority (QIA), SoftBank, and Jungle Ventures [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/) [[14]](https://restofworld.org/2025/builderai-ai-apps-downfall/). At its peak valuation, Builder.ai commanded a \$1.2 billion to \$1.5 billion company valuation [[10]](https://techstartups.com/2025/05/24/builder-ai-a-microsoft-backed-ai-startup-once-valued-at-1-2-billion-files-for-bankruptcy-is-ai-becoming-another-com-bubble/) [[12]](https://siliconcanals.com/uks-builder-ai-collapses/) [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/).

**The Core Product Claim**

Builder.ai's central promise was encapsulated in its proprietary AI system called "Natasha CodeGen." The company claimed that Natasha could autonomously generate approximately 80% of application code, fundamentally "democratizing software development" by allowing non-technical users to build software without writing code [[10]](https://techstartups.com/2025/05/24/builder-ai-a-microsoft-backed-ai-startup-once-valued-at-1-2-billion-files-for-bankruptcy-is-ai-becoming-another-com-bubble/) [[12]](https://siliconcanals.com/uks-builder-ai-collapses/).

**Financial Deception and Revenue Overstatement**

In mid-2025, evidence emerged of systematic financial misrepresentation. The company had projected 2024 revenues of \$220 million but achieved only approximately \$55 million in audited revenue—a 70% overstatement [[10]](https://techstartups.com/2025/05/24/builder-ai-a-microsoft-backed-ai-startup-once-valued-at-1-2-billion-files-for-bankruptcy-is-ai-becoming-another-com-bubble/). This discrepancy was not a forecasting miss but evidence of deliberate financial inflation.

A complex scheme emerged: Builder.ai had engaged in round-tripping transactions with VerSe Innovation (an Indian venture capital/incubator firm) from 2021 to 2024 [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/). The two entities exchanged invoices for services that were never actually rendered, artificially inflating Builder.ai's revenue figures by up to 300% [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/). These fictitious transactions were recorded in audited financial statements, violating basic accounting standards.

**Debt Crisis and Creditor Action**

By May 2025, Builder.ai faced massive outstanding debts [[9]](https://www.cnbctv18.com/technology/how-a-london-based-startups-artificial-ai-gambit-backfired-ws-l-19613692.htm):
- Amazon Web Services: \$85 million in unpaid cloud infrastructure charges
- Microsoft: \$30 million in unpaid cloud service costs

On May 2025, creditor Viola Credit—a major lender—seized control of the situation by freezing Builder.ai's bank accounts and claiming most remaining funds [[9]](https://www.cnbctv18.com/technology/how-a-london-based-startups-artificial-ai-gambit-backfired-ws-l-19613692.htm) [[17]](https://synergystartup.substack.com/p/builder-ai-what-went-wrong). After Viola's seizure, the company retained only \$5 million, much of which was restricted or in currency-controlled jurisdictions and thus unavailable for operations [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/).

**Leadership Changes and Attempted Recovery**

CEO Sachin Dev Duggal exited the company in February 2025, relocating to Dubai [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/). Manpreet Ratia assumed the CEO position in March 2025 and immediately initiated cost-cutting measures, reducing the workforce from approximately 1,350 employees by cutting 270 staff members [[10]](https://techstartups.com/2025/05/24/builder-ai-a-microsoft-backed-ai-startup-once-valued-at-1-2-billion-files-for-bankruptcy-is-ai-becoming-another-com-bubble/).

In March 2025, Ratia launched a desperate \$75 million insider funding round designed to restructure the company. This effort collapsed when the financial discrepancies became public knowledge [[16]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ai-collapses-into-bankruptcy-at-1-3-billion/).

**Bankruptcy and Employee Impact**

On May 20–22, 2025, Ratia announced bankruptcy in a remote video town hall [[10]](https://techstartups.com/2025/05/24/builder-ai-a-microsoft-backed-ai-startup-once-valued-at-1-2-billion-files-for-bankruptcy-is-ai-becoming-another-com-bubble/) [[12]](https://siliconcanals.com/uks-builder-ai-collapses/). The company terminated approximately 1,000 employees—roughly 80% of the total workforce—within 30 minutes of the announcement [[14]](https://restofworld.org/2025/builderai-ai-apps-downfall/). Builder.ai filed for Chapter 7 bankruptcy in Delaware in late May 2025 [[9]](https://www.cnbctv18.com/technology/how-a-london-based-startups-artificial-ai-gambit-backfired-ws-l-19613692.htm).

**The "Fake AI" Dispute**

A contentious narrative emerged regarding whether Natasha CodeGen represented genuine artificial intelligence or was substantially human-powered labor. The "fake AI" narrative alleged that the platform relied on outsourced developers in India and Ukraine rather than automated code generation, and that internal directives instructed engineers to hide geographic location and manipulate time-stamped updates to suggest UK business hours [[11]](https://www.techspot.com/news/108173-builderai-collapses-after-revelation-ai-hundreds-engineers.html) [[13]](https://www.webpronews.com/builder-ais-collapse-ai-hype-fuels-1-5b-startups-2025-bankruptcy/).

However, analyst Gergely Orosz disputed this characterization in June 2025, arguing that Natasha CodeGen was indeed "a working AI tool built by a dedicated UK team" and that the platform represented a legitimate technical achievement even if the company's financial representations were fraudulent [[11]](https://www.techspot.com/news/108173-builderai-collapses-after-revelation-ai-hundreds-engineers.html). This distinction—between technological failure and financial/operational fraud—remains contested and unresolved in available sources [[11]](https://www.techspot.com/news/108173-builderai-collapses-after-revelation-ai-hundreds-engineers.html).

**Post-Bankruptcy Status and Investigations**

Following bankruptcy, Builder.ai's intellectual property and team assets were reportedly transferred to post-bankruptcy administrators, though no final disposition has been publicly reported [[14]](https://restofworld.org/2025/builderai-ai-apps-downfall/). The US Securities and Exchange Commission and Department of Justice initiated investigations into securities fraud and misrepresentation of AI capabilities [[10]](https://techstartups.com/2025/05/24/builder-ai-a-microsoft-backed-ai-startup-once-valued-at-1-2-billion-files-for-bankruptcy-is-ai-becoming-another-com-bubble/) [[15]](https://medium.com/@tahirbalarabe2/the-truth-behind-builder-ais-1-5b-failure-77817464897e).

**Location and Workplace**

Builder.ai maintained its primary headquarters in London with offices in Shoreditch, East London, a technology hub that has itself experienced significant real estate pressures and startup departures due to soaring commercial rents [[7]](https://www.theguardian.com/media-network/2016/apr/12/startups-abandon-tech-city-commercial-rent-soars-east-london-shoreditch).

**Surviving Elements**

The technical team—regardless of AI authenticity questions—possessed skilled software engineering talent that dispersed across other firms. Intellectual property from the bankruptcy may have been acquired by other technology companies, though no major acquisitions were announced.
