# The Algiers Metro

## Chronology of Conception and Delays

The Algiers Metro represents not abandonment but cascading suspension, each linked to major external shocks. The concept originated in the 1970s as Algerian planners recognized the capital's explosive population growth and traffic congestion. [[18]](https://mapa-metro.com/en/Algeria/Algiers/Algiers-Metro-map.htm) In 1982, a comprehensive 64-kilometer metro network proposal emerged, encompassing three planned lines to serve Algiers's metropolitan area of 3.5 million inhabitants. [[18]](https://mapa-metro.com/en/Algeria/Algiers/Algiers-Metro-map.htm) [[12]](https://www.cockpitdz.com/en/post/the-history-of-public-transport-in-algeria-4-10-the-metro)

## First Major Suspension: Oil Collapse and Civil War

Construction feasibility studies and planning occurred during the 1980s, and the Algerian state created the transport authority (Entreprise Métro Algérien, or EMA) in 1984. Initial construction began in 1990. However, the project encountered its first critical suspension when global oil prices collapsed in 1986. [[9]](https://www.railway-technology.com/projects/algiers-metro/) As Algeria's hydrocarbon revenues evaporated, metro financing became impossible to sustain. By the 1990s, only 4 of 14 stations had been completed during the preceding decade-long stall. [[18]](https://mapa-metro.com/en/Algeria/Algiers/Algiers-Metro-map.htm) The project made minimal progress during the 1988-1999 interval—only 3,500 meters of tunnel were excavated in this entire period.

The situation deteriorated further when Algeria's civil war (1991-2002) erupted, creating a security crisis that made large-scale urban construction hazardous and diverting state resources to internal conflict. [[9]](https://www.railway-technology.com/projects/algiers-metro/) Construction officially discontinued during the civil war years. The metro project remained suspended for over a decade despite engineering work continuing on design refinements.

## Infrastructure and Contractual Difficulties

The engineering challenges proved substantial. The metro required deep underground tunneling through dense urban substrate, compounded by challenging soil conditions. During 1988-1989, Algeria awarded construction contracts to two national companies—COSIDER and GENISIDER—neither of which possessed experience operating large urban transit development projects. [[13]](https://structurae.net/en/structures/algiers-metro) This institutional capacity gap contributed to slow progress and technical disputes.

## Revival, Completion, and Archaeological Complications

Construction resumed in 2003 after the civil war ended, with renewed state commitment and support from international contractors. The metro opened for limited service on November 1, 2011—a 28-year lag between initial 1980s conception and operational reality. [[11]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Algiers_Metro) The completed initial segment (Line 1) spans 9.5 to 13.2 kilometers with 14-19 stations, substantially smaller than the original 64-kilometer three-line vision. [[11]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Algiers_Metro) [[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Metro)

Archaeological complications emerged during construction. Excavation at Place des Martyrs station uncovered remains of Ikosim, an ancient Punic settlement, requiring integration of archaeological preservation into the metro structure itself. This discovery delayed completion further but also created a historically significant transit hub.

## Current Operation and Expansion

The metro now operates with one functioning line and continues to expand. By 2018, the system had grown to 18.5 kilometers with continued expansion planned. [[14]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algiers_Metro) The system carries approximately 88 million passengers annually. [[11]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Algiers_Metro) This represents the eventual success of a project that experienced multiple decades of suspension, yet the final achievement remains substantially scaled-down from the original 1982 vision.
