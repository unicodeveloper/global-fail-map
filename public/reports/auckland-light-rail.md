# Auckland Light Rail

## The Ambition

Between 2017 and January 2024, Auckland pursued an expansive light rail project that represented the most comprehensive attempt to introduce rapid transit since the 1970s Rapid Rail proposal. The scheme envisioned a 24-kilometre partially-tunnelled light rail line connecting Auckland's central business district to the airport via southern suburbs including Māngere. [[7]](https://www.globalconstructionreview.com/new-zealand-cancels-multi-billion-dollar-auckland-light-rail-project/) [[8]](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/government-officially-cancels-15-billion-auckland-light-rail-as-part-of-100-day-plan/QPMW7N743FDBXHDFNY7XWBA2OE/)

The project evolved through multiple design phases, each escalating in cost and scope. Initial estimates from 2017 placed the project at approximately NZ\$9.8 billion. By 2020–2021, revised estimates had climbed to NZ\$14.6 billion for the preferred option. Final figures presented before cancellation indicated potential cost escalation to as much as NZ\$19 billion—nearly double the original estimate and equivalent to NZ\$29.2 billion in some assessments. [[7]](https://www.globalconstructionreview.com/new-zealand-cancels-multi-billion-dollar-auckland-light-rail-project/) [[8]](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/government-officially-cancels-15-billion-auckland-light-rail-as-part-of-100-day-plan/QPMW7N743FDBXHDFNY7XWBA2OE/) [[13]](https://newswire.co.nz/2024/01/aucklands-light-rail-project-cancelled-national-focuses-on-alternative-transport-plans/)

## Investment and Expenditure Before Cancellation

Despite the eventual cancellation, substantial public money was committed to the project before its termination. According to government sources and independent analyses, approximately NZ\$228–229 million was expended on design, planning, community engagement, and initial preparatory work. The government's own "100-day plan" in 2024 detailed that approximately NZ\$165 million went to consultants and contractors on light rail and one other project combined before both were scrapped. [[18]](https://www.1news.co.nz/2024/03/08/govts-100-day-plan-scrapped-projects-and-2-billion-invested/)

Critically, no track was laid and no permanent infrastructure was constructed. The entire expenditure represented planning and preliminary phases. The project consumed time and resources from 2017 through January 2024—seven years of urban policy focus.

## Status and Cancellation

On 14 January 2024, the newly elected National-led coalition government formally cancelled the Auckland Light Rail project as part of its "100-day plan." Transport Minister Michael Wood announced the decision, declaring the project unaffordable within the fiscal constraints his government faced. [[7]](https://www.globalconstructionreview.com/new-zealand-cancels-multi-billion-dollar-auckland-light-rail-project/) [[8]](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/nz/government-officially-cancels-15-billion-auckland-light-rail-as-part-of-100-day-plan/QPMW7N743FDBXHDFNY7XWBA2OE/) [[13]](https://newswire.co.nz/2024/01/aucklands-light-rail-project-cancelled-national-focuses-on-alternative-transport-plans/)

Government statements cited "lack of progress despite significant expenditure" and acknowledged the escalating cost estimates as the primary rationale. [[13]](https://newswire.co.nz/2024/01/aucklands-light-rail-project-cancelled-national-focuses-on-alternative-transport-plans/) The project had existed in planning limbo for months before the decision, with uncertainty about funding and scope preventing construction commencement. [[9]](https://thespinoff.co.nz/society/25-06-2020/derailed-how-aucklands-light-rail-network-went-off-the-tracks)

## Geographic Connection and Infrastructure Intent

The light rail corridor was to run from Britomart Station in central Auckland (where the newly completed City Rail Link terminates) through the city centre via downtown stations, then south through New Lynn, Māngere, and Papakura, with a dedicated branch extending to Auckland International Airport. [[7]](https://www.globalconstructionreview.com/new-zealand-cancels-multi-billion-dollar-auckland-light-rail-project/) [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail_in_Auckland) The line would have integrated with the City Rail Link's new underground tunnel (completed September 2026) to create a comprehensive rapid transit network.

## What Survived

While the light rail line itself was cancelled, its planned route corridor remains designated in Auckland Council planning documents. Transport sector advocates, including a public transport advocate whose submission made it onto the New Zealand Infrastructure Commission's priorities list, have argued that the corridor "is a problem worth solving" and should become Auckland's long-term transport focus. [[14]](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/02/28/light-rail-revival-scrapped-auckland-corridor-a-problem-worth-solving/) [[12]](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/national/582704/scrapped-light-rail-must-become-auckland-s-long-term-transport-plan-advocate-says)

In March 2026, the Transport Minister acknowledged that the public transport corridor from the city centre to the airport "needs to be worked on," suggesting potential future alternatives to light rail, though no funded project has been approved. [[14]](https://www.1news.co.nz/2026/02/28/light-rail-revival-scrapped-auckland-corridor-a-problem-worth-solving/) The government's decision redirected some rail investment toward the City Rail Link completion and other regional initiatives.
