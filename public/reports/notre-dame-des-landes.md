# Aéroport du Grand Ouest

## Genesis and Planning (1963–2008)

The airport proposal emerged in 1963 from regional economic planning assumptions that were never substantively reexamined [[9]](https://grokipedia.com/page/ZAD_de_Notre-Dame-des-Landes). In the 1970s, the regional council officially designated Notre-Dame-des-Landes as the site, responding to anticipated congestion at the existing Nantes-Atlantique Airport [[19]](https://autonomies.org/2017/02/the-zad-an-autonomous-zone-in-the-heart-of-france/). Early opposition came from local farmers and landowners; the site was formally classified in 1974 as a "Zone à Aménagement Différé" (Zone Reserved for Later Development), which legally placed the land in a limbo awaiting future public use [[17]](https://newint.org/node/16380).

By the 2000s, regional authorities framed the airport as economically essential. In February 2008, the project received official approval as a public utility declaration, with an estimated construction cost of **€580 million** and an anticipated opening in 2015 [[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame-des-Landes). The planning phase extended from 1971 through 2008—37 years of technical studies, environmental impact assessments, and administrative procedures. The total expenditure on pre-construction work, studies, and legal procedures reached **€400 million** before a single earth-moving vehicle arrived [[4]](https://www.spacegroup.no/e400-million-wasted-west-of-nantes-the-promised-airport-has-ended-up-as-1650-hectares-of-fields/).

## Geography and Operational Design

The airport was planned for the commune of Notre-Dame-des-Landes itself. Phase one design specified capacity of four million passengers annually, with phased expansion toward nine million capacity through additional construction phases [[14]](https://www.airport-technology.com/projects/newnantes/). The 1,650-hectare footprint encompassed wetland habitat that environmental impact assessments acknowledged would be substantially altered [[4]](https://www.spacegroup.no/e400-million-wasted-west-of-nantes-the-promised-airport-has-ended-up-as-1650-hectares-of-fields/).

Nantes-Atlantique Airport, by contrast, sits on the communes of Bouguenais and Saint-Aignan-Grandlieu, approximately 8 kilometres southwest of central Nantes [[16]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Nantes_Atlantique_Airport). By 2014, DGAC (Direction Générale de l'Aviation Civile, France's civil aviation authority) [[44]](https://grokipedia.com/page/directorate_general_for_civil_aviation_france) technical studies found that the existing airport was expandable to serve 4 million passengers annually, rendering the new airport's 9-million-capacity design redundant for foreseeable demand horizons [[40]](https://www.debatpublic.fr/sites/default/files/2021-04/Final%20report%2018July2019%20CNDP.pdf).

## Occupation and Resistance (2007–2017)

In August 2007, the first major occupation occurred when activists, environmental groups and local residents established what became known as the ZAD de Notre-Dame-des-Landes (Zone à Défendre—Zone to Defend), repurposing the administrative term to mean a space defended against development rather than reserved for it [[8]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZAD_de_Notre-Dame-des-Landes). The occupation expanded rapidly as a heterogeneous coalition: farmers facing land expropriation, ecological activists, anarchist collectives, and social housing advocates consolidated an illegal settlement that would persist for eleven years [[18]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/anti.12809).

By October 2012, the government launched "Opération César," a police operation aimed at dismantling the occupation. The operation succeeded temporarily, but within weeks the zone was reoccupied [[12]](https://crimethincdotcom.wordpress.com/2018/04/13/la-zad-another-end-of-the-world-is-possible-learning-from-50-years-of-struggle-at-notre-dame-des-landes/). The cycle of evacuation and reoccupation persisted through 2017, with each cycle generating further polarisation. In November 2012, approximately 40,000 counter-demonstrators—supporters of the airport and regional development—rallied at Nantes to express backing for the project [[18]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/anti.12809).

## Political Reassessment and Cancellation (2017–2018)

By 2015–2016, political calculations at the national level shifted. A 2016 referendum in the region showed 55% support for the airport locally, but national public opinion polling indicated approximately 70% opposition nationally [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9roport_du_Grand_Ouest). Emmanuel Macron's administration, elected in 2017, positioned itself as environmentally progressive while pragmatic on economic development. The political cost of continuing the airport—legal challenges, ongoing occupation, national environmental criticism—began to outweigh the perceived benefits.

In December 2017, the government commissioned mediators to propose a resolution. On January 17, 2018, Prime Minister Édouard Philippe announced the definitive abandonment of the airport project [[2]](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/world-europe-42723146) [[1]](https://batinfo.com/en/actuality/notre-dame-des-landes-le-projet-daeroport-est-officiellement-abandonne_9333). The announcement stated that "divisions unleashed by the proposed new airport" and lack of broad popular support justified abandonment [[7]](https://www.rfi.fr/en/france/20180117-France-abandons-controversial-Nantes-airport-project).

## The April 2018 Evacuation and Aftermath

In April 2018, approximately four months after the cancellation announcement, police operations removed the remaining ZAD occupants. The operation was extensive: structures on the site included 100 to 150 buildings erected by the occupying community over eleven years [[5]](https://radicalecologicaldemocracy.org/the-airport-project-of-notre-dame-des-landes-is-dead-long-live-the-zad/). The police response deployed significant force; approximately 11,000 projectiles were fired during the operation [[12]](https://crimethincdotcom.wordpress.com/2018/04/13/la-zad-another-end-of-the-world-is-possible-learning-from-50-years-of-struggle-at-notre-dame-des-landes/). Approximately 250–300 persons were displaced, though the precise figure remains contested in sources [[11]](https://brooklynrail.org/2018/07/field-notes/ZAD-The-State-of-Play/).

The post-evacuation settlement proved unexpected. Rather than complete dispersal, the Loire-Atlantique Department proceeded to sign approximately 38 agricultural lease agreements with former occupants and allied farmers, regularising their presence on a portion of the zone [[9]](https://grokipedia.com/page/ZAD_de_Notre-Dame-des-Landes). By 2023, approximately 100–150 residents remained legally present through these leases, operating agricultural activities and maintaining community structures [[6]](https://thepointmag.com/politics/forms-of-life-zadistes/).

## Financial Consequences and Vinci's Compensation Claim

Vinci, through its subsidiary Aéroport du Grand Ouest (AGO), held the concession contract and had prepared for construction management. Following cancellation, Vinci sought compensation from the French state for losses related to the project termination. In March 2024, Vinci filed a compensation claim for approximately **€1.6 billion** (sometimes reported as €1.59 billion) [[28]](https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/pays-de-la-loire/loire-atlantique/nantes/notre-dame-des-landes-la-justice-rejette-la-demande-d-indemnisation-record-de-vinci-suite-a-l-abandon-du-projet-2953142.html), arguing that the state had terminated the concession agreement without proper grounds and without fault on Vinci's part [[32]](https://www.economiematin.fr/vinci-notre-dame-des-landes-etat).

On April 10, 2024, the Administrative Court of Nantes ruled against Vinci's primary claim [[28]](https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/pays-de-la-loire/loire-atlantique/nantes/notre-dame-des-landes-la-justice-rejette-la-demande-d-indemnisation-record-de-vinci-suite-a-l-abandon-du-projet-2953142.html) [[29]](https://www.abcbourse.com/marches/notre-dame-des-landes-la-justice-deboute-vinci-qui-reclamait-1-6-milliard-d-eur_627278) [[30]](https://www.ouest-france.fr/environnement/amenagement-du-territoire/nddl/aeroport-notre-dame-des-landes-pas-de-faute-de-letat-vinci-perd-sa-premiere-bataille-judiciaire-a4e1b834-f713-11ee-b61a-805a85d39d9d). The court determined that the state had not committed a fault (faute) in abandoning the project, and that termination was justified by "general interest" (motifs d'intérêt général) [[30]](https://www.ouest-france.fr/environnement/amenagement-du-territoire/nddl/aeroport-notre-dame-des-landes-pas-de-faute-de-letat-vinci-perd-sa-premiere-bataille-judiciaire-a4e1b834-f713-11ee-b61a-805a85d39d9d) [[31]](https://www.ouest-france.fr/environnement/amenagement-du-territoire/nddl/aeroport-de-notre-dame-des-landes-les-questions-en-suspens-autour-de-lindemnisation-de-vinci-15ded958-f733-11ee-b61a-805a85d39d9d). The court left open the possibility of reduced compensation based on the contract's termination provisions, but rejected Vinci's demand for full loss recovery [[33]](https://www.globalconstructionreview.com/court-rejects-vincis-claim-for-e1-6bn-over-cancelled-nantes-airport/) [[34]](https://www.zonebourse.com/cours/action/VINCI-4725/actualite/France-La-justice-rejette-une-demande-d-indemnisation-de-Vinci-pour-Notre-Dame-des-Landes-46404218/) [[35]](https://www.actu-environnement.com/ae/news/notre-dame-des-landes-convention-concession-rupture-faute-indemnite-etat-vinci-43882.php4) [[36]](https://www.batiactu.com/edito/notre-dame-landes-justice-dit-non-a-demande-dindemnisation-68310.php) [[37]](https://www.mediacites.fr/breve/nantes/2024/04/10/notre-dame-des-landes-pas-indemnisation-pour-vinci-pour-le-moment/).

## The Alternative: Nantes-Atlantique Expansion

After abandoning the Notre-Dame-des-Landes project, the French state committed to modernizing and expanding the existing Nantes-Atlantique Airport. From 2019 onward, the DGAC undertook a major redevelopment initiative. In September 2021, the DGAC confirmed plans to extend the airport's runway by 400 metres [[38]](https://actu.fr/pays-de-la-loire/nantes_44109/aeroport-de-nantes-la-dgac-confirme-l-allongement-de-la-piste-de-400-metres_45041085.html).

By 2023–2024, detailed public consultation on the redevelopment project commenced [[39]](https://www.20minutes.fr/societe/4026846-20230308-nantes-reamenagement-aeroport-dgac-fait-point) [[43]](https://metropole.nantes.fr/actualites/deux-reunions-sur-le-reamenagement-de-nantes-atlantique). The state investment in expansion reached **€450 million** [[15]](https://centreforaviation.com/analysis/reports/nantes-concession-to-be-decided-soon-part-one-no-new-airport-but-existing-one-to-be-improved-651891). As of April 2024, the concession authority launched a **€26 million** work programme for the 2024–2025 period [[42]](https://www.lejournaldesentreprises.com/article/un-programme-de-26-millions-deuros-de-travaux-est-lance-pour-laeroport-nantes-atlantique-2094873). The DGAC continued to release detailed planning documents through 2025, with a fourth interim consultation report issued in March 2025 [[41]](https://www.debatpublic.fr/sites/default/files/2025-03/R%C3%A9am%C3%A9nagement%20Nantes%20Atlantique%20bilan%20interm%C3%A9diaire%2031%20d%C3%A9c%202024%20VF.pdf), indicating that the expansion project remains actively under development rather than abandoned.
