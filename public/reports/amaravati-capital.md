# Amaravati

## The Grand Announcement and Initial Momentum

On October 22, 2015, Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu jointly laid the foundation stone for a greenfield capital city at Amaravati, marking what was framed as a historic restart: 18 centuries after the ancient Mahachaitya had thrived as a Buddhist centre, the site was to be reborn as a modern administrative hub. [[20]](https://www.financialexpress.com/policy/economy-andhra-pradesh-new-capital-amaravati-top-10-things-to-know-155482/) The project was positioned as a template for India's 100 smart cities initiative, a flagship undertaking that would establish India's first purpose-built capital in the digital age. [[19]](https://www.financialexpress.com/policy/economy-top-10-points-about-amaravati-ancient-citys-rebirth-as-modern-andhra-pradeshs-capital-156213/)

The initial master plan, delivered by Singapore's Ascendas-Singbridge consortium in May 2015, proposed a phased development across three zones: the seed capital area (8 square kilometres for initial administration), the capital city proper (122 square kilometres), and the broader Capital Region Development Area (7,068 square kilometres across Guntur and Krishna districts). [[18]](https://www.telugu360.com/a-brief-history-amaravathi-master-plan/) The total scope encompassed 33,000 acres pooled from 2,903 farming families, an unprecedented land-pooling arrangement intended to distribute development gains equitably. [[22]](https://www.financialexpress.com/policy/economy-andhra-pradesh-new-capital-amaravati-how-the-10-lakh-acre-land-pool-problem-was-cracked-155502/)

## Financing Structure and International Partnership

The project's financial architecture depended on international development finance. The planned \$715 million commitment comprised:

- World Bank: \$300 million (Amaravati Sustainable Infrastructure and Institutional Development Project)
- Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (AIIB): \$200 million
- Andhra Pradesh government: \$215 million equivalent

[[56]](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/230719/after-world-bank-china-backed-bank-drops-usd-200-million-loan-for-ama.html)

By 2015–2018, project costs were estimated at \$2 billion USD equivalent or approximately Rs 51,208–51,687 crore in Indian currency. [[21]](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy-politics/story/amaravati-proposed-capital-of-andhra-pradesh-faces-challenge-53397-2015-07-31) The state government had already spent approximately Rs 15,000 crore in upfront investment before the financing crisis of 2019. [[50]](https://www.indiatoday.in/magazine/special-report/story/20191028-capital-chaos-1610235-2019-10-18) The project was positioned not merely as capital construction but as a model for institutional development, environmental sustainability, and inclusive urban planning. [[20]](https://www.financialexpress.com/policy/economy-andhra-pradesh-new-capital-amaravati-top-10-things-to-know-155482/)

## The Inspection Panel Investigation and Compliance Crisis

Beneath the façade of momentum, structural problems emerged. In May 2017, the World Bank's Inspection Panel—an independent accountability mechanism for Bank projects—received multiple representations from landowners and civil society organisations regarding the capital project. [[46]](https://www.cenfa.org/inspection-panels-report-on-amaravati-project-only-validates-the-issues-raised-by-csos/) The complaints centred on three categories of violation:

1. **Involuntary Resettlement:** Farmers were losing productive land with inadequate consultation and compensation frameworks that deviated from World Bank safeguard policies.
2. **Land Acquisition Irregularities:** Notifications issued under hastily enacted state legislation lacked due process protections; land pooling operated under a model that favoured developer interests over farmer protections.
3. **Environmental Concerns:** The capital site sat in a floodplain vulnerable to Krishna River inundation; hydrological impact assessments were inadequate. [[23]](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/world-bank-says-it-dropped-amaravati-project-due-to-centre-withdrawing-finance-request/articleshow/70294727.cms) [[24]](https://scroll.in/article/934122/within-five-years-andhra-pradesh-capital-amaravati-has-gone-from-a-promised-utopia-to-ghost-town)

The Inspection Panel formally investigated and issued its Third Report in July 2019, validating the core concerns raised by civil society. [[46]](https://www.cenfa.org/inspection-panels-report-on-amaravati-project-only-validates-the-issues-raised-by-csos/) The report explicitly documented that the project non-complied with the World Bank's Operational Policies on Involuntary Resettlement (OP 4.12) and Environmental Assessment (OP 4.01).

## The Withdrawal Decision: July 2019

On July 15, 2019, the Government of India formally withdrew its request for World Bank financing of the Amaravati Sustainable Infrastructure and Institutional Development Project. [[47]](https://www.indiatoday.in/business/story/india-withdrew-request-for-financing-amaravati-project-world-bank-1572038-2019-07-22) The Government of India's official statement provided a procedural rationale: the central government determined that the project conditions had changed and no longer required World Bank support. [[23]](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/world-bank-says-it-dropped-amaravati-project-due-to-centre-withdrawing-finance-request/articleshow/70294727.cms) [[45]](https://www.news18.com/news/india/dropped-amaravati-project-after-govt-withdrew-finance-request-says-world-bank-tdp-ysrc-engage-in-blame-game-2238067.html)

However, independent analysis and civil society accounts indicate the underlying motivation was distinct. The Inspection Panel investigation was poised to document systematic violations that could have set a precedent for future World Bank scrutiny of India's development projects, particularly the New Capital city plan for Andhra Pradesh. The government withdrew the financing request to forestall international oversight—a decision that effectively allowed the project to proceed without external compliance review, but at the cost of losing \$300 million in concessional finance. [[40]](https://www.cenfa.org/world-bank-pulls-out-of-amaravati-capital-city-project-a-major-victory-to-people-activists-say/)

The World Bank formally dropped the project on July 18, 2019. [[42]](https://www.indiatoday.in/india/story/world-bank-drops-chandrababu-naidu-s-amaravati-project-1571057-2019-07-19) [[43]](https://www.ndtv.com/india-news/amaravti-andhra-pradesh-world-bank-drops-300-million-amaravati-project-2071861) [[44]](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/world-bank-pulls-out-of-amaravati-construction-project-65719)

## The AIIB Exit and Financial Collapse

The Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank had committed \$200 million as co-financier. When the World Bank withdrew on July 18, the AIIB, faced with the same compliance documentation and lacking independent financing incentives to remain, withdrew its \$200 million commitment on July 23, 2019—just five days later. [[54]](https://www.livemint.com/news/india/asian-infrastructure-investment-bank-withdraws-funding-to-amaravati-project-1563946400320.html) [[57]](https://www.ndtv.com/andhra-pradesh-news/china-backed-bank-drops-200-million-loan-for-amaravati-project-2073871) [[58]](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/after-world-bank-china-based-aiib-drops-200-million-loan-amaravati-105979)

The two lenders together had committed \$500 million. Their withdrawal removed the critical financing floor that made the project economically viable. The state government could not absorb the gap: Andhra Pradesh was already under fiscal stress following the partition of the state in 2014 (when Telangana was separated), and the YSR Congress Party government, which came to power in May 2019 elections, inherited both the ambitious capital project and a severely constrained budget. [[60]](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/finance/china-led-development-bank-joins-world-bank-in-pulling-funds-for-amaravati/articleshow/70344481.cms)

## Project Suspension: June 2019 Onwards

On June 1, 2019 (days after the YSR Congress victory), the Andhra Pradesh chief secretary issued a sweeping order: all projects awarded before April 1, 2019, that had not achieved 25 per cent completion would have their contracts cancelled. [[1]](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/infrastructure/andhra-govt-set-to-pull-the-plug-on-infra-projects-worth-rs-50000-crore/articleshow/69605925.cms) This administrative order suspended the Amaravati capital project and approximately Rs 30,000–50,000 crore in other pre-election infrastructure contracts, effectively placing the entire development under hold. [[1]](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/infrastructure/andhra-govt-set-to-pull-the-plug-on-infra-projects-worth-rs-50000-crore/articleshow/69605925.cms)

Construction activities ground to a halt. Multiple projects that had commenced—including officer housing, government buildings, and foundational infrastructure—were frozen mid-work. By February 2025, when construction resumed under a new government, workers discovered hundreds of deteriorated cement bags and construction materials that had been exposed to the elements for five years, creating a "ghost town" impression. [[9]](https://www.ap7am.com/en/95777/amaravati-revamp-uncovers-abandoned-cement-bags-and-construction-materials)

## What Survived: The High Court Building

One structure was completed before the freeze: the Andhra Pradesh High Court building, which was inaugurated on February 3, 2019 (before the election and the suspension order). [[64]](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/amaravati-to-remain-andhra-capital-till-aug-14-hc-stays-govt-order-on-3-capitals/story-GrM22zNWgQzcROa59V4GDN.html) The building began operations on March 18, 2019. [[64]](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/amaravati-to-remain-andhra-capital-till-aug-14-hc-stays-govt-order-on-3-capitals/story-GrM22zNWgQzcROa59V4GDN.html) This single structure stands as evidence that the capital city project was not entirely stillborn—basic administrative infrastructure was functional even as the broader vision was dismantled.

Land acquisition notifications were also withdrawn. In March 2024, the state government signalled its intention to scrap land acquisition notifications for 4,300 acres issued in 2016, citing litigation and changed policy priorities. [[53]](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/vijayawada/amaravati-land-acquisition-likely-to-be-scrapped-by-government/articleshow/108252918.cms) Farming families who had pooled their land lost the unified capital guarantee that had legitimized their land contribution; during the 2019–2024 suspension, 4,422 landless families had their pensions stopped. [[26]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932024_Amaravati_protests)
