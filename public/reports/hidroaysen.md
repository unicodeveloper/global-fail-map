# HidroAysen

## What Was Proposed

HidroAysén represented the most ambitious hydroelectric project in South American history at the time of its conception. The megaproject aimed to construct five hydroelectric dams across two of Chile's wildest rivers—the Baker and Pascua—in the remote Aysén Region of Patagonia, approximately 1,500 kilometers south of Colbún. The dams would generate 2,750 megawatts of capacity and required investment estimated at \$3.2 billion. [[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HidroAys%C3%A9n) [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/) [[17]](https://www.deseret.com/2011/5/9/20190583/chile-approves-huge-dam-project-in-wild-patagonia/)

The project's direct geographic connection to Colbún lies through the Colbún S.A. power company, which held a 49 percent stake in the HidroAysén joint venture created in 2006 with Italian-owned Endesa (which held 51 percent). [[12]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HidroAys%C3%A9n) [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/) [[16]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/chile-s-endangered-rivers-1774/) The Colbún S.A. company itself was founded in 1985 to operate the successful Colbún and Machicura hydroelectric complexes, making its corporate headquarters located in the Colbún municipality. [[1]](https://www.colbun.cl/en/corporate/about-us/our-history)

## Why the Project Mattered

The megaproject represented an attempt to address Chile's electricity supply constraints while providing the nation with renewable generation capacity independent of the drought-vulnerable Central hydroelectric system. Proponents argued it would stabilize power prices and enable industrial expansion in the far south. Endesa and Colbún presented the first formal project study in 2007 during President Michelle Bachelet's first administration, and by May 2011 the project received environmental approval from Chile's environmental commission despite intense public opposition. [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/) [[17]](https://www.deseret.com/2011/5/9/20190583/chile-approves-huge-dam-project-in-wild-patagonia/) At the time of environmental approval in 2011, 74 percent of Chile's population opposed the project, according to published polling. [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/)

## Timeline and Regulatory History

- **2006**: HidroAysén joint venture formed by Endesa (51%) and Colbún S.A. (49%)
- **2007**: First formal project study presented
- **2008–2010**: Environmental impact assessment (EIA) conducted
- **May 2011**: Environmental approval granted by Chile's environmental commission, despite public opposition
- **June 2014**: The Chilean government explicitly rejected the project, denying environmental approval continuation following changes in national administration
- **November 2017**: The two partner companies officially cancelled the project, abandoning all further development efforts

The project suffered from a fundamental regulatory vulnerability: environmental approvals in Chile are not permanent. When the government changed political control in March 2014 (transition from President Piñera to President Michelle Bachelet's second administration), the new administration signaled opposition to the project based on environmental and social grounds. By June 2014, formal government rejection closed the pathway to development. [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/) [[14]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/ongoing-suspension-of-hidroays%C3%A9n-confirms-project-infeasibility/) [[25]](https://www.nrdc.org/press-releases/hidroaysen-cancelled-chile-rejects-environmental-approval)

## Environmental and Social Opposition

The project's impact zone covered 5,900 hectares of pristine Patagonian wilderness, including protected areas and river valleys that generated significant ecological and aesthetic value to local communities and Chilean conservation constituencies. [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/) Public opposition was sustained and visible: the project became the focal point of an eight-year environmental campaign waged by national and international conservation organizations, indigenous groups, and Chilean civil society. [[25]](https://www.nrdc.org/press-releases/hidroaysen-cancelled-chile-rejects-environmental-approval)

Opposition centered on several documented harms: The dams would have flooded river valleys with significant biodiversity value; created massive reservoirs in a region famous for wilderness tourism; required 2,400 kilometers of transmission lines through remote areas, further fragmenting habitat; and altered seasonal water flows affecting downstream communities in Argentina (the Baker and Pascua rivers form part of the Chile-Argentina border). [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/) [[14]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/ongoing-suspension-of-hidroays%C3%A9n-confirms-project-infeasibility/) [[16]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/chile-s-endangered-rivers-1774/)

## What Ended and What Survived

The project was definitively cancelled in November 2017 when Endesa and Colbún announced they would not pursue the project further. Approximately \$500 million had already been invested in planning, environmental studies, engineering, and baseline surveys by the time of cancellation. [[13]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/a-blow-to-hidroays%C3%A9n-patagonia-dams-on-hold-8266/) [[14]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/ongoing-suspension-of-hidroays%C3%A9n-confirms-project-infeasibility/) [[25]](https://www.nrdc.org/press-releases/hidroaysen-cancelled-chile-rejects-environmental-approval) This capital was entirely lost—no assets, infrastructure, or intellectual property from the project entered commercial service.

What survived was the clarification of Chile's environmental regulatory limits: HidroAysén established a precedent that even large private investments backed by major utilities could not overcome sustained public opposition and government environmental policy when those forces aligned. The project's failure accelerated Chile's pivot toward renewable energy (solar and wind) rather than large-scale hydroelectric megaprojects.
