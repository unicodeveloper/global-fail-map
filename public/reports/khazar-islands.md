# The Khazar Islands

## Announcement and Vision

In January 2012, Ibrahim Ibrahimov, head of the Azerbaijani real estate developer Avesta Concern, unveiled plans for what would have been the world's most ambitious artificial island development: the Khazar Islands project, positioned 25 kilometers south of Baku in the Caspian Sea. [[16]](https://www.reuters.com/article/uk-azerbaijan-islands/azerbaijan-aims-to-put-up-worlds-tallest-building-idINLNE83A03520120412/) The scheme proposed 41 artificial islands spanning 3,000 hectares (30 square kilometers) extending across undersea shallows of the Absheron Peninsula. [[32]](https://alchetron.com/Khazar-Islands)

The centerpiece was the Azerbaijan Tower, an 1,050-meter-tall structure with 189 floors, designed by an unnamed international team to exceed the Burj Khalifa by 220 meters and become the world's tallest building. [[12]](https://www.skyscrapercity.com/threads/baku-azerbaijan-tower-1050m-3445ft-189-fl-canceled.1477349/) [[13]](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/news/newsavesta-unveils-plans-to-build-worlds-tallest-tower-in-azerbaijan/) The tower was scheduled for completion in 2019. [[15]](https://www.luxuo.com/properties/luxury-locations/azerbaijan-tower-world-tallest-building.html) The entire project promised to house 1 million residents in a planned city comparable to Venice, with 150 schools, 50 hospitals, parks, a university, an airport, 150 bridges, and a Formula 1 racetrack. [[16]](https://www.reuters.com/article/uk-azerbaijan-islands/azerbaijan-aims-to-put-up-worlds-tallest-building-idINLNE83A03520120412/) The total estimated cost exceeded \$100 billion. [[13]](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/news/newsavesta-unveils-plans-to-build-worlds-tallest-tower-in-azerbaijan/)

Ibrahimov described the vision in concrete terms: "One million people will live here. It will be Venice, new Venice." [[16]](https://www.reuters.com/article/uk-azerbaijan-islands/azerbaijan-aims-to-put-up-worlds-tallest-building-idINLNE83A03520120412/) The project appeared to address a fundamental strategic imperative for Azerbaijan—economic diversification away from oil and gas, which accounted for over 90 percent of state export revenue. Baku's existing territory was constrained and densifying; offshore expansion offered space for a generational reimagining of the city.

## Geographic Location and Construction Timeline

The Khazar Islands are positioned at 40°14′29″ North, 49°38′03″ East, approximately 25 kilometers south-southwest of central Baku. [[32]](https://alchetron.com/Khazar-Islands) The project passed state registration on June 13, 2012. [[14]](https://en.trend.az/business/economy/2043075.html) Construction commenced in November 2010—predating the formal announcement—with initial dredging and land reclamation operations visible in satellite imagery and contemporary reports. [[13]](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/news/newsavesta-unveils-plans-to-build-worlds-tallest-tower-in-azerbaijan/)

The planned construction sequence involved three phases: first-phase infrastructure including restaurants, residential, and social facilities (completion slated for 2016); second-phase completion of the Azerbaijan Tower (2019); final-phase buildout of the full island complex (2022). [[13]](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/news/newsavesta-unveils-plans-to-build-worlds-tallest-tower-in-azerbaijan/)

## Collapse: Oil Crisis and Developer Arrest

The project's trajectory turned sharply downward beginning in mid-2014. Global oil prices, which had sustained above \$100 per barrel through 2013, collapsed to \$42 per barrel by March 2015. [[25]](https://www.reuters.com/article/azerbaijan-economy-oil/azerbaijan-puts-brave-face-on-slumping-oil-price-idUSL6N0UV1CN20150116/) [[27]](https://www.reuters.com/article/idUSL6N0V645F/) Azerbaijan's oil export revenues fell by approximately 30 percent. The national economy contracted 3.1 percent in 2016. [[26]](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/022/0052/004/article-A007-en.xml) Government budgets allocated to non-essential infrastructure projects faced immediate suspension.

In parallel, on May 12, 2015, Ibrahim Ibrahimov was arrested by Azerbaijan's Interior Ministry Department for Fight Against Organized Crime. [[28]](https://anews.az/en/biznes/88458/azeri-businessman-who-promised-to-build-worlds-tallest-building-arrested/) The charges stemmed from financial irregularities connected to the Khazar Islands project: Ibrahimov had obtained a state loan of \$57 million from the International Bank of Azerbaijan (IBA) to fund initial development. Facing the revenue crisis, the Avesta Concern could not service the loan, and Ibrahimov was detained on suspicion of fraud, misappropriation of public funds, and large-scale financial malfeasance. [[30]](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-azerbaijan-bank-exchief-idUSKBN0TO0PF20151206/) [[29]](https://www.intellinews.com/clean-up-of-iba-black-hole-reveals-cost-of-azerbaijan-s-crony-capitalism-86518/) The arrest effectively removed project leadership and signaled state loss of confidence in the initiative.

One month after Ibrahimov's detention, on October 10, 2015, the Ministry of Ecology of Azerbaijan declined formal approval for the Khazar Islands project. The stated reason was the developer's failure to complete and submit a comprehensive environmental impact assessment prior to dredging and land reclamation activities. [[33]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Khazar_Islands) This bureaucratic obstacle—entirely justified from an environmental perspective but practically fatal given the prior financial collapse—provided formal regulatory closure.

## Current Status and Remnants

Construction ceased around 2017. As of 2024–2025, the Khazar Islands remain characterized as a "stalled development" or informally as a "ghost town." Partial island structures, channel dredging, and bridge pilings are visible in satellite imagery and occasional visitor reports, but no active construction has occurred for nearly a decade. The project has not been formally cancelled in public announcements; instead, it has been allowed to lapse into permanent suspension. [[31]](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/projects/khazar-islands-azerbaijan/) [[33]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Khazar_Islands) [[34]](https://mapcarta.com/W882979753) [[35]](https://parsalandco.com/khazar-islands-project-azerbaijan/)

What survived the collapse was not the megaproject itself but a recalibrated alternative. Baku White City, initiated in 2011 on reclaimed land within Baku Bay (a much smaller and more modest mixed-use development occupying 221 hectares rather than 3,000), proceeded to completion with state backing after the Khazar Islands collapsed. [[4]](https://architizer.com/projects/baku-white-city/) The successor project retained some urbanism goals of the original vision—mixed residential and commercial use, waterfront development—but at a radically reduced scale and cost.

The Khazar Islands themselves remain submerged, undeveloped, and uninhabited. The only visible legacy is the framework of partially completed infrastructure—silted channels, incomplete island cores, abandoned construction equipment—a monument to overambition that met economic and institutional failure simultaneously.
