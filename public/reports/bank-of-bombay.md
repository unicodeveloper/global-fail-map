# The Bank of Bombay

The Bank of Bombay represents the earliest and most consequential financial failure documented in this research, with cascading effects across India's banking system and shareholder base.

## Origins and Status

The Bank of Bombay was established on **April 15, 1840**, as the second presidency bank in India, following the Bank of Bengal, and preceding the Bank of Madras. [[10]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Bank_of_Bombay) The institution was chartered by the British East India Company and operated as a semi-government bank with headquarters in the Fort area of Bombay (now Mumbai), serving as the primary financial intermediary for merchants, the colonial administration, and cotton traders. [[10]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Bank_of_Bombay)

## The Crisis: Cotton Collapse and Suspension (1866–1868)

Between 1860 and 1866, the American Civil War disrupted cotton supplies to British textile mills, initially inflating the price of Indian cotton and creating speculative opportunity. Mumbai's cotton merchants and traders undertook aggressive leveraging and inventory accumulation. [[9]](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/13e6129c-3dda-4830-b6af-a6a1c2247949) When American cotton resumed shipping in 1866, prices collapsed. The Bank of Bombay, having extended massive credit to the cotton trade, faced cascading defaults.

In **June 1866**, the Bank suspended payments to depositors. [[12]](https://parsikhabar.net/bombay/untold-story-of-bank-of-bombay/15255/) The crisis deepened over eighteen months as the bank attempted resolution. In **January 1868**, formal liquidation proceedings commenced. The shareholder losses were catastrophic: approximately **₹1.89 crore was written off** directly, representing near-total capital destruction. [[13]](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1872/may/03/india-old-bank-of-bombay-government) All documents from the period 1840–1867 were destroyed during the collapse, complicating later historical reconstruction. [[12]](https://parsikhabar.net/bombay/untold-story-of-bank-of-bombay/15255/)

## Institutional Succession and Final Dissolution

The bank's operations were liquidated by 1868, but its institutional legacy persisted through regulatory succession. The government of the Bombay Presidency decided to write off the historical dues and establish a new bank with the same name to replace it. [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Bombay_(1720)) This successor entity operated until it was formally merged into the **Imperial Bank of India on January 27, 1921**. [[14]](https://www.gatewayhouse.in/indian-banking-history/) The Imperial Bank later became the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and subsequently the State Bank of India (SBI)**, preserving banking continuity even as the original 1840 institution ceased independent operations.

## What Survived

Unlike the corporation itself, the banking function and regulatory framework survived and evolved. The failure of the Bank of Bombay prompted institutional reforms and led to the creation of more robust central banking structures. The geographic footprint (Fort area) continues as Mumbai's financial center, though the original Bank of Bombay building and operations are no longer extant.
