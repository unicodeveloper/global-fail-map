# Yung Drung

## Conception and Design (2021-2023)

In 2021, a South African-American entrepreneur named Art Finch, backed by the Thiel-aligned venture capital firm Pronomos Capital, registered his company Yung Drung Inc. with Bhutan's foreign direct investment authority with an audacious proposal: to construct an autonomous special administrative region across approximately 60,000 acres—roughly 2,500 square kilometres—in Gelephu's Sarpang District. [[4]](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2023/12/15/bhutan-futuristic-city-dragon-king/) [[12]](https://dashstartup.com/blog/thiel-backed-startup-dreams-up-futuristic-city-bhutans-dragon-king-delivers/) The project represented a transfer of the "charter city" model, an institutional framework in which a designated geographic territory operates under a legal regime distinct from the host nation's broader legal system, thereby creating what proponents term "jurisdictional competition" for governance quality.

Finch's pitch aligned with the libertarian technological optimism of his backers. Pronomos Capital, itself a venture fund structured to identify and finance charter city and special economic zone opportunities globally, had limited partners including Peter Thiel, venture capitalist Marc Andreessen, and investor Joe Lonsdale. [[4]](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2023/12/15/bhutan-futuristic-city-dragon-king/) The fund's explicit thesis was that sovereign nations might welcome entrepreneurial governance frameworks in designated zones, attracting capital and talent through institutional innovation rather than geographic arbitrage alone.

The proposed Yung Drung project was framed as an "eco-industrial city" intended to function as an economically autonomous Special Administrative Region with independent executive, legislative, and legal authority—a level of autonomy far exceeding conventional special economic zones. [[4]](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2023/12/15/bhutan-futuristic-city-dragon-king/) Finch successfully secured letters of intent from nineteen companies collectively representing over \$2 billion in capital commitments, demonstrating that appetite existed among international firms for such an arrangement. [[4]](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2023/12/15/bhutan-futuristic-city-dragon-king/) The venture raised \$1 million in seed funding from Pronomos Capital to advance the proposal.

## Government Licensing and Rapid Disavowal (2022-2023)

In 2022, Yung Drung Inc. received licensing from the Bhutanese government, progressing from foreign direct investment registration to formal project authorization. [[4]](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2023/12/15/bhutan-futuristic-city-dragon-king/) This legitimacy proved fleeting. In April 2023—less than nine months after receiving government approval—Bhutan's royal government explicitly disavowed the Yung Drung project, severing all association with Finch's vision. [[4]](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2023/12/15/bhutan-futuristic-city-dragon-king/) The government's statement made clear that it would not cede jurisdictional authority or economic control to a foreign entrepreneur and his network of international investors, regardless of the capital inflows or development promises the project carried.

The disavowal was definitive. Art Finch himself later acknowledged the project's termination, referring to it in retrospective interviews as having been superseded by the "Dragon King" (a reference to Bhutan's monarch, King Jigme Khesar Namgyel Wangchuck) building the nation's own version of a megacity in the same location. [[4]](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sarahemerson/2023/12/15/bhutan-futuristic-city-dragon-king/) [[12]](https://dashstartup.com/blog/thiel-backed-startup-dreams-up-futuristic-city-bhutans-dragon-king-delivers/)

## Why It Failed: Institutional Misalignment and Sovereignty Concerns

The Yung Drung project failed not because of inadequate funding, weak demand, or technical infeasibility, but because it represented a fundamental misalignment between the libertarian, investor-friendly institutional framework that Pronomos Capital advocated and the actual preferences and constitutional constraints of a sovereign Buddhist monarchy with deep commitments to national integration and cultural continuity.

Bhutan's government concluded—whether stated publicly or operative as policy—that ceding autonomous legal jurisdiction to a foreign-controlled corporation would:

1. **Compromise national sovereignty** by creating a territory with external governance authority
2. **Risk institutional capture** by international investor interests in a manner inconsistent with Gross National Happiness (GNH), Bhutan's constitutional development philosophy
3. **Subordinate state revenue capture and labor regulation** to private management
4. **Expose the nation to reputational risk** should the zone become a financial hub for capital flight, regulatory arbitrage, or other practices internationally perceived as destabilizing

The government's rapid pivot—from licensing to explicit disavowal within months—suggests that either the scale of autonomy promised in the formal proposal was misjudged during the licensing process, or political pressure from civil society, international NGOs, or India (Bhutan's closest strategic ally) led the government to reconsider its initial approval.

## What Survived; What Was Lost

The Yung Drung Inc. venture itself dissolved, leaving no operational legacy. The \$1 million seed capital and the \$2 billion in letters of intent from international companies were effectively forfeit. Finch's vision of a Bhutanese charter city operated under private governance retreated into the historical record as a footnote to early-2020s techno-utopianism.

However, the project's geographic footprint—the 2,500-square-kilometre designation for Gelephu—was immediately redeployed. Within months of April 2023, in a striking inversion of the initial licensing decision, the Bhutanese government announced its own megaproject for the exact same location: the Gelephu Mindfulness City (GMC), a \$100 billion planned special administrative region announced on December 17, 2023. [[5]](https://time.com/7204652/gelephu-mindfulness-city-bhutan-economy/) [[18]](https://www.bhutanwiki.org/articles/kasho-gelephu-mindfulness-city-2023) Rather than allowing a foreign entrepreneur to develop Gelephu, the monarchy would develop it under state sovereignty, framed explicitly through the language of Gross National Happiness and Buddhist economics rather than libertarian institutional innovation.

By September 2026—today—Gelephu Mindfulness City is actively under construction. The project appointed its chief executive officer in October 2024, began airport construction in April 2026, and raised capital through a nation-building bond in 2025. [[19]](https://www.bhutanwiki.org/articles/gelephu-mindfulness-city) It is not a failed project; it is the repurposing of a failed proposal into a state-led alternative with explicitly opposed philosophical foundations.
