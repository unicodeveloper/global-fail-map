# Xiong'an New Area

## The Gap Between Target and Reality

By 2024–2026, nearly nine years after announcement, the occupancy and population trajectory of Xiong'an had diverged dramatically from projections. Official statistics claim that approximately 120,544 permanent residents now live in the New Area as of the 2020 census [[11]](https://www.bannedbook.org/bnews/cbnews/20260909/2357518.html). However, these figures are misleading. The official count includes the existing population of the three counties—Xiongxian, Rongcheng, and Anxin—which together housed over 1 million people before the project began. Many individuals counted as "new residents" are former residents of rural villages demolished to make way for construction; they were relocated within the same geographic area, not attracted from outside as new migrants [[11]](https://www.bannedbook.org/bnews/cbnews/20260909/2357518.html).

A 2025 report from the Chinese government acknowledged that "over the past seven years, 120,000 people have moved into new housing," but clarified that many of these were local displaced residents rather than new inbound population [[11]](https://www.bannedbook.org/bnews/cbnews/20260909/2357518.html). This distinction is critical: it indicates that Xiong'an has failed to attract net migration. Instead of becoming a destination for people seeking economic opportunity, it has recycled the existing local population into new residential units while struggling to attract external migrants at scale.

Visual documentation from residents and independent observers supports severe structural vacancy. Photographs and videos uploaded by residents document entire neighborhoods with darkened windows, shuttered storefronts, and streets with minimal foot traffic despite complete infrastructure [[13]](https://www.visiontimes.com/2026/03/31/xiongan-branded-a-ghost-city-as-empty-streets-fuel-doubts-over-xis-signature-project.html) [[14]](https://www.msn.com/en-us/travel/news/inside-the-chinese-megacity-worth-60-billion-that-is-now-abandoned-and-the-eerie-reason-why/ar-AA1TmymT) [[40]](https://www.visiontimes.com/2025/12/09/china-now-counts-50-ghost-cities-and-xiongans-empty-streets-tell-a-troubling-story.html) [[41]](https://www.mirror.co.uk/news/world-news/inside-eerie-new-65bn-mega-34832665). Wanda Golden Street, a major retail district in Xiong'an's commercial center, reportedly had zero transfer fees for retail space as of 2025—an unprecedented collapse in demand for high-visibility retail locations historically among the most expensive in Chinese cities [[5]](https://www.newcitiesatlas.com/xiongan/).

## Occupancy Estimates and the "Ghost City" Characterization

Formal occupancy statistics are not published by the Xiong'an administration, creating a data gap. However, multiple independent assessments estimate occupancy rates between 30% and 70% vacancy, depending on district and property type [[5]](https://www.newcitiesatlas.com/xiongan/) [[13]](https://www.visiontimes.com/2026/03/31/xiongan-branded-a-ghost-city-as-empty-streets-fuel-doubts-over-xis-signature-project.html) [[40]](https://www.visiontimes.com/2025/12/09/china-now-counts-50-ghost-cities-and-xiongans-empty-streets-tell-a-troubling-story.html). Kangbashi district, the most developed residential sector, shows visible vacancy ranging from 60–90% based on nighttime lighting analysis and street-level photography [[28]](https://newpolis.media/ordos-ghost-city-navigating-the-pitfalls-of-new-city-development/) [[32]](https://www.dezeen.com/2016/01/10/ordos-a-failed-utopia-china-raphael-olivier-photo-essay/). Rongdong district exhibits similar patterns of incomplete occupancy despite completed construction [[39]](https://en.people.cn/n3/2025/0401/c90000-20296488.html).

The media characterization of Xiong'an as a "ghost city" became widespread by 2025–2026. The BBC described it as a project that "if it succeeds, could become a template for replication across China; if it fails, will completely expose the fundamental limitations of relying on top-down design alone to create innovation and urban vitality" [[12]](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cpd5zj8e3p9o/trad). Vision Times reported that by March 2026, Xi Jinping personally defended the project during an inspection tour, describing it as "completely correct" despite documented public criticism and external skepticism [[15]](https://www.visiontimes.com/2026/03/24/xi-jinping-backs-xiongan-development-despite-heavy-backlash-from-residents.html).

The high-speed rail station, completed and operational since December 2020 with a capacity to handle approximately 100,000 passengers daily, typically operates at a fraction of that capacity, with long periods showing minimal passenger flow [[1]](https://nextmetropolis.com/construction/china-megaproject-xiongan-new-area/). This infrastructure—built to exceptional standards and connected to Beijing by a modern transportation corridor—exists as an emblem of underutilization, a monument to planning that did not account for the absence of compelling reasons for people to make the daily journey.
