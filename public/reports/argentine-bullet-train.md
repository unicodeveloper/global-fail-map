# The Argentine bullet train

## Project Conception and Formal Contracting

On April 26, 2006, President Néstor Kirchner publicly announced the Buenos Aires–Rosario–Córdoba high-speed railway (TAVe—Tren de Alta Velocidad de Argentina, also called the Argentine bullet train) as a flagship infrastructure initiative intended to connect Argentina's three largest metropolitan areas with modern rail transport. [[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires%E2%80%93Rosario%E2%80%93C%C3%B3rdoba_high-speed_railway) The corridor spanned 710 kilometers, with Rosario positioned as the critical intermediate station between Buenos Aires (Constitución) and Córdoba. The project was conceived to provide 320 km/h (200 mph) service, with an 85-minute journey time from Buenos Aires to Rosario—a reduction from the existing approximately 4-hour conventional rail journey. [[10]](https://www.railway-technology.com/projects/argentina/)

The proposed operating fleet consisted of eight Alstom "Cobra" TGV trainsets, purpose-built for Argentina's unique 1,676 mm broad-gauge track specification—a non-standard gauge used nowhere else in the world outside Argentina, Brazil, and Paraguay, which required special engineering and foreclosed standardized procurement. [[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires%E2%80%93Rosario%E2%80%93C%C3%B3rdoba_high-speed_railway) The entire system was designed as a fully electrified, signaled network operated under a turnkey contract model.

The project's cost estimate stood at \$4 billion, with external financing structured as \$2 billion in European loans approved March 27, 2008, from Natixis (a large French bank). [[10]](https://www.railway-technology.com/projects/argentina/) The Veloxia consortium was selected on June 20, 2007, to lead the project, with Alstom formally designated as the primary contractor on January 16, 2008. [[15]](https://www.skyscrapercity.com/showthread.php?t=943190) On April 29, 2008, a turnkey contract was formally signed between the Argentine government and the contractor consortium, establishing binding construction timelines and performance obligations.

## The Global Financial Crisis and Suspension

On February 7, 2009, less than ten months after the turnkey contract was signed, the CEO of Alstom announced that the entire project was being placed "on stand-by" due to the ongoing global financial crisis triggered by the September 2008 collapse of Lehman Brothers. [[15]](https://www.skyscrapercity.com/showthread.php?t=943190) The CEO expressed optimism that the project would be completed "in the future," but no restart date or revised timeline was provided. The suspension came at a critical moment: pre-construction site preparation and engineering design work had begun, but no main construction contracts had been awarded, and no track, stations, or rolling stock had been ordered or manufactured.

## Formal Abandonment and Political Closure

The project remained in suspension through 2009–2012 without material progress. In December 2012, Argentina's Transport Minister Florencio Randazzo publicly rejected the high-speed railway vision, declaring that conventional bus services were sufficient for Argentina's intercity transportation needs and that high-speed rail was an unaffordable luxury. [[9]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires%E2%80%93Rosario%E2%80%93C%C3%B3rdoba_high-speed_railway) This political decision effectively terminated the project—no major infrastructure was ever constructed, and the Argentine government made no further attempts to revive it through alternative financing or contractors.

## Alstom's Corruption Legacy

In December 2014, Alstom pleaded guilty to criminal charges under the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and agreed to pay a record \$772.3 million in criminal penalties to the U.S. Department of Justice to settle charges that the company had systematically paid bribes to government officials in Indonesia and other countries to secure transportation contracts. [[23]](https://www.cnbc.com/2014/12/22/alstom-pleads-guilty-and-will-pay-record-772m-in-bribery-charges-justice-dept.html) [[24]](http://fortune.com/2014/12/22/alstom-to-pay-record-772-million-to-settle-bribery-charges/) [[25]](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-alstom-bribery/alstom-to-pay-record-772-million-to-settle-bribery-charges-with-u-s-idUSKBN0K01DF20141222/) [[26]](https://www.reuters.com/article/alstom-bribery/alstom-to-pay-record-772-mln-to-settle-bribery-charges-with-u-s-idUSL1N0U611V20141222/) While the available DOJ settlement documentation does not explicitly link the TAVe project to the bribery charges, Alstom's demonstrated global pattern of corruption during the exact period (2006–2009) when the TAVe contract was being negotiated and signed raises questions about the integrity of the procurement process, though the formal settlement focuses on Indonesia-specific schemes. [[22]](https://www.debevoise.com/~/media/files/insights/publications/2014/12/fcpa_update_dec_2014_final.pdf)

## Current Status and Geographic Footprint

As of September 2026, the TAVe project remains formally abandoned with no infrastructure constructed. Rosario was designated as the primary intermediate station on the 710 km corridor between Buenos Aires and Córdoba, positioning the city at the geographical and operational heart of the project. No tunnels, embankments, stations, viaducts, or right-of-way acquisition occurred. The only surviving artifacts are the original 2006 announcement, the 2008 financing documentation, the April 2008 turnkey contract, and Alstom's 2014 guilty plea—all of which stand as bureaucratic and legal evidence of a project that was never built.
