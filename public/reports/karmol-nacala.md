# KARMOL Nacala

## What Was Planned

Between 2019 and 2024, a floating liquefied natural gas (LNG) regasification and power-generation facility operated under the commercial name KARMOL was proposed for Nacala Harbor. The facility was designed as an integrated powership solution: a floating storage and regasification unit (FSRU) would receive imported LNG at sea, vaporize it, and feed the resulting gas to onboard electricity generators with a capacity of approximately 50 megawatts, delivering power directly to Mozambique's grid via submarine cable. This approach bypassed the need for shore-based LNG terminal infrastructure and land acquisition—a significant economic advantage in a region with competing development priorities. [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU)

## Geographic and Institutional Context

The project site was positioned in Nacala Harbor at coordinates -14.534204, 40.656408, within the Nampula Province in northern Mozambique. The initiative was part of broader regional energy development alongside competing large-scale LNG export projects (principally Mozambique LNG Area 1 in Cabo Delgado, approximately 300 kilometers north). [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU) [[18]](https://www.mozambiquelng.co.mz/about-the-mozambique-liquefied-natural-gas-project/) [[19]](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/projects/area-1-mozambique-lng-project/) The powership solution targeted electricity supply to the Nacala Corridor region, which serves as a strategic trade route for landlocked Malawi and Zambia. [[3]](https://360mozambique.com/business/infrastructure/nampula-government-to-concession-nacala-port-expansion-and-development-project/) [[8]](https://clubofmozambique.com/news/mozambique-to-concession-nacala-port-expansion-and-development-project/)

## Timeline and Commercial History

**August 2019**: Karpowership, a Turkish marine engineering and power-generation company, announced the proposed FSRU-powership facility for Nacala. [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU)

**2021**: Construction and procurement activities began, with detailed engineering specifications developed. [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU)

**2022**: The designated vessel—a purpose-built powership designed for the Nacala deployment—was diverted from its intended Mozambique assignment and redeployed to Brazil, indicating a shift in Karpowership's commercial priorities or financing constraints. [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU)

**2024 (inferred)**: The project was formally shelved as the vessel did not arrive at Nacala, and no alternative FSRU was secured for the site. [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU)

**August 2024**: Notably, Karpowership re-engaged with the Mozambican government with a revised proposal worth approximately \$1 billion, indicating that the underlying need for gas-to-power capacity in the region remained recognized as strategically important despite the earlier project failure. [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU)

## Why the Vessel Was Diverted and Project Shelved

The research sources do not explicitly document the commercial or operational rationale for the vessel's 2022 diversion to Brazil. Global Energy Monitor, the principal tracker documenting this project, classifies the facility as "shelved (inferred 2 y)"—meaning the abandonment timeline was inferred from vessel tracking data and the absence of any subsequent project news. Possible factors consistent with vessel diversion include:

- **Financing constraints**: Mozambique's sovereign debt context and Nampula Province's creditworthiness during 2020–2022 may have made the Nacala project less attractive relative to higher-margin Brazilian deployments. [[7]](https://d3n8a8pro7vhmx.cloudfront.net/eurodad/pages/2581/attachments/original/1633508342/case-study-nacala-FINAL.pdf)
- **Operational obstacles**: Unspecified regulatory, port-access, or grid-connection obstacles may have delayed or complicated the project at the engineering phase.
- **Commercial opportunity cost**: Karpowership may have identified higher-return deployments in Brazil's growing energy transition agenda during the same period.

The 2024 re-engagement with a \$1 billion proposal by Karpowership confirms that the company did not abandon the Nacala opportunity entirely but rather reallocated vessel resources according to market conditions. [[6]](https://www.gem.wiki/Nacala_FSRU)

## What Survived

The KARMOL FSRU itself did not materialize at Nacala, but the strategic need it was designed to address—grid electricity generation from imported natural gas—has become more acute. A separate gas-fired power plant proposal for Nacala-Porto (Nampula Province) entered public consultation as of September 2025, targeting a 200-megawatt capacity with similar intent. [[20]](https://clubofmozambique.com/news/mozambique-nacala-gas-fired-power-plant-project-enters-public-consultation-286464/) The infrastructure gap that KARMOL was meant to fill thus remains visible in current development planning, and Karpowership's August 2024 proposal indicates that floating regasification-to-power remains a commercially viable technology class for the region.
