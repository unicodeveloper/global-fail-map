# Kiwayu Safari Village

## Resort Operation and Luxury Tourism (pre-2011)

**Kiwayu Safari Village** was a luxury beach resort located on **Kiwayu Island, approximately 30–35 miles (48–56 kilometers) north of Lamu**, within the **Kiunga Marine National Reserve** near the Kenya-Somalia border. The facility offered **18 thatched-roof cottages** with ocean views, providing upscale accommodation to high-end tourists seeking isolated beach experiences. Room rates reached **\$450 per person per night**, positioning the resort in the international luxury segment.

The resort employed approximately **120 permanent and seasonal staff members**, providing wages and economic activity to local Lamu communities. Tourism operations provided foreign exchange earnings and international visibility for Lamu as an upscale destination alongside its historical and cultural significance.

## Security Incidents and Rapid Closure (September–October 2011)

The resort's operational trajectory changed decisively in **September 2011**, when the region experienced a sequence of kidnappings of foreign tourists. On **September 11, 2011**, gunmen attacked accommodations at Kiwayu Safari Village, killing **David Tebbutt** (a British tourist) and kidnapping his wife **Judith Tebbutt**, who was held in Somalia for approximately six months before being recovered.[[55]](https://www.the-independent.com/news/world/africa/briton-killed-and-wife-kidnapped-at-luxury-resort-in-kenya-2353149.html)

Days later, on an unspecified date in late September 2011, a second kidnapping occurred at a different resort near Lamu. On **October 1, 2011**, French journalist **Marie Dedieu** was kidnapped near Lamu by gunmen; she died in captivity in Somalia.[[57]](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Africa/2011/1003/Second-tourist-kidnapping-raises-alarms-on-Kenya-coast-video)

These incidents—two fatal or near-fatal kidnappings within weeks—triggered rapid contraction of international tourism to Lamu and surrounding areas. **Kiwayu Safari Village closed within days of the September 11 attack**. The facility was abandoned as a tourism operation, with **120 staff members placed on unpaid leave or terminated**.[[63]](https://www.dailymail.co.uk/travel/article-2045973/Kenyan-kidnap-hotel-closed-pirate-attack-tourism-ravaged.html)

## Broader Sectoral Collapse and Permanent Closure

The security incidents triggered broader tourism contraction across Lamu:

- **Foreign travel advisories were expanded** from an initial 60-kilometer coastal security zone to a 150-kilometer exclusion zone, encompassing Lamu and surrounding coastal areas.
- **International tourist bookings were cancelled systematically** across all Lamu tourism operators.
- **Hotel occupancy collapsed** at remaining open facilities as tour operators redirected clients to competing East African destinations.[[58]](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna44788475)

Kiwayu Safari Village never reopened. As of 2025, the facility **remained abandoned**, with no operational tourism resumption or facility rehabilitation documented. The physical structures deteriorated in the tropical coastal climate, with minimal maintenance investment by the owner. The resort represents infrastructure that was actively operational and profitable, then abandoned permanently in response to security threats that persisted for years.

The broader tourism sector in Lamu has recovered partially since 2011, with restored international visitor arrivals and hotel operations, but Kiwayu Safari Village itself never returned to service.[[18]](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/coast/128611/lamu-intensifies-efforts-to-market-region-as-key-tourist-destination)
