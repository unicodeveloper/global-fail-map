# African Minerals

## Project Origins and Development (2005–2010)

African Minerals Limited (AML), a London-based mining company, identified the Tonkolili iron ore deposit in Tonkolili District, Northern Province, Sierra Leone. The company developed a large-scale mining operation targeting magnetite iron ore in the Dasogoia, Sambaia, and Simiria chiefdoms of Tonkolili District [[14]](https://www.hrw.org/report/2014/02/19/whose-development/human-rights-abuses-sierra-leones-mining-boom).

AML invested substantial capital in project development. Between project initiation and commencement of full production, the company spent approximately \$2.5 billion developing the Tonkolili asset [[21]](https://www.mining-journal.com/africa/news/1174852/tonkolili-set-expansion). This figure encompasses exploration, feasibility studies, mine infrastructure, port facilities, and workforce training.

## Mining Operations Commencement and Community Displacement (2010–2012)

Mining operations commenced in 2010 [[14]](https://www.hrw.org/report/2014/02/19/whose-development/human-rights-abuses-sierra-leones-mining-boom). In 2010, African Minerals Ltd. forcibly relocated hundreds of villagers to establish the mining operation [[5]](https://ejatlas.org/conflict/african-minerals-limiteds-tonkolili-iron-ore-mine-in-bumbuna-sierra-leone). The scale of displacement was substantial: Human Rights Watch documented that approximately 5,600 residents were forcibly relocated from their communities to make way for the mine [[14]](https://www.hrw.org/report/2014/02/19/whose-development/human-rights-abuses-sierra-leones-mining-boom).

This displacement process was marked by documented human rights violations. Police brutality against female and male protestors continued with impunity, according to Human Rights Watch's 96-page field investigation [[5]](https://ejatlas.org/conflict/african-minerals-limiteds-tonkolili-iron-ore-mine-in-bumbuna-sierra-leone). The relocation was implemented without adequate compensation or consultation with affected communities. In April 2012, police action resulted in deaths among protestors, a critical flashpoint in community opposition to the project [[14]](https://www.hrw.org/report/2014/02/19/whose-development/human-rights-abuses-sierra-leones-mining-boom).

The company's action undermined villagers' access to food security by reallocating ancestral farmland to mining concessions. The company was, at its time of peak operations, the country's largest private employer [[12]](https://www.hrw.org/news/2014/02/19/sierra-leone-mining-boom-brings-rights-abuses).

## Commodity Price Vulnerability and Workforce Crisis (2014–2015)

The global iron ore market experienced severe contraction in 2014–2015. Spot prices for iron ore declined sharply from historical highs, directly impacting project economics. A company dependent on high commodity prices and narrow operating margins became vulnerable to price collapse.

Compounding the commodity downturn, the 2014–2015 Ebola epidemic in West Africa created severe logistical and workforce management challenges. The company faced acute difficulties in maintaining workforce payment and operations continuity.

## Governance Failure and Shandong Engagement (2015)

In March 2015, African Minerals Limited suspended operations, stating it lacked working capital to continue operations [[19]](https://www.reuters.com/article/sierra-leone-mining-african-minerals-idUSL5N0W15P420150227/). The company had failed to negotiate release of funds from its partner Shandong Iron and Steel Group, a Chinese state-owned steel producer [[19]](https://www.reuters.com/article/sierra-leone-mining-african-minerals-idUSL5N0W15P420150227/).

In response to AML's collapse, Shandong Iron and Steel Group acquired the remaining 75 percent stake in the Tonkolili mining operation that it did not already own [[20]](https://www.reuters.com/article/africanminerals-shandong-steel-tonkolili-idAFL4N0XH5LB20150420/). Shandong formally took control by appointing new directors with voting majorities to AML's holding companies [[18]](https://awokonewspaper.sl/sierra-leone-news-shandong-steel-takes-over-african-minerals/).

## Institutional Failure and Regulatory Action (2015–2019)

Following the acquisition by Shandong, operations at Tonkolili remained unstable. In November 2015, AML was suspended from the AIM London Stock Exchange due to lack of adequate financial or governance reporting [[17]](https://www.nma.gov.sl/press-release-on-the-status-of-african-minerals-limited-aml/). The company went into administration (the UK/Commonwealth legal process for insolvency), formally dissolving as a functional entity [[22]](https://www.reuters.com/article/african-minerals-administration/update-1-iron-ore-producer-african-minerals-to-go-into-administration-idUSL5N0W83NH20150306/).

The Shandong takeover, however, did not stabilize the operation. Operations at Tonkolili were suspended in November 2017 [[27]](https://www.fitchsolutions.com/bmi/mining/sierra-leone-government-intervention-bring-iron-ore-production-standstill-22-09-2020). In August 2019, the Sierra Leone government revoked Shandong Iron and Steel Group's mining license, alleging that the company had outstanding financial obligations and was no longer operationally compliant [[27]](https://www.fitchsolutions.com/bmi/mining/sierra-leone-government-intervention-bring-iron-ore-production-standstill-22-09-2020).

## Asset Transfer and Kingho Mining Restart (2020–2021)

In September 2020, the Sierra Leone government, through the Ministry of Mines and Mineral Resources, handed over the Tonkolili Iron Ore mining operations to Kingho Mining Co Ltd, a Chinese-owned firm [[26]](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/chinas-kingho-mining-takes-over-tonkolili-iron-ore-mining-operations-in-sierra-leone/) [[31]](https://subsaharamining.com/chinas-kingho-mining-takes-over-tonkolili-iron-ore-mining-operations-in-sierra-leone/). The transfer was formalized as a lease agreement for the mining concession [[32]](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/head-of-chinas-kingho-energy-group-colin-ding-arrives-in-sierra-leone/).

Kingho Mining received a large-scale mining license in 2020 to operate the New Tonkolili Iron Ore Mines [[30]](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/kingho-mining-starts-full-scale-operation-in-sierra-leone/). The company began full-scale mining operations in March 2021 [[30]](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/kingho-mining-starts-full-scale-operation-in-sierra-leone/), meaning there was approximately a 3.5-year hiatus between license revocation (August 2019) and Kingho's operational restart (March 2021).

Kingho Mining's strategy diverged from AML's production model. Under Kingho's management, the company focused on optimizing the ore grade from the baseline 57 percent Fe (iron) average content [[34]](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/leone-rock-set-to-roll-out-phase-ii-of-tonkolili-iron-ore-project/). This ore-quality improvement strategy reflects both the lessons of commodity price volatility and the need to maximize value per tonne extracted. Leone Rock Metal Group Limited subsequently announced Phase 2 expansion of the integrated iron ore project to further optimize grade, targeting improvement to higher-specification concentrates [[34]](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/leone-rock-set-to-roll-out-phase-ii-of-tonkolili-iron-ore-project/).

## Production and Regulatory Compliance (2020–2026)

Kingho Mining initiated iron ore shipments in 2020–2021. In June 2020, SL Mining, a subsidiary of Gerald Group, announced the first shipment of iron ore concentrate branded "Marampa Blue" from Freetown Port [[28]](https://miningdigital.com/smart-mining/sl-mining-confirms-first-shipment-marampa-blue-iron-ore-concentrate-sierra-leone) [[29]](https://www.mining-technology.com/news/sl-mining-ships-first-iron-ore-cargo/). Kingho's operations proceeded through 2021 with parliamentary ratification of the mining agreement in June 2022 [[33]](https://cocorioko.net/parliament-ratifies-kingho-mining-agreement/).

However, operational controversies persisted. In February 2021, reports surfaced of illegal Chinese shipments of iron ore from Sierra Leone without proper government authorization or documentation, raising questions of whether all ore extraction was being properly reported and taxed [[35]](https://www.thesierraleonetelegraph.com/illegal-chinese-shipment-of-iron-ore-from-sierra-leone-raises-questions-of-impropriety/).

## What Survived

The mining concession, geological surveys, mine infrastructure, processing facilities, and port logistics infrastructure established by African Minerals Limited remained intact and were transferred to Kingho Mining. The Tonkolili deposit itself—the resource base—remained economically viable and is currently being exploited at full scale.

However, African Minerals Limited as a corporate entity ceased to exist in 2015. The human cost of the operation persists: the 5,600 displaced residents remain relocated, and their community land rights have not been restored. A multimillion-pound lawsuit by affected villagers against African Minerals Limited was filed in the London High Court seeking compensation for forced evictions and alleged violent treatment [[13]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/nov/29/sierra-leone-african-minerals-london-high-court).
