## The drug changed a biomarker, not the outcome

Verubecestat was an experimental Alzheimer's drug designed to inhibit BACE1, an enzyme involved in producing amyloid-beta. Merck's EPOCH study tested whether that approach would slow decline in people with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease. On February 14, 2017, the company announced that it would stop EPOCH for lack of efficacy following an external monitoring committee's recommendation. [[1]](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-scientists-publish-new-research-detailing-the-early-development-of-verubecestat-an-investigational-bace1-inhibitor-in-science-translational-medicine/) [[2]](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-announces-epoch-study-of-verubecestat-for-the-treatment-of-people-with-mild-to-moderate-alzheimers-disease-to-stop-for-lack-of-efficacy/)

The trial is registered as **NCT01739348**. Its terminated status belongs to this particular study, not to all Alzheimer's research, all amyloid treatments or every BACE1 inhibitor. The published results explain the clinical disappointment behind the status. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01739348) [[4]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706441)

## What EPOCH tested

The randomized, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 1,958 people. Participants received one of two daily verubecestat doses or placebo. The main study period was 78 weeks, with cognition and everyday function assessed using established clinical scales. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01739348) [[4]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706441)

The choice of population mattered. These were people with established mild-to-moderate disease, not an asymptomatic prevention cohort. A conclusion from this experiment should therefore keep the intervention, dose, stage of illness and measured outcomes attached to it.

Merck had previously published evidence that the compound could reduce amyloid-beta production. That supported carrying out a clinical test; it did not guarantee that people would decline more slowly. [[1]](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-scientists-publish-new-research-detailing-the-early-development-of-verubecestat-an-investigational-bace1-inhibitor-in-science-translational-medicine/) [[5]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scitranslmed.aad9704)

## Why the study stopped

The monitoring committee judged that EPOCH had almost no prospect of showing a positive clinical effect. The announcement distinguished that efficacy recommendation from a requirement to stop for safety. It was a decision about the likelihood of achieving the clinical aim, not evidence that no biological effect had occurred. [[2]](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-announces-epoch-study-of-verubecestat-for-the-treatment-of-people-with-mild-to-moderate-alzheimers-disease-to-stop-for-lack-of-efficacy/)

The final New England Journal of Medicine report found no meaningful slowing of cognitive or functional decline with verubecestat. It also documented adverse events that were more common with treatment. The relevant balance is the whole clinical result, not an isolated favourable change in amyloid measurements. [[4]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706441)

It would go beyond the experiment to say that this alone disproved every version of the amyloid hypothesis. The study demonstrated that these treatment regimens did not deliver the hoped-for clinical benefit in this population. Explaining precisely why requires more than naming the intended target.

## EPOCH was not APECS

Merck initially continued APECS, a separate verubecestat study registered as **NCT01953601**, in people with prodromal Alzheimer's disease. That programme was already under way before EPOCH stopped. It was not a new trial invented in response to EPOCH's result. [[6]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01953601) [[2]](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-announces-epoch-study-of-verubecestat-for-the-treatment-of-people-with-mild-to-moderate-alzheimers-disease-to-stop-for-lack-of-efficacy/)

APECS subsequently stopped in 2018, and its results were published separately. Its population and outcome findings should not be pasted into EPOCH's timeline or presented as if they were one continuous experiment. Together the studies provide a broader record, but the distinction matters for both causality and citation. [[7]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1812840) [[6]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01953601)

## What survived the termination

Both registry entries and the peer-reviewed reports remain accessible. They document study design, efficacy findings and safety observations that later researchers can compare with other interventions. Early development work also remains published, preserving the distinction between successful target engagement and unsuccessful clinical translation. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01739348) [[4]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1706441) [[7]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1812840) [[5]](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scitranslmed.aad9704)

The ending therefore produced evidence, even though it did not produce an effective marketed medicine. A negative result can prevent the same assumption being carried forward untested.

## Location and confidence

The pin represents Merck's sponsor headquarters in Kenilworth, New Jersey, at the time of EPOCH's termination. It does not represent a single trial hospital or imply that all participants were treated in New Jersey. [[8]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/310158/000122520817009615/0001225208-17-009615-index.htm)

Confidence is high in the study identity, February 2017 stop decision and lack of clinical benefit, supported by the registry, company statement and primary publication. The mechanism behind the gap between amyloid reduction and clinical outcome remains a scientific question, not a fact resolved by a map label.
