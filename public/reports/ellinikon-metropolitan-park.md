# The Ellinikon Metropolitan Park

This case involves four distinct phases spanning 22 years, each representing a different status: initial suspension (1995–1997), competition and advanced design (2005–2007), academic feasibility study (2008–2012), and final displacement through privatization (2012–2017).

## Phase A: Initial Park Concept, Suspended (1995–1997)

Following the ratification of the contract to build the new Spata airport (Eleftherios Venizelos), the Greek state initiated planning for conversion of the Ellinikon site into a public metropolitan park. This concept began in **1995** with preliminary research commencing in **1996** [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellinikon_Metropolitan_Park).

The status of this initial park concept was **suspended** in **1997**, not formally cancelled. The cause was the award of the 2004 Summer Olympics to Athens (decided by the International Olympic Committee in 1997). As noted above, the Olympic Games preparation preempted the park development timeline, and the airport site was allocated instead to temporary Olympic facilities—a political reallocation of the site's use rather than rejection of the park concept on technical grounds [[2]](https://greekreporter.com/2019/09/15/the-hellinikon-project-greeces-modern-tale-of-agony-and-ambition/).

## Phase B: International Architecture Competition and Advanced Design (2005–2007)

In **2005**, the Greek Ministry of Environment and the Union Internationale des Architectes (UIA) announced an international architecture competition to redesign the Ellinikon site as a metropolitan park with integrated sustainable development. The competition received **over 300 submissions** [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellinikon_Metropolitan_Park).

#### The Winning Design: Serero, Fernandez, and Coignet

The competition was won by an international team led by **David Serero** (French architect, born 1974 in Grenoble [[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Serero_(architect))), **Elena Fernandez** (architect), and **Philippe Coignet** (landscape architect). The winning design was announced in **2005** [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellinikon_Metropolitan_Park) [[19]](https://www.vassilismakris.com/departures/).

Serero was at the time a fellow at the Villa Medici (French Academy in Rome) from **2004–2005**, where he was developing advanced architectural concepts in urban design [[20]](https://villamedici.it/en/resident/david-serero/).

#### The "Softscapes" Concept

The winning design proposed a 530-hectare integrated park centered on a concept termed **"Softscapes"**—a water-management and landscape integration system. The design featured **seven north-south green corridors** that would channel and manage rainwater and stormwater across the site [[19]](https://www.vassilismakris.com/departures/). Approximately **80% of the park's water requirements would be sourced from on-site natural water collection and infiltration systems**, eliminating dependency on external water infrastructure. The design integrated terraced slopes, naturalized water features, and permeable surface systems intended to create a climate-resilient landscape [[19]](https://www.vassilismakris.com/departures/).

The design proceeded to two advanced planning phases during **2006–2007**, with detailed site analysis, hydrological modeling, and landscape architectural specification [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellinikon_Metropolitan_Park).

#### Why This Design Was Shelved

Despite advanced design development, the Serero-Fernandez-Coignet Metropolitan Park design was **shelved in 2007** and never proceeded to construction. The cause was not technical infeasibility, but rather a deliberate shift in state development policy. In **2007**, the Greek Ministry of Environment presented a revised development strategy that proposed:

- **130 hectares of commercial malls and retail centers** (instead of 530 hectares of park)
- **Residential development** with substantial built area
- **Golf courses and resort facilities**
- **Integrated casino and hospitality uses**

This represented a fundamental departure from the public metropolitan park model [[2]](https://greekreporter.com/2019/09/15/the-hellinikon-project-greeces-modern-tale-of-agony-and-ambition/). The shift was explicitly toward a private commercial development model.

#### Municipal Opposition

Four municipalities bordering the Ellinikon site—**Alimos, Hellinikon, Argyroupoli, and Glyfada**—formed a coordinated resistance movement to the privatization of what had been designated as public green space. The municipalities filed legal challenges with the Greek **Council of State** in **June 2012**, contesting the legality of converting the site to private development [[12]](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2025/05/08/hellinikon-project-municipalities-anger/). These legal proceedings remained unresolved within the timeline of privatization and do not appear to have prevented the eventual transfer of the site to private development.

## Phase C: Academic Feasibility Study and Civil Society Mobilization (2008–2012)

Despite the 2007 shift toward commercial development, the metropolitan park concept retained sufficient scientific and public support to generate a formal research project. The **National Technical University of Athens (NTUA) School of Architecture** was assigned a research project titled *"Basic Plan Principles for a Metropolitan Park at the Former Airport of Hellinikon"* (completed **2011**).

The NTUA study documented the **technical and financial feasibility of a dedicated metropolitan park**, establishing that no engineering or financial impediments existed to implementation of a public park model [[19]](https://www.vassilismakris.com/departures/). The mere fact of this study's commission and completion indicates that the park concept was scientifically sound and implementable; the subsequent abandonment of the park pathway was driven by policy choice, not technical limitation.

#### Civil Society Mobilization

A significant civil society movement coalesced around the metropolitan park concept. The **"Struggle Committee for the Metropolitan Park"** was formally established on **March 15, 2011**, and coordinated opposition to privatization across the Attica region. The committee occupied portions of the airport premises, establishing a **Metropolitan Community Clinic** and an **agricultural field initiative** as alternative models for the site's use [[19]](https://www.vassilismakris.com/departures/).

#### Legislative Foreclosure

The death blow to the metropolitan park concept came through legislative action. In **February 2012**, the Greek Ministry approved legislative changes that:

- **Increased built-surface ratio caps** on the Ellinikon site, permitting denser development
- **Authorized coastal construction** on previously restricted zones
- **Permitted golf course development** in areas designated for public green space

These legislative changes effectively foreclosed the public park development pathway by law, rendering future park-focused development planning incompatible with the new regulatory framework [[19]](https://www.vassilismakris.com/departures/). The legislation was enacted in the context of the 2009 Greek sovereign debt crisis and the subsequent Troika (IMF, ECB, and European Commission) bailout conditions, which explicitly mandated accelerated privatization of state assets.

## Phase D: Displacement by Private Development (2012–2017)

In **2014**, the Greek state's privatization entity, **HELLINIKON Global I. S.A.** (operating under the asset development authority HELLINIKON Global I. S.A.), signed a concession agreement with **Lamda Development S.A.** granting exclusive development rights to the Ellinikon site. Lamda Development was the **sole bidder** for the concession (February 2014) [[14]](https://chinaandgreece.com/memorandum-understanding-hellinikon/) [[13]](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/projects/the-ellinikon-greece/).

The valuation of the site became a point of significant dispute. The **Technical Chamber of Greece** assessed the site's nominal value at **USD 34.2 billion** (October 2014). However, Lamda Development's contractual commitment to the Greek state for the concession was only **USD 1.1 million**—a discrepancy of over 30,000 times [[14]](https://chinaandgreece.com/memorandum-understanding-hellinikon/).

#### The Integrated Development Plan

Lamda Development filed an **Integrated Development Plan (IDP)** in **August 2017** proposing a mixed-use conversion including:

- **6.2 million square meters** of total development area
- **25,000–35,000 residential units** (planned residential population)
- **3–6 mixed-use towers** reaching up to 200 meters in height
- **65 hectares of designated green space** (less than the previous 530-hectare park concept, and less than the 200 hectares nominally designated as "park")
- **Casino and integrated resort facilities**
- **Shopping centers and commercial zones**
- **Marina with 310 berths** [[15]](https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/athens-greece-ellinikon-plans-move-ahead-mega-project)

This development plan formally **cancelled** the metropolitan park concept as originally envisioned. The public greenspace was reduced from 530 hectares (proposed by Serero et al.) and 200 hectares (nominal reserve) to 65 hectares embedded within a predominantly residential and commercial development [[15]](https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/athens-greece-ellinikon-plans-move-ahead-mega-project) [[13]](https://www.worldconstructionnetwork.com/projects/the-ellinikon-greece/).

## What Survived from the Metropolitan Park Efforts

Despite the cancellation of the public park concept:

- **Architectural documentation** from the 2005 competition remains preserved in design archives and professional portfolios.
- **Design visualizations and competition drawings** by Serero Architectes Urbanistes remain accessible [[22]](https://serero-architectes.tumblr.com/).
- **Civil society networks and coordination mechanisms** established during the 2011–2012 mobilization remain intact as advocacy and planning organizations [[19]](https://www.vassilismakris.com/departures/).
- **Academic and technical feasibility documentation** from the NTUA study persists in institutional archives and legal proceedings.
- **Legal proceedings** brought by the four municipalities (Alimos, Hellinikon, Argyroupoli, Glyfada) remain in the Greek Court system, though they did not prevent privatization [[12]](https://www.keeptalkinggreece.com/2025/05/08/hellinikon-project-municipalities-anger/).
