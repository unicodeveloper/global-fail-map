# Thu Thiem New Urban Area

## Scope and Planning Origins

Thu Thiem New Urban Area represents Ho Chi Minh City's most ambitious single-area redevelopment, spanning a peninsula on the Saigon River opposite downtown District 1. The planned development area comprises approximately 2,600+ hectares in what is now District 2 (created 2018 from part of District 7) and surrounding districts [[11]](https://www.vietnam-briefing.com/news/hcmc-guide-thu-thiem-new-urban-area.html/) [[33]](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/guide-district-2-ho-chi-minh-city-thu-thiem-new-urban-mimi-huynh). Planning for the area began in the late 1990s, with formal master planning initiated in the 2000s.

## Master Plan and International Design Selection

In 2003, the municipal government selected Sasaki Associates, a major international planning and design firm, to develop a comprehensive master plan for Thu Thiem [[33]](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/guide-district-2-ho-chi-minh-city-thu-thiem-new-urban-mimi-huynh). Sasaki's appointment reflected Ho Chi Minh City's ambition to pursue world-class urban design comparable to Saigon South's SOM partnership [[11]](https://www.vietnam-briefing.com/news/hcmc-guide-thu-thiem-new-urban-area.html/).

## Land Clearance and Critical Administrative Breakdown

Land clearance to enable the new development began in 2002, removing existing agricultural and low-density residential uses. A critical administrative failure occurred early in the process: the original land-title planning map, created in 1996 as the authoritative record for land ownership and compensation calculations, went missing from municipal records [[32]](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/news/urban-drama-thu-thiem-residents-fight-for-their-homes-after-planning-map-goes-missing-3747221.html). This loss of the foundational document meant that resettlement compensation amounts and property right allocations could not be definitively verified against the original baseline.

## Resettlement Crisis and Prolonged Gridlock

The disappearance of the original map created a cascading series of disputes. Residents had no transparent way to verify their allocated resettlement units or compensation amounts [[34]](https://en.sggp.org.vn/thu-thiem-residents-receive-resettlement-decisions-post102259.html). Property rights claimants could not definitively establish their land holdings or family units' sizes on the basis of contemporaneous official records. Between 2002 and 2023, the resettlement process moved forward incrementally while generating ongoing disputes [[34]](https://en.sggp.org.vn/thu-thiem-residents-receive-resettlement-decisions-post102259.html) [[35]](https://en.sggp.org.vn/resettlement-zone-in-thu-thiem-new-urban-area-ensures-dwellers-rights-post123020.html) [[36]](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/khu-do-thi-moi-thu-thiem-bao-dam-quyen-loi-nguoi-dan).

By 2023, approximately two decades after land clearance began, the first cohorts of affected residents began receiving resettlement decision letters and land-use right certificates [[34]](https://en.sggp.org.vn/thu-thiem-residents-receive-resettlement-decisions-post102259.html). However, completion remained far from assured. In 2026, Ho Chi Minh City authorities were still auctioning off resettlement apartments in the Thu Thiem area—attempting to place 3,790 apartments through multiple rounds of unsuccessful auctions [[37]](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/tp-hcm-ban-dau-gia-3-790-can-ho-tai-dinh-cu-o-thu-thiem-vao-thang-5-2026) [[38]](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/lan-thu-5-tp-ho-chi-minh-dua-ra-dau-gia-3-790-can-ho-tai-dinh-cu). The fact that these apartments remained unoccupied and were being offered for sale at public auction, rather than distributed to original residents, suggested that the resettlement process had become decoupled from the initial displacement and had evolved into a broader housing allocation problem [[38]](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/lan-thu-5-tp-ho-chi-minh-dua-ra-dau-gia-3-790-can-ho-tai-dinh-cu).

## Selective Development and Incomplete Master Plan Realization

Despite the stalled comprehensive redevelopment, selective projects proceeded. The Thu Thiem metro station on Ho Chi Minh City Metro Line 1 was built and opened in 2024–2025 as part of the city's first operational metro line [[19]](https://time.com/collections/worlds-greatest-places-2025/7263149/ho-chi-minh-city-metro/) [[21]](https://futuresoutheastasia.com/ho-chi-minh-city-metro-2022-construction-update/). The Sala City residential complex and other individual projects moved forward [[33]](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/guide-district-2-ho-chi-minh-city-thu-thiem-new-urban-mimi-huynh). However, the Saigon Planning Exhibition Center, a showcase building for the Thu Thiem vision, remained unfinished as of 2026 [[5]](https://futuresoutheastasia.com/stalled-projects-in-ho-chi-minh-city/) [[12]](https://vietnaminsiders.com/insider-story-unfinished-buildings-in-saigon/).

**Status: MIRED IN INDEFINITE DELAYS** — Planning initiated late 1990s; resettlement gridlock ongoing since 2002; selective development proceeding (metro, commercial projects) but comprehensive master plan vision unrealized; resettlement process still incomplete as of 2026 with 3,790 units unsold.

## Surviving and Lost Elements

The infrastructure that has been completed—the metro line, individual commercial projects, partial utility corridors—remains functional. The Sasaki master plan exists in archived form but has been substantially revised multiple times to accommodate the extended timeline and changing land-use assumptions. Thousands of residents have received resettlement apartments and relocation assistance, though the prolonged process has imposed decades of disruption. The Saigon River waterfront environment, the primary geographic asset that motivated the development, remains partially unrealized as a public amenity due to the extended development timeline.
