# King Abdullah Economic City

## Announcement and Vision

King Abdullah Economic City (KAEC) was announced in December 2005 as Saudi Arabia's largest free-trade zone and planned economic hub. [[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_Economic_City) Developed by Emaar Properties, the vision was to create a city from scratch that would be economically productive, attracting manufacturing, logistics, and service-sector employment. The city was projected to house 2 million residents by 2035. [[12]](https://riyadh2030.ai/giga-projects/king-abdullah-economic-city/) [[15]](https://www.meed.com/king-abdullah-economic-city/)

The project represented, in its own era, what Neom would represent a decade later: a bet that if you built massive infrastructure in the desert with clear economic incentives, global capital and human population would arrive to utilize it. The model was deliberate: create the conditions, remove the barriers, and growth would follow inevitably.

## Realization: A 99.98 Percent Shortfall

As of 2026, over twenty years after the initial announcement, KAEC had attracted between 10,000 and 15,000 residents. [[12]](https://riyadh2030.ai/giga-projects/king-abdullah-economic-city/) [[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_Economic_City) [[14]](https://interestingengineering.com/culture/king-abdullah-economic-city-future) This represents a 99.98 percent shortfall against the 2 million resident target. The timeframe for reaching planned population was repeatedly extended: from 2010 to 2020 to 2035, with each revision deferring reckoning with the fundamental failure of the model. [[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_Economic_City) [[17]](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/industries/travel-hospitality/427948-inside-saudi-arabias-king-abdullah-economic-city)

The failure was not uniform across all components. The King Abdullah Port became operational and functional. [[12]](https://riyadh2030.ai/giga-projects/king-abdullah-economic-city/) [[16]](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/learn-from-the-past-build-for-the-future-saudi-arabias-new-city-on-the-red-sea) Certain industrial components achieved limited success. But the residential city, the commercial core, and the service economy that was supposed to employ the resident population never materialized at scale.

The distinction is important: KAEC represents failure of the "if you build it, they will come" strategy. Investors could not be convinced to invest in residential real estate; tenants could not be convinced to relocate to the city; the economic base could not be established through subsidy. Unlike The Line, which failed primarily due to geometric and financial impossibility, KAEC failed because human behavior did not align with planning assumptions.

## Geographic and Financial Context

KAEC is located in Rabigh, within Mecca Province, approximately 100 kilometers north of Jeddah. [[13]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Abdullah_Economic_City) The original investment totaled \$27 billion, with the project developed by Emaar Properties under public-private partnership. [[15]](https://www.meed.com/king-abdullah-economic-city/) [[12]](https://riyadh2030.ai/giga-projects/king-abdullah-economic-city/)

The failure pattern is consistent across sources: a mega-announcement with bold targets, early optimism, gradually widening gap between targets and actuals, repeated revision of timelines and targets, and eventual acceptance of a much smaller outcome than originally projected.
