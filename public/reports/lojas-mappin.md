# Lojas Mappin

**What was built:** Lojas Mappin (Casa Anglo-Brasileira S/A) was founded in 1913 as a department store, pioneering retail innovation in Brazil for over 85 years. The company opened its first Brazilian store in São Paulo and established an iconic location at Praça Ramos in the city center. [[2]](https://vejasp.abril.com.br/coluna/memoria/grandes-lojas-fecharam-sp/) [[19]](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/dinheiro/fi20049920.htm) Mappin pioneered the crediário system—an installment payment plan for consumer purchases—that became a standard mechanism enabling middle-class consumers to purchase high-value goods (appliances, furniture, electronics) on credit. [[3]](https://boletimnacional.com.br/2022/02/18/relembre-grandes-lojas-e-empresas-que-fecharam-as-portas-no-brasil/) The company also introduced Brazil's first shopping mall-integrated department store and pioneered in-store parking at the Avenida São João location during the 1950s, establishing new retail infrastructure standards. [[3]](https://boletimnacional.com.br/2022/02/18/relembre-grandes-lojas-e-empresas-que-fecharam-as-portas-no-brasil/) These innovations positioned Mappin as a cultural and commercial institution in São Paulo's retail landscape.

**Expansion and diversification:** During the 1990s, Mappin expanded by incorporating the Mesbla retail subsidiary (acquired 1996), diversifying its store footprint and attempting to scale operations across more geographic markets. [[3]](https://boletimnacional.com.br/2022/02/18/relembre-grandes-lojas-e-empresas-que-fecharam-as-portas-no-brasil/) This expansion strategy, however, was not matched by adequate management and organizational capability to operate at larger scale.

**Decline and closure:** By the mid-1990s, Mappin experienced financial difficulties attributed to mismanagement and inadequate preparation for operational growth at increased scale. [[3]](https://boletimnacional.com.br/2022/02/18/relembre-grandes-lojas-e-empresas-que-fecharam-as-portas-no-brasil/) The company lacked the financial and operational resilience to compete against larger consolidated retailers entering the Brazilian market. In 1999, Mappin ceased all activities, marking the permanent end of the company's operations. [[2]](https://vejasp.abril.com.br/coluna/memoria/grandes-lojas-fecharam-sp/) [[3]](https://boletimnacional.com.br/2022/02/18/relembre-grandes-lojas-e-empresas-que-fecharam-as-portas-no-brasil/) Lojas Mappin stores were closed, and the company's liabilities exceeded assets available for liquidation.

**Asset seizures and looting:** Following closure announcements in 1999, several Mappin store locations were looted (saqueadas) by customers and opportunistic actors. In Campinas (in the São Paulo interior), a Mappin store was ransacked in August 1999 after news of closure spread. [[20]](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/fsp/campinas/cm18089907.htm) This looting reflected the economic desperation and social unrest accompanying the retail collapse.

**Subsidiary and creditor impacts:** The company's Mesbla subsidiary, incorporated into Mappin's corporate structure in 1996, was pulled down with the parent company's failure. Both Mappin and Mesbla faced liquidation simultaneously. Sources indicate that the Crefisul financial institution, a creditor providing store financing, faced significant writedowns from Mappin's liquidation, creating secondary financial distress for Crefisul and threatening its stability. [[21]](https://www.folhadelondrina.com.br/geral/mesbla-e-mappin-podem-ser-prejudicados-por-liquidacao-do-crefisul-136092.html)

**What survived — Consumer practices and brand resurrection:** The crediário installment payment system pioneered by Mappin survived the company's dissolution and became standard across Brazilian retail. Competitors—notably Casas Bahia and later Lojas Marabraz—adopted and continued the crediário model, extending the consumer innovation beyond the failed organization. In 2012 and thereafter, the Mappin brand was resurrected as an online and street-retail venture operated by Lojas Marabraz, representing a partial brand revival though not a restoration of the original company's operations or financial model. [[3]](https://boletimnacional.com.br/2022/02/18/relembre-grandes-lojas-e-empresas-que-fecharam-as-portas-no-brasil/) The brand name and consumer recognition persisted as an asset, though severed from the original corporate entity.

**Geographic connection:** Mappin was headquartered in São Paulo city, with primary operations in the metropolitan region, though the company maintained stores across Brazil by the time of its 1999 closure.
