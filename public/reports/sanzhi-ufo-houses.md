# The Sanzhi UFO Houses

## The Vision and Construction

The most visually distinctive discontinued project in New Taipei City is the Sanzhi UFO Houses (三芝飛碟屋), a cluster of modular pod-shaped vacation resort buildings constructed in Sanzhi District along the northern coast adjacent to Danshui [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/). Construction began in 1978, initiated by the Hung Kuo Group (宏國集團), a local developer, with design contributions from Yu-chou Co., a plastics manufacturer based in the Sanzhi area [[10]](https://spectralcodex.com/sanzhi-ufo-houses/). The buildings were modeled on the Finnish Futuro house design created by architect Matti Suuronen in the late 1960s—a prefabricated, futuristic dwelling intended originally as portable ski chalets capable of being airlifted onto remote terrain [[16]](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/wanli-ufo-village).

The Sanzhi resort was deliberately positioned as a vacation destination for U.S. military personnel stationed in East Asia [[12]](https://en.advisor.travel/poi/Sanzhi-UFO-Houses-5210). The rounded, pod-like aesthetic of the structures was meant to evoke science fiction and technological optimism, aligning with marketing positioning targeting affluent international visitors who would find the architecture novel and appealing [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/). The project was geographically situated on Taiwan's northern coast, a location that offered scenic oceanfront property but also exposed the structures to the island's challenging climate patterns.

## The Abandonment and Its Causes

Construction proceeded for only two years before the project was abandoned in 1980, never accepting a single guest. Multiple documented sources identify fatal accidents during the construction phase as a significant immediate cause. Sources reference "fatal car accidents during construction" but do not provide precise casualty figures [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/) [[10]](https://spectralcodex.com/sanzhi-ufo-houses/) [[11]](https://www.abandonedspaces.com/public/sanzhi-taiwan.html). Beyond accidents, investment capital depletion and mounting investment losses became insurmountable obstacles [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/). The combination of construction difficulties and financial pressure brought construction to a halt before completion.

A brief revival attempt occurred in 1989, approximately nine years after initial abandonment. However, structural engineers conducted assessments of the completed pods and identified significant earthquake safety concerns. The underlying reinforced concrete structure combined with the fiber-reinforced plastic exterior covering was deemed at risk in Taiwan's seismic environment [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/). This technical assessment, combined with continued financial constraints, eliminated any realistic prospect of reopening. The project remained abandoned for an additional two decades.

## Demolition and Redevelopment

By the late 2000s, the property represented an increasingly untenable asset—a cluster of deteriorating structures on valuable beachfront land that would never operate as designed. Demolition commenced on December 29, 2008, and was completed by 2010 [[1]](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/city-and-architecture/a5191-lost-in-time-sanzhi-taiwan/) [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/). All physical traces of the pod buildings were removed from the site.

The land itself survived—and was successfully redeveloped. Following demolition, the Sanzhi beachfront property was converted into conventional vacation resort facilities, with hotels and beach amenities replacing the iconic pods [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/). The geographic location proved valuable for commercial hospitality development once freed from the failed pod resort concept.

## What Survived and What Did Not

The Sanzhi UFO Houses themselves did not survive as functioning structures, residential units, or even preserved architectural specimens. The project was wholly discontinued as an operational concept, and the buildings were completely demolished. However, the cultural memory of the pods persists through extensive photographic documentation by urban explorers and architecture enthusiasts who visited before demolition [[1]](https://www.re-thinkingthefuture.com/city-and-architecture/a5191-lost-in-time-sanzhi-taiwan/) [[9]](https://theghostinmymachine.com/2014/09/17/abandoned-the-sanzhi-ufo-houses-and-the-future-that-never-was-photos-new-taipei-city-taiwan-pod-houses/) [[11]](https://www.abandonedspaces.com/public/sanzhi-taiwan.html). The site's geographic value—beachfront land in a developed area near Taipei—survived and was repurposed.

What failed was not the modular pod technology itself (identical structures were successfully deployed elsewhere in Taiwan, at Wanli); what failed was the specific venture's business model, structural engineering assessment in a seismic context, and investment capacity. The pods as architectural technology were sound enough to persist elsewhere; the Sanzhi project as a commercial and operational enterprise was not sustainable.
