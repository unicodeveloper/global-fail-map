# Dara Sakor

The most ambitious unrealized vision for the Sihanoukville region is the Dara Sakor integrated resort and city project, initiated in 2008 by Union Development Group (UDG), a Tianjin-based private Chinese company [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049). The scope of the original plan was extraordinary: a 36,000-hectare concession that was subsequently expanded to 45,000 hectares within Botum Sakor National Park, located approximately 30 kilometers southeast of central Sihanoukville [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049). The developer's vision was to construct a self-contained megacity on the scale of an entirely new metropolis, marketed as a "Chinese colony" in promotional materials [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049).

The stated ambitions reveal the scale of the unrealized vision. The project was designed to include an international airport, a deep-sea port, coal-fired power stations, hospitals, casinos, luxury residential villas, and supporting infrastructure. Promotional materials promised 1.3 million long-term residents, capacity to handle 7 million tourists annually, and the creation of 1 million jobs by 2030 [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049). These figures represent an entire city comparable in size to Cambodia's capital, positioned in a protected national park.

What was actually built falls far short of these ambitions. After more than 15 years of development, the completed infrastructure consists primarily of a single operating casino with attached five-star hotel and apartment complexes. An airport runway remains under construction, unfinished. The site is dominated by construction equipment, partially cleared land, and scattered completed facilities amid a landscape that was supposed to be a thriving metropolitan region [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049).

The project has encountered multiple interlocking obstacles that explain why it remains largely unrealized. First, fundamental development authorization appears to have proceeded without required environmental assessment. In 2016, the Global Environmental Institute (GEI), a Chinese environmental monitoring organization, conducted a study finding no evidence that Union Development Group had completed the environmental impact assessment (EIA) mandated under Cambodian law for developments of this scale [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049). Remote sensing data from Global Forest Watch documented primary forest loss of approximately 20 percent within Botum Sakor National Park since 2008, suggesting environmental degradation occurred despite the absence of formal environmental review [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049).

Second, financial commitments failed to materialize. The project secured only a \$15 million bond issued in 2017 by the China Development Bank [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049). Initial promises of approximately \$4 billion in investment capital never materialized, leaving the project severely underfunded relative to its stated scope [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049).

Third, geopolitical sanctions constrained the project. The United States Department of the Treasury imposed sanctions against Union Development Group in 2020 on two grounds: documented human rights abuses related to the forced displacement of village communities, and intelligence concerns about the airport runway's dimensions, suggesting potential military use incompatible with civilian aviation [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049). The sanction documentation indicated that over 1,000 families were uprooted and displaced from fishing communities, which were relocated approximately 30 kilometers away to create space for development [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049).

The project has continued operating at minimal capacity into the present. By 2023–2024, the site became known as a location hosting scam call centers operated by criminal syndicates, according to reports documented by the Bangkok office of the United Nations Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights [[11]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-asia-66851049). The airport remains unfinished, and there are no public announcements of progress toward the stated 1.3-million-resident megacity vision.

As of September 2026, Dara Sakor represents a clear case of an unrealized megaproject vision. The gap between ambition and realization—a promised 1.3 million residents and 1 million jobs versus one operating casino and an unfinished airport—constitutes a definitive failure of the development plan as originally conceived. The project was neither cancelled formally nor superseded by an alternative plan; rather, it simply stalled under multiple constraints (environmental, financial, geopolitical) and evolved into a low-intensity operational site far removed from its original scope.
