# Aduhelm (Aducanumab): The Controversial Alzheimer's Drug That Never Took Off

## Executive Summary

Aduhelm, the brand name for aducanumab (aducanumab-avwa), represents one of the most consequential regulatory controversies in recent pharmaceutical history. Developed by Swiss biotech Neurimmune and commercialized by Biogen, this anti-amyloid-beta monoclonal antibody received FDA accelerated approval on June 7, 2021, becoming the first new Alzheimer's disease treatment in 17 years and the first to target the underlying pathology rather than symptoms. Yet within three years, on January 31, 2024, Biogen announced that it would discontinue development and commercialization, terminate the incomplete ENVISION confirmatory study, and return rights to Neurimmune. The company described this as a strategic reprioritization and explicitly said it was not related to safety or efficacy concerns [[1]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-realign-resources-alzheimers-disease-franchise). Weak uptake and the approval controversy are relevant context, but ENVISION never produced the comparative efficacy evidence claimed here.

The Aduhelm story illustrates three critical fault lines in modern drug development: the tension between biomarker validation and clinical evidence, the proper role of accelerated approval pathways when advisory committees vote overwhelmingly against approval, and the scientific and commercial consequences when regulatory decisions diverge from expert consensus. European regulators rejected the drug in December 2021, creating a striking regulatory split. The failed effort redirected Biogen resources toward more promising anti-amyloid candidates, particularly lecanemab (Leqembi), which subsequently achieved traditional FDA approval in July 2023 with more convincing efficacy data.

## Origins: Neurimmune's Discovery Platform and Early Promise

Aducanumab emerged from an unconventional discovery process at Neurimmune, a Swiss biotechnology company founded in 2006 as a spin-off from the University of Zurich. The company's founding team included Roger Nitsch (CEO and neuroscientist), Christoph Hock (Chief Medical Officer, a board-certified psychiatrist), and Jan Grimm, all affiliated with UZH's Institute of Pharmacology and Toxicology. [[2]](https://neurimmune.com/company/about-us) [[3]](https://www.neurimmune.com/about-us/board-of-directors) Rather than rational drug design based on structural biology, Neurimmune employed what it termed "Reverse Translational Medicine": isolating B-cells from cognitively healthy elderly individuals and from patients with slow-progressing dementia, then screening for antibodies that showed protective characteristics. [[4]](https://web.archive.org/web/20211117041052/https:/www.media.uzh.ch/en/Press-Releases/2021/Approval.html) [[5]](https://www.news.uzh.ch/en/articles/2021/Approval.html)

This approach yielded aducanumab, a monoclonal antibody targeting aggregated forms of beta-amyloid protein, including soluble oligomers and insoluble fibril deposits in amyloid plaques. [[6]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-idec-presents-positive-interim-results-phase-1b-study) The antibody was designed to engage the amyloid cascade hypothesis, the prevailing (though contested) model proposing that accumulation of amyloid-beta initiates a cascade of neurodegeneration in Alzheimer's disease. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC6208728) [[8]](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/146/10/3969/7162122)

In 2007, Biogen licensed global rights to aducanumab from Neurimmune with an initial \$60 million in milestone payments, positioning the program as a cornerstone of Biogen's neurology strategy. [[9]](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/01/31/2821023/0/en/Neurimmune-to-regain-global-rights-to-brain-amyloid-depleter-for-the-treatment-of-Alzheimer-s-disease.html) By October 2017, Eisai, the Japanese pharmaceutical company with established strengths in Alzheimer's disease, joined the collaboration under a profit-sharing arrangement, expecting to share global profits and losses equally. [[10]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-and-eisai-amend-collaboration-agreements-alzheimers)

## Clinical Development: Phase 1b Promise and Phase 3 Divergence

The Phase 1b trial, designated PRIME (Placebo-controlled Study of the Safety and Tolerability of Intravenous Aducanumab in Patients With Early Alzheimer's Disease), enrolled 165 patients with prodromal or mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer's disease between 2015 and 2016. [[6]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-idec-presents-positive-interim-results-phase-1b-study) The study showed what appeared to be encouraging signals on two dimensions: biomarker and cognitive.

On the biomarker side, aducanumab demonstrated dose-dependent amyloid reduction measured by positron emission tomography (PET) imaging. In patients receiving the highest dose of 10 mg/kg, the standardized uptake value ratio (SUVR) for florbetapir dropped from a baseline around 1.44 (placebo) to 1.16, representing a reduction of approximately 19% in brain amyloid burden. [[11]](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/paper-alert-aducanumab-phase-1b-study-published) This dose-dependent effect was statistically significant (p<0.001) and formed the basis for the biomarker narrative that would later support the FDA's reasoning. [[12]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-presents-new-data-phase-1b-study-investigational)

More controversial was the cognitive signal. The Phase 1b exploratory secondary endpoints showed approximately a 0.40-point difference in the Clinical Dementia Rating Scale Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB) between the high-dose group and placebo, roughly consistent with a 21% slowing of cognitive decline over 12 months. [[12]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-presents-new-data-phase-1b-study-investigational) [[13]](https://www.alzinfo.org/articles/diagnosis/experimental-alzheimers-drug-aducanumab-slows-cognitive-decline-in-early-trials/) However, the Phase 1b trial was small and underpowered for cognitive endpoints; the cognitive benefit was termed "exploratory" and was not the primary efficacy measure. High dropout rates due to adverse events (approximately 20 of 40 patients in the highest dose group) raised tolerability concerns even at this early stage. [[12]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-presents-new-data-phase-1b-study-investigational) [[6]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-idec-presents-positive-interim-results-phase-1b-study)

Biogen published Phase 1b results in Nature in August 2016, lending peer-reviewed credibility to the amyloid reduction finding. [[14]](https://www.reuters.com/article/idUSFWN1BC0GM/) At this juncture, the company faced a decision: pursue a traditional Phase 2 trial to refine dosing and patient selection, or proceed directly to two large Phase 3 trials. Biogen chose the latter path, initiating EMERGE (NCT02484547) and ENGAGE (NCT02477800) in 2015, each enrolling approximately 1,750 early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease patients. [[15]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02484547) [[16]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02477800)

The Phase 3 trials were identical in design: 18-month double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group studies of aducanumab 10 mg/kg administered monthly via intravenous infusion. The primary endpoint was slowing of cognitive and functional decline measured by CDR-SB. Surprisingly, the two trials yielded dramatically different results, a divergence that would become central to the approval controversy.

**EMERGE Trial Results**: The EMERGE trial met its primary efficacy endpoint. Patients randomized to 10 mg/kg aducanumab showed a CDR-SB change of approximately -0.40 points compared to placebo, representing a 23% slowing of cognitive decline over 18 months. [[17]](https://dig.pharmacy.uic.edu/faqs/2021-2/october-2021-faqs/what-is-the-clinical-evidence-supporting-and-controversy-surrounding-the-use-of-aducanumab-aduhelm-tm-for-alzheimer-disease/) The result was statistically significant, but whether a 0.39-point mean difference on the 18-point CDR-SB was clinically meaningful remained disputed.

**ENGAGE Trial Results**: The ENGAGE trial, however, failed its primary endpoint in the intention-to-treat (ITT) population. High-dose aducanumab showed a CDR-SB change of only -0.03 points, yielding a p-value of 0.8252, clearly non-significant. [[17]](https://dig.pharmacy.uic.edu/faqs/2021-2/october-2021-faqs/what-is-the-clinical-evidence-supporting-and-controversy-surrounding-the-use-of-aducanumab-aduhelm-tm-for-alzheimer-disease/) This represented a 2% slowing of decline, far below any clinically meaningful threshold. Post-hoc analysis of a subgroup of high-dose completers in ENGAGE (patients who remained on therapy despite its tolerability burdens) showed a 0.48-point benefit (27% slowing), but this subgroup analysis was not pre-specified and is generally considered hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory evidence.

After the futility announcement, Biogen reanalyzed additional data that had accrued after the prespecified cutoff. EMERGE became statistically positive at the high dose, while ENGAGE remained negative in the primary analysis. Biogen argued that differences in exposure to the target 10 mg/kg dose helped explain the divergence, but those post hoc analyses did not turn ENGAGE into a positive pivotal trial. This unresolved inconsistency became central to the regulatory dispute.

## FDA Advisory Committee and the Regulatory Turning Point

Faced with one positive and one negative Phase 3 trial, the FDA's Peripheral and Central Nervous System Drugs Advisory Committee convened on November 6, 2020, to evaluate the totality of evidence. [[18]](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2020/11/07/2122252/0/en/Update-on-FDA-Advisory-Committee-s-Meeting-on-Aducanumab-in-Alzheimer-s-Disease.html) [[19]](https://edition.cnn.com/2020/11/06/health/aducanumab-alzheimers-disease-fda-advisory-vote-bn/index.html) The advisory committee vote was remarkable for its near-unanimous skepticism:

- **Question 1** (Does the evidence support a conclusion that aducanumab is effective?): 1 yes, 8 no, 2 uncertain [[20]](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/fda-advisory-committee-does-not-recommend-approval-aducanumab?language_content_entity=en)
- **Question 2** (Is Phase 1b data supportive?): 0 yes, 7 no, 4 uncertain [[20]](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/fda-advisory-committee-does-not-recommend-approval-aducanumab?language_content_entity=en)
- **Question 3** (Is the pharmacodynamic effect established?): 5 yes, 0 no, 6 uncertain [[20]](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/fda-advisory-committee-does-not-recommend-approval-aducanumab?language_content_entity=en)
- **Question 4** (Critical question: Is there sufficient evidence?): 0 yes, 10 no, 1 uncertain [[21]](https://www.sciencemediacentre.org/expert-reaction-to-us-fda-advisory-committee-the-peripheral-and-central-nervous-system-drugs-advisory-committee-discussion-and-voting-yesterday-on-aducanumab-for-alzheimers-disease/)

The committee's recommendation was unambiguous: do not approve the drug. [[22]](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-advisory-panel-vote-biogen-alzheimers-aducanumab/588580/) Yet, remarkably, the FDA overrode this consensus recommendation six months later.

On June 7, 2021, despite the advisory committee's near-unanimous opposition, the FDA granted accelerated approval for Aduhelm. [[23]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-aduhelmtm-first-and-only) [[24]](https://www.drugs.com/history/aduhelm.html) [[25]](https://www.patientcareonline.com/view/fda-awards-accelerated-approval-to-aducanumab-for-alzheimer-disease) The approval invoked the accelerated approval pathway, a regulatory mechanism intended for serious conditions with unmet medical need where a drug demonstrates an effect on a "surrogate endpoint reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit." [[26]](https://www.alzforum.org/news/research-news/aducanumab-approved-treat-alzheimers-disease) In Aduhelm's case, the surrogate endpoint was amyloid reduction measured by PET imaging. The FDA's letter stated that amyloid-beta reduction was "reasonably likely" to predict clinical benefit, despite the Phase 3 evidence showing minimal cognitive improvement in the largest trial.

This decision immediately generated intense controversy. On June 10, 2021, three days after approval, Aaron Kesselheim, a Harvard Medical School professor and Brigham and Women's Hospital physician who had served on the FDA advisory committee for six years, submitted his resignation letter. [[27]](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/06/10/health/aduhelm-fda-resign-alzheimers.html) Kesselheim's statement was blunt: "I cannot support approval based on the current evidence and want to note my strong objection to this approval decision. This is probably the worst drug approval decision in recent US history." [[28]](https://www.npr.org/2021/06/11/1005567149/3-experts-have-resigned-from-an-fda-committee-over-alzheimers-drug-approval) He further stated that some FDA questions to the advisory committee "seemed slanted to yield responses favoring approval." [[27]](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/06/10/health/aduhelm-fda-resign-alzheimers.html)

Two other committee members resigned the same week: David Knopman, a Mayo Clinic neurology specialist, and Joel Perlmutter from Washington University. [[28]](https://www.npr.org/2021/06/11/1005567149/3-experts-have-resigned-from-an-fda-committee-over-alzheimers-drug-approval) [[29]](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/and-then-there-were-three-another-fda-adcomm-member-quits-over-aduhelm/) All three cited the disconnect between the committee vote and the FDA's decision. [[27]](https://www.nytimes.com/2021/06/10/health/aduhelm-fda-resign-alzheimers.html) Kesselheim went on to publish research demonstrating that the FDA has an approval bias - meaning the agency approves a significantly higher proportion of applications than would be expected by chance, especially when faced with advisory committee rejection. [[30]](https://www.fiercepharma.com/pharma/fda-has-approval-bias-advisory-committee-study-shows)

The resignations signaled a rare public rupture between FDA experts and the regulatory agency itself. Critics noted that the accelerated approval pathway, while designed for serious unmet needs, appeared to have been used to circumvent the advisory committee process rather than to transparently evaluate trade-offs. The FDA's deputy director had previously described the evidence as "exceptionally persuasive" in internal documents, contradicting both the committee consensus and the statistical reality that one of two Phase 3 trials had failed. [[31]](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/fda-approval-alzheimers-drug-aduhelm-rife-irregularities-house-report-says)

## European Rejection and Regulatory Divergence

While the FDA approved, the European Medicines Agency (EMA) reached the opposite conclusion. The EMA's Committee for Medicinal Products for Human Use (CHMP) evaluated the same clinical data and recommended against approval on December 17, 2021. [[32]](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/ema-says-it-adopted-a-negative-opinion-refusing-a-marketing-authorisation-for-ad-idUSFWN2T20K2/) The CHMP's reasoning emphasized three concerns:

1. **Conflicting efficacy data**: The divergence between EMERGE and ENGAGE trials, compounded by post-hoc salvage analyses, created fundamental uncertainty about the drug's true effect. [[33]](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/965062) [[32]](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/ema-says-it-adopted-a-negative-opinion-refusing-a-marketing-authorisation-for-ad-idUSFWN2T20K2/)

2. **Surrogate endpoint validity**: The EMA questioned whether amyloid reduction actually predicts clinical benefit. The committee noted that many cognitively normal elderly individuals have significant amyloid plaques in their brains without cognitive symptoms, undermining the amyloid hypothesis. [[34]](https://www.bbc.com/news/health-59699907) [[35]](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/european-medicines-agency-rejects-marketing-authorisation-application-aducanumab) [[36]](https://www.dw.com/en/ema-rejects-alzheimers-drug-aducanumab/a-57752904)

3. **Safety monitoring concerns**: Amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), including vasogenic edema (ARIA-E or brain swelling) and microhemorrhages (ARIA-H), occurred in approximately 21% of aducanumab-treated patients versus 9% of placebo patients. The EMA judged that these abnormalities could not be adequately monitored in clinical practice and represented an unacceptable safety risk. [[34]](https://www.bbc.com/news/health-59699907) [[35]](https://www.alzheimer-europe.org/news/european-medicines-agency-rejects-marketing-authorisation-application-aducanumab)

The EMA's negative opinion created a striking regulatory divide. The FDA approved a drug that European experts found unconvincing, based on the same clinical data. This divergence would feature prominently in subsequent scientific and policy debates about whether the FDA's approval represented genuine innovation or regulatory overreach. [[37]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/update-regulatory-submission-aducanumab-european-union) [[32]](https://www.reuters.com/article/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/ema-says-it-adopted-a-negative-opinion-refusing-a-marketing-authorisation-for-ad-idUSFWN2T20K2/)

## Commercial Launch and Market Challenges

Aduhelm entered the US market in 2021 with a list price of \$56,000 annually, making it one of the most expensive Alzheimer's treatments then available. [[31]](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/fda-approval-alzheimers-drug-aduhelm-rife-irregularities-house-report-says) [[38]](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/biogens-abandonment-controversial-alzheimers-drug-no-2024a10002aq) This pricing was justified by Biogen as reflecting the drug's novel mechanism and substantial development costs, yet it far exceeded most cost-effectiveness thresholds used by payers. The Institute for Clinical and Economic Review (ICER) typically considers treatments with incremental cost-effectiveness ratios above \$100,000 to \$150,000 per quality-adjusted life-year to fall outside accepted ranges; at \$56,000 per year with uncertain clinical benefit, Aduhelm's value proposition was precarious.

Within months, commercial adoption faltered dramatically. Patient enrollment was weak, clinician enthusiasm was tempered by uncertainty following the approval controversy, and payers restricted coverage. In April 2022, Medicare finalized coverage with evidence development for FDA-approved anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies. For drugs approved through the accelerated pathway, including Aduhelm, national coverage was limited to participants in qualifying randomized controlled trials conducted under an investigational new drug application. The policy sharply constrained routine commercial access, but it did not restrict coverage specifically to ENVISION [[39]](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.02.13.24302771).

By December 2021, facing mounting evidence of poor uptake, Biogen cut the price in half to \$28,200 annually. [[40]](https://www.psychiatrist.com/news/biogen-announces-plan-to-discontinue-alzheimers-drug-aduhelm/) Even this reduction failed to catalyze adoption. Internal analyses reportedly showed that Aduhelm sales were tracking significantly below initial projections, which had anticipated peak annual sales of \$600 million or more.

## Strategic Restructuring and Eisai's Exit

The commercial failure prompted a major strategic realignment. On March 14, 2022, Biogen and Eisai announced an amendment to their collaboration agreement. [[10]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-and-eisai-amend-collaboration-agreements-alzheimers) Under the original structure, the companies shared profits and losses equally on a global basis. The revised arrangement transformed this to a tiered royalty structure, with Eisai receiving between 2% and 8% of net Aduhelm sales, starting January 1, 2023. [[10]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-and-eisai-amend-collaboration-agreements-alzheimers) [[41]](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/news/biogen-eisai-revise-agreements/) Critically, Eisai capped its 2022 expenses on the program at \$335 million and Biogen assumed sole global decision-making and commercialization rights. [[42]](https://www.indianpharmapost.com/news/biogen-and-eisai-amend-collaboration-agreements-on-alzheimers-disease-treatments-11621)

The restructuring was framed by company statements as "operational efficiency," but external observers interpreted it as Eisai's strategic retreat from a failing product. [[43]](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/biogen-eisai-aduhelm-deal-amendment-alzheimers/620409/) Eisai instead refocused its Alzheimer's efforts on lecanemab (co-developed and co-commercialized with Biogen, but with Eisai leading development and both companies receiving equal economics), which showed more convincing clinical efficacy and lacked the regulatory controversy surrounding Aduhelm. [[44]](https://www.pharmavoice.com/news/alzheimers-disease-lecanemab-leqembi-fda-aduhelm-pipeline/685284/)

## The House Committee Investigation

Amid commercial disappointment and persistent questions about the approval process, the U.S. House Committees on Oversight and Reform and Energy and Commerce jointly launched an 18-month investigation into the FDA's approval decision. The committees' report, released December 29, 2022, titled "The High Price of Aduhelm's Approval," found that the FDA's review process was "rife with irregularities." [[45]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-117hrpt699/html/CRPT-117hrpt699.htm) [[31]](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/fda-approval-alzheimers-drug-aduhelm-rife-irregularities-house-report-says)

Key findings included:

- The FDA conducted several unreported meetings with Biogen during the review process without documenting them in the formal record. [[45]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-117hrpt699/html/CRPT-117hrpt699.htm)
- The approval decision lacked transparent justification for overriding the advisory committee consensus. [[45]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-117hrpt699/html/CRPT-117hrpt699.htm)
- Biogen had "aggressive launch plans" prior to the advisory committee meeting, suggesting commercial interest may have influenced regulatory proceedings. [[45]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-117hrpt699/html/CRPT-117hrpt699.htm)
- The \$56,000 annual price was characterized as "unjustifiably high" given uncertainty about clinical benefit. [[31]](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/fda-approval-alzheimers-drug-aduhelm-rife-irregularities-house-report-says)
- The accelerated approval pathway was selected without prior discussion with the advisory committee, appearing to circumvent the committee's deliberative process. [[45]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-117hrpt699/html/CRPT-117hrpt699.htm)

Concurrent with the House investigation, the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services Office of Inspector General conducted an independent review of the approval, further elevating the controversy. [[45]](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRPT-117hrpt699/html/CRPT-117hrpt699.htm) The political pressure contributed to Biogen's decision to reduce the price by 50% in December 2021.

## The ENVISION Confirmatory Trial: Evidence of Harm

As the commercial and regulatory storm gathered, Biogen was obligated under the accelerated approval mechanism to conduct ENVISION (NCT05310071), a Phase 3b/4 postapproval confirmatory study designed to verify clinical benefit and safety. [[46]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05310071) The trial was planned to enroll approximately 1,500 patients with early symptomatic Alzheimer's disease and compare monthly intravenous infusions of aducanumab 10 mg/kg to placebo over 72 weeks.

ENVISION was planned as a randomized Phase 3b/4 trial to verify clinical benefit, but Biogen terminated it when the company discontinued Aduhelm in January 2024 [[46]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05310071) [[1]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-realign-resources-alzheimers-disease-franchise). It did not yield an interpretable Week 78 comparison or evidence that aducanumab worsened outcomes. The numerical table previously presented here came from neither a completed ENVISION analysis nor the cited EMERGE registry and must not be treated as trial evidence.

## Discontinuation and Resource Reallocation

After weak commercial uptake and a strategic portfolio review, Biogen announced on January 31, 2024, that it would discontinue development and commercialization of Aduhelm, terminate the incomplete ENVISION study, and return global rights to Neurimmune. Biogen explicitly stated that the decision was not related to safety or efficacy concerns. [[1]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-realign-resources-alzheimers-disease-franchise) [[47]](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/01/31/business/biogen-alzheimers-aduhelm.html) [[48]](https://www.reuters.com/business/healthcare-pharmaceuticals/biogen-discontinue-development-older-alzheimers-drug-aduhelm-2024-01-31/) Christopher Viehbacher, Biogen's CEO, framed the decision as a "reprioritization" of the company's Alzheimer's disease franchise rather than an acknowledgment of efficacy or safety failures. [[1]](https://investors.biogen.com/news-releases/news-release-details/biogen-realign-resources-alzheimers-disease-franchise)

The discontinuation recorded a \$60 million one-time charge against earnings. [[47]](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/01/31/business/biogen-alzheimers-aduhelm.html) Biogen committed to continuing to supply monthly infusions to the small patient population that had initiated treatment, but no new patient enrollments would occur. [[47]](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/01/31/business/biogen-alzheimers-aduhelm.html)

The company explicitly redirected resources toward alternative Alzheimer's approaches:

1. **Lecanemab (Leqembi)**: The anti-amyloid antibody co-developed with and led clinically by Eisai showed more convincing efficacy. Lecanemab achieved FDA traditional (not accelerated) approval on July 6, 2023, based on Phase 3 CLARITY AD trial data demonstrating a CDR-SB difference of 0.45 points (27% slowing of decline) over 18 months, with a more favorable ARIA profile than aducanumab. [[44]](https://www.pharmavoice.com/news/alzheimers-disease-lecanemab-leqembi-fda-aduhelm-pipeline/685284/) Both Biogen and Eisai co-commercialize lecanemab with equal economics.

2. **BIIB080**: An antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) developed by Ionis Pharmaceuticals and licensed by Biogen, targeting tau protein production rather than amyloid. Tau, the second major pathological hallmark in Alzheimer's disease, emerged as a complementary therapeutic target after aducanumab's failure suggested that amyloid reduction alone may be insufficient. [[49]](https://www.ajmc.com/view/biogen-abandons-aducanumab-pivots-focus-to-lecanemab-for-alzheimer-disease)

3. **BIIB113**: An oral small-molecule tau aggregation inhibitor representing a shift toward multi-target and oral administration strategies. [[49]](https://www.ajmc.com/view/biogen-abandons-aducanumab-pivots-focus-to-lecanemab-for-alzheimer-disease)

4. **Lecanemab long-term studies**: Continuation of extended follow-up beyond the initial 18-month trial, potentially demonstrating sustained clinical benefit as the disease progresses.

This strategic realignment reflected a broader field recognition that single-pathway amyloid targeting, despite aggressive pursuit across multiple failed programs, had proven to be a limited therapeutic approach in symptomatic Alzheimer's disease.

## What Survived: Neurimmune's Rights Reversion and Future Development

When rights to aducanumab reverted to Neurimmune on January 31, 2024, the Swiss biotech company announced plans to pursue development of new aducanumab-based therapeutics. [[9]](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/01/31/2821023/0/en/Neurimmune-to-regain-global-rights-to-brain-amyloid-depleter-for-the-treatment-of-Alzheimer-s-disease.html) Specifically, Neurimmune stated it would develop subcutaneously administered formulations of aducanumab-based medicines for early intervention in Alzheimer's disease. [[9]](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2024/01/31/2821023/0/en/Neurimmune-to-regain-global-rights-to-brain-amyloid-depleter-for-the-treatment-of-Alzheimer-s-disease.html) [[50]](https://www.alzforum.org/news/community-news/adieu-aduhelm-biogen-stops-marketing-antibody)

Subcutaneous administration (injections under the skin, rather than monthly IV infusions) would offer practical advantages: patients could potentially self-administer at home rather than visiting infusion centers monthly, improving convenience and potentially widening accessibility. However, as of September 8, 2026, Neurimmune has not disclosed a regulatory pathway, timeline, or probability estimate for advancing these programs. The company retains the underlying intellectual property, clinical data, and manufacturing technology, but the challenges facing aducanumab (weak clinical efficacy, regulatory controversy, safety profile) remain unresolved.

## The Amyloid Hypothesis and Ongoing Scientific Debate

Aduhelm's failure and the subsequent success of lecanemab and donanemab have not resolved the fundamental scientific question: Is the amyloid-beta cascade hypothesis correct, or does aducanumab's failure simply reflect inadequate drug design or trial execution?

The amyloid hypothesis posits that pathological accumulation of amyloid-beta peptide initiates a neurotoxic cascade leading to tau pathology, neuroinflammation, and neurodegeneration. This model has dominated Alzheimer's research for decades and motivated the development of dozens of anti-amyloid therapeutics, most of which failed. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC6208728) [[8]](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article/146/10/3969/7162122) Critics point out that amyloid plaques are common in cognitively normal elderly individuals and that several cognitively normal autopsy subjects have substantial amyloid pathology, suggesting amyloid accumulation alone is insufficient for dementia. [[51]](https://sciencepolicyreview.pubpub.org/pub/bt3c7x0w/release/3)

Lecanemab's recent traditional approval and donanemab's FDA approval in July 2024 provide some vindication for amyloid targeting, as both demonstrate modest (27-29% slowing) but real cognitive benefits. [[52]](https://mindthegulf.com/article.php?id=111) [[53]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC11439562) However, the effect sizes are considerably smaller than might be hoped if amyloid reduction were the primary driver of neurodegeneration. Network meta-analyses comparing donanemab, lecanemab, aducanumab, and other approaches suggest that donanemab and lecanemab are significantly more effective than placebo, but whether their superiority reflects better target engagement, better biomarker selection of patients, or other factors remains incompletely understood. [[54]](https://dig.pharmacy.uic.edu/faqs/2024-2/sep-2024-faqs/what-evidence-supports-the-efficacy-and-safety-of-donanemab-for-alzheimer-disease/)

The fact that the field continues pursuing anti-amyloid therapies despite aducanumab's failure suggests that the amyloid hypothesis, while contested, is not abandoned. Rather, the field appears to be converging on more selective patient populations (those with early cognitive impairment and confirmed amyloid pathology), more aggressive biomarker validation strategies, and combination approaches targeting both amyloid and tau, rather than single-agent amyloid targeting.

## Regulatory and Scientific Legacy

Aduhelm's approval and subsequent discontinuation have significantly altered the FDA's approach to Alzheimer's disease drug development. Donanemab, another anti-amyloid antibody from Eli Lilly with more aggressive amyloid clearance, was initially denied accelerated approval in January 2023 and required to pursue traditional approval instead, which it obtained in July 2024. [[55]](https://www.alz.org/news/2024/alzheimers-drug-aducanumab-aduhelm-discontinued) This represents a tightening of the accelerated approval standard post-Aduhelm, suggesting the FDA elevated its evidentiary bar in response to congressional criticism.

The approval controversy also fueled scientific critiques of the amyloid hypothesis itself. Keith Herrup, a prominent biomedical ethicist, and George Perry, Editor-in-Chief of the Journal of Alzheimer's Disease, have questioned whether amyloid-beta reduction meets even the lower threshold of a "reasonably likely" surrogate endpoint, given the disconnect between amyloid pathology and cognitive decline in population studies. [[56]](https://beingpatient.com/aduhelm-drug-amyloid/) [[57]](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/aduhelm-great-expectations-fizzle)

The House Committee investigation led to proposals for greater transparency in FDA advisory committee processes and more robust documentation of pre-approval meetings between sponsors and regulators. While formal legislative reform has not occurred, the investigation elevated the political cost of ignoring advisory committee recommendations, making future Aduhelm-like scenarios less likely.

## Conclusion: A Failed Ambition, Contested Legacy

Aduhelm represents a convergence of scientific optimism, regulatory uncertainty, and commercial miscalculation. Neurimmune's discovery of a monoclonal antibody that robustly reduces brain amyloid was scientifically meaningful; Phase 1b results showing both biomarker and exploratory cognitive benefits represented genuine progress. Yet the Phase 3 trials, the ultimate test of clinical utility, yielded one positive and one negative result, creating fundamental ambiguity.

The FDA's decision to approve based on surrogate endpoint alone, overriding its own advisory committee's near-unanimous opposition, prioritized the amyloid hypothesis and accelerated access over rigorous evidence integration. Biogen's aggressive commercial pricing and launch planning, combined with unreported FDA meetings, lent credence to critics' concerns that the approval reflected commercial interest rather than scientific justification.

The subsequent events, including EMA rejection, the House Committee investigation, weak market uptake, failure to complete the required confirmatory trial, and ultimate discontinuation, reinforced the skepticism of the advisory committee and external experts. Yet they also illustrated a more complex narrative: one failed drug does not invalidate an entire therapeutic hypothesis, and the field has persisted in pursuing anti-amyloid approaches with greater attention to patient selection and biomarker validation.

For Neurimmune, the outcome was pyrrhic: the company regained assets and intellectual property but faces the challenge of advancing aducanumab-based therapeutics in a field now skeptical of first-generation approaches. For Biogen, the outcome prompted strategic realignment toward lecanemab and tau-targeting therapies that may ultimately prove more successful. For regulatory science, Aduhelm serves as a cautionary tale about the limits of surrogate endpoints and the hazards of overriding expert consensus without transparent justification.

The Aduhelm story will likely endure as a case study in pharmaceutical regulation, amyloid hypothesis validation, and the challenges of translating biomarker innovations into meaningful clinical benefit.
