# Dawei Special Economic Zone

The most consequential infrastructure saga centers on an ambitious port and industrial development spanning **250 square kilometers**. The project emerged from strategic logic: Myanmar needed port access to the Indian Ocean independent of Thailand's chokepoint at the Strait of Malacca, and a deep-sea facility at Dawei could serve as a regional shipping shortcut, particularly for landlocked Central Asian nations. [[3]](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17530350.2022.2098517) [[4]](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/asia/myanmars-dawei-port-megaproject-could-offer-regional-shipping-shortcut-viability-doubts-linger-6341866)

## The Timeline of Ambition and Collapse

**Initial Partnership (2008–2010)**

In May 2008, Myanmar and Thailand signed a memorandum of understanding initiating the complex. On November 2, 2010, Italian-Thai Development Public Co., Ltd. (ITD), a major Thai construction conglomerate, executed a formal framework agreement to develop the project. Myanmar passed the Special Economic Zone Law on January 27, 2011. [[12]](https://www.mpa.gov.mm/ports/dawei-deep-sea-port/) The combined vision was extraordinary: original cost estimates reached **\$58 billion** for the complete zone, with the **initial phase budgeted at \$8.6 billion** (later revised to **\$10.7 billion** in 2013 restructuring). [[1]](https://www.gem.wiki/Dawei_Deep_Sea_Port_and_Special_Economic_Zone)

The zone was designed as an integrated complex combining three core elements: a deep-sea port on the Andaman Sea, industrial zones for manufacturing, and supporting energy and water infrastructure. Zone planning identified an initial **405-hectare operational area (Zone A)** with a proposed **101-hectare extension (Zone B)** to the south. [[13]](https://www.daweisezmc.com/content/overview-dawei-sez) [[15]](http://www.daweisez.gov.mm/content/overview-dawei-sez)

**Coal Plant Cancellation: The First Major Reversal (2012)**

The centerpiece energy component was a **4,000-megawatt coal-fired thermal power plant**—among Southeast Asia's largest proposed coal projects. Designed to supply electricity for the industrial zone, it attracted regional opposition organized by the **Dawei Development Association** and **Tavoyan Women's Union**, which launched a "No Coal in Dawei" campaign in 2012. The campaign succeeded. On January 9–10, 2012, Myanmar's government announced cancellation following environmental pressure. [[2]](https://www.gem.wiki/Dawei_power_station)

Minister of Energy Khin Maung Soe issued the most widely quoted official statement: "We made the decision to halt this project after reading the concerns about the environmental impact of this plant in local media reports." [[17]](https://www.powermag.com/myanmar-halts-construction-of-4-gw-coal-plant-on-environmental-worries/) This cancellation marked the first major retreat from the original master plan, though smaller alternatives were proposed: a **36-megawatt, 50-megawatt, 180-megawatt, 400-megawatt**, and **500-megawatt gas-fired plant**, none of which were subsequently built. [[18]](https://gastopowerjournal.com/uncategorized/myanmar-abandons-plans-to-build-dawai-coal-power-plant-favours-gas-fired-plants/)

**Suspension and Restructuring (2013–2015)**

ITD pressed forward despite the coal setback, reportedly investing **\$189 million** in preliminary works by 2013. However, construction activity deteriorated sharply. In November 2013, Myanmar government authorities announced a comprehensive suspension and restructuring of ITD's role. The SEZ would be redesigned; ITD would be replaced as lead developer. [[5]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawei_Port_Project)

By August 2015, Myanmar and Thailand announced revised plans with Japan joining as a "third equal partner," boosting investor confidence. [[14]](https://icsf.net/newss/myanmar-the-dawei-special-economic-zone/) The restructured initial phase was downsized to **\$1.7 billion**. [[11]](https://thailand-construction.com/russia-eyes-long-stalled-dawei-megaproject-in-myanmar/) Despite this revision, construction did not resume meaningfully. The port remained unbuilt. The industrial zones remained vacant. The zone existed on paper.

**Thai Consortium Termination (January 2021)**

On January 15–18, 2021, Myanmar's Dawei Special Economic Zone Management Committee formally terminated ITD's concession agreements, citing repeated failure to submit required financial and operational documents and breach of payment obligations. [[19]](https://thailand-construction.com/myanmar-government-terminates-thai-led-consortiums-involvement-in-dawei-mega-project/) [[21]](https://www.irrawaddy.com/factiva/myanmar-terminates-thai-led-consortiums-involvement-dawei-mega-project.html) ITD contested the allegation, "preparing a letter challenging" the accusations. [[20]](https://www.irrawaddy.com/news/burma/myanmar-says-thai-firm-dawei-sez-breached-contract.html) The termination notice covered **seven separate concession agreements** for various zone components. [[22]](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/2053671/prayut-set-on-repairing-cancelled-dawei-contract) [[23]](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/2051839/itd-seeks-state-help-over-dawei-issue)

The formal termination statement emphasized: "Italian-Thai Development failed to make concession payments and submit documents." [[21]](https://www.irrawaddy.com/factiva/myanmar-terminates-thai-led-consortiums-involvement-dawei-mega-project.html) Thai Prime Minister Prayut Chan-ocha declared Thailand remained committed to resurrecting the project and negotiated with Myanmar to find a replacement developer. [[22]](https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/2053671/prayut-set-on-repairing-cancelled-dawei-contract) No replacement materialized.

## Current Status: Russian Pivot and Military Operations (2025–2026)

After a four-year hiatus, the Russian Federation entered the picture. On **February 23–25, 2025**, Russia's Minister of Economic Development Maxim Reshetnikov and Myanmar's Minister of Investment and Foreign Economic Relations Kan Zaw signed an investment cooperation memorandum covering Dawei SEZ, proposing a deep-sea port, **coal-fired thermal power plant, and oil refinery**. [[31]](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/asean/40047113) [[32]](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/asean/40046710) [[33]](https://www.irrawaddy.com/business/myanmar-junta-inks-deal-with-russia-to-kickstart-dawei-sez-project.html)

In March 2025, during discussions between President Putin and Myanmar military leader Min Aung Hlaing in Moscow, Russia proposed additional components: a **110-megawatt nuclear power plant using Russian technology**. [[31]](https://www.nationthailand.com/news/asean/40047113) On **June 20, 2025**, in St. Petersburg, Russia and Myanmar formalized the investment agreement. [[34]](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/russia-signs-investment-deal-with-myanmar-sees-offshore-oil-gas-prospects-2025-06-20/) The Russian plan specified a **660-megawatt coal-fired thermal power plant** as the energy component. [[34]](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/russia-signs-investment-deal-with-myanmar-sees-offshore-oil-gas-prospects-2025-06-20/) [[16]](https://www.irrawaddy.com/business/moscow-agrees-to-build-power-plant-for-myanmars-stalled-dawei-sez.html)

However, the military terrain became contested. Starting in late July 2026, Myanmar's military junta deployed **approximately 700 troops** into the Dawei zone area, targeting land designated for the Russian project. On **August 21, 2026**, Reuters reported: "Myanmar's military has deployed hundreds of troops to forcibly clear areas marked for a Russia-backed special economic zone in the country's south, burning villages and sealing off the zone from outside observation." [[35]](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/world/2026/08/21/myanmar-military-offensive-targets-land-for-russia-backed-port-project) [[36]](https://www.asiaone.com/asia/myanmar-military-offensive-targets-land-russia-backed-port-project) [[37]](https://bworldonline.com/world/2026/08/21/771722/myanmar-military-offensive-targets-land-for-russia-backed-port-project/) [[38]](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/se-asia/myanmar-military-offensive-targets-land-for-russia-backed-port-project)

The Tanintharyi Region People's Defence Force (a local armed group opposed to the military junta) resisted the clearance operations, sparking armed conflict. Reuters noted it "could not independently verify" reports of village burning or three detained aid workers (reportedly killed July 9, 2026, by MAGGA Initiative records). [[35]](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/world/2026/08/21/myanmar-military-offensive-targets-land-for-russia-backed-port-project) [[36]](https://www.asiaone.com/asia/myanmar-military-offensive-targets-land-russia-backed-port-project) The military offensive represented the clearest signal that the junta intended to use force to reclaim land for the Russian-backed project, reversing the pattern of suspension.

**Current Status:** The Dawei SEZ remains **suspended and contested**. No port has been constructed. No industrial zones are operational. The zone exists as designated land, contested between military forces, Russian commitments (without financial execution), and local armed resistance.
