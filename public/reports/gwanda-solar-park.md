# The Gwanda Solar Project

## Project Conception and Tender Award

The Gwanda Solar Project was conceived as a critical renewable energy installation intended to address Zimbabwe's electricity crisis. On October 23, 2015, the Zimbabwe Power Company (ZPC) awarded a \$172.8 million Engineering, Procurement, and Construction contract to Intratrek Zimbabwe Pvt Ltd, a company owned by controversial businessman Wicknell Chivayo. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[2]](https://globalbusinessoutlook.com/energy/zimbabwe-government-faces-heat-over-alleged-irregularities-involving-gwanda-solar-project/) The project scope specified construction of a 100 megawatt photovoltaic power generation facility on a 262-hectare site within Gwanda district, with planned connection to the national grid via a 33 kilovolt (KV) power transmission line. [[2]](https://globalbusinessoutlook.com/energy/zimbabwe-government-faces-heat-over-alleged-irregularities-involving-gwanda-solar-project/) [[6]](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/gwanda-solar-project-in-zimbabwe-10-years-later-and-5mln-spent-wk6m531l) The contract represented one of Zimbabwe's largest renewable energy commitments at the time, reflecting government strategy to reduce dependence on hydroelectric power from Lake Kariba and address chronic electricity shortages affecting the mining and manufacturing sectors. [[27]](https://theenergypioneer.com/amid-hydropower-crisis-zimbabweans-turn-to-solar-energy/)

The project site, located within Gwanda district boundaries, represented a strategic location for solar energy generation given the region's high solar irradiance and proximity to existing mining and agricultural infrastructure. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/)

## Construction Phase and Mobilization (2016–2018)

Mobilization began in 2016 following contract signature. By 2018, Intratrek Zimbabwe had advanced approximately \$5.7 million in initial project costs, with the company claiming these funds were expended on site acquisition, preliminary surveying, and engineering design work. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[4]](https://cite.org.zw/stalled-gwanda-solar-project-raises-concerns-parliamentary-visit-exposes-lack-of-progress/) However, critical infrastructure dependencies were not fulfilled during this period. Specifically, the 33 KV power transmission line connecting the solar facility to the main grid had not been constructed, and communications infrastructure necessary for facility operations remained incomplete. [[4]](https://cite.org.zw/stalled-gwanda-solar-project-raises-concerns-parliamentary-visit-exposes-lack-of-progress/) [[8]](https://www.esi-africa.com/industry-sectors/generation/zimbabwe-gwanda-solar-project-not-making-progress/) These gaps, though distinct from the solar generation technology itself, represented binding conditions precedent under the original contract—contingencies that prevented full operational commissioning despite partial site development.

Parliamentary inspections conducted in May 2024 found evidence of limited infrastructure deployment: the site contained partially completed civil works, administrative structures affected by termite infestation, and only a single solar panel installed for security lighting purposes, underscoring the minimal technical progress despite the contractual obligation and advance payments. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[4]](https://cite.org.zw/stalled-gwanda-solar-project-raises-concerns-parliamentary-visit-exposes-lack-of-progress/)

## Contract Termination and Legal Dispute (April 2018–December 2023)

In April 2018, approximately three years after contract award and two years after mobilization, the Zimbabwe Power Company unilaterally terminated the engineering, procurement, and construction contract with Intratrek Zimbabwe, citing "lapse of conditions precedent"—asserting that contractual milestones for infrastructure completion had not been met and that the contract had reached a termination trigger under its provisions. [[3]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/zpc-intratek-revive-abandoned-gwanda-solar-project/) [[5]](https://www.thezimbabwean.co/2024/03/zpc-intratek-revive-abandoned-gwanda-solar-project/) [[8]](https://www.esi-africa.com/industry-sectors/generation/zimbabwe-gwanda-solar-project-not-making-progress/) The demobilization order halted all on-site activity, though the 262-hectare facility site remained under notional project control with basic security provision.

Wicknell Chivayo and Intratrek Zimbabwe contested the termination through Zimbabwe's Supreme Court. In December 2023, nearly five years after termination, the Zimbabwe Supreme Court issued a judgment affirming the contract's validity and rejecting ZPC's grounds for termination. [[7]](https://mbaretimes.com/2024/03/gwanda-solar-project/) The court simultaneously ordered ZPC to honor its contractual obligations to resume project execution. [[7]](https://mbaretimes.com/2024/03/gwanda-solar-project/) However, the Supreme Court ruling did not automatically restore on-site mobilization or secure the financing prerequisites for recommencement.

## Project Status: Suspension and Abandonment (2018–2024)

Between April 2018 and November 2024—a six-year hiatus—the project site experienced progressive deterioration. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[2]](https://globalbusinessoutlook.com/energy/zimbabwe-government-faces-heat-over-alleged-irregularities-involving-gwanda-solar-project/) [[6]](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/gwanda-solar-project-in-zimbabwe-10-years-later-and-5mln-spent-wk6m531l) Photographs documented by news organizations in November 2024 revealed heavily overgrown vegetation across the 262-hectare site, termite-damaged structures, and absence of active construction activity. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[6]](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/gwanda-solar-project-in-zimbabwe-10-years-later-and-5mln-spent-wk6m531l) The solar panels that had been installed were either removed or cannibalized; only a single panel remained operational for security lighting at the facility's perimeter. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[6]](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/gwanda-solar-project-in-zimbabwe-10-years-later-and-5mln-spent-wk6m531l) The water systems supporting site operations had been compromised, and equipment left on-site had begun to degrade.

In March 2024, following the December 2023 Supreme Court ruling, ZPC and Intratek Zimbabwe announced a formal agreement to revive the project with joint operational responsibility. [[3]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/zpc-intratek-revive-abandoned-gwanda-solar-project/) [[5]](https://www.thezimbabwean.co/2024/03/zpc-intratek-revive-abandoned-gwanda-solar-project/) [[7]](https://mbaretimes.com/2024/03/gwanda-solar-project/) [[10]](https://www.zimlive.com/zpc-intratek-revive-abandoned-gwanda-solar-project/) The announcement stated that mobilization would resume and that project completion would be pursued in phases. However, as of November 2024—eight months after this announcement—no substantive evidence of resumed on-site activity was documented in available sources. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[6]](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/gwanda-solar-project-in-zimbabwe-10-years-later-and-5mln-spent-wk6m531l) Parliamentary follow-up visits in May 2024 found conditions unchanged, with infrastructure deterioration continuing and no evidence of mobilization preparations. [[4]](https://cite.org.zw/stalled-gwanda-solar-project-raises-concerns-parliamentary-visit-exposes-lack-of-progress/)

## Project Impact and Local Response

The extended suspension generated significant frustration among Gwanda residents and the broader energy sector. The project's failure to deliver its intended 100 megawatt output perpetuated Zimbabwe's electricity deficit, contributing to ongoing national loadshedding that affected industrial productivity and domestic energy access. [[1]](https://www.zimbabwesituation.com/news/pictures-gwanda-solar-project-10-years-later-us5-million-spent/) [[9]](https://www.newsday.co.zw/southerneye/local/article/200013738/failed-gwanda-solar-project-angers-residents) The stalled project also represented a loss of immediate employment: the original engineering and construction phase had been projected to generate skilled and semi-skilled employment during the 2016–2018 mobilization period. [[8]](https://www.esi-africa.com/industry-sectors/generation/zimbabwe-gwanda-solar-project-not-making-progress/)

## Status Classification and Technical Assessment

The Gwanda Solar Project is classified as **suspended** rather than abandoned or cancelled. The contract remains legally valid, and both parties nominally retain obligations under the December 2023 Supreme Court judgment. However, the project exhibits characteristics of functional abandonment: no meaningful technical progress for six consecutive years, progressive site deterioration, and absence of operational capacity despite contractual authority to resume. The suspension reflects not technological failure of solar photovoltaic generation systems themselves—a proven and increasingly deployed technology in Zimbabwe and globally—but rather a failure of project governance, financing continuity, and contractual execution. The solar technology and engineering design remain viable; the discontinuation stems from organizational, financial, and contractual dysfunction between the generating entity and the purchasing authority.
