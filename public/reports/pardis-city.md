# Pardis City

## What Was Planned

Between 2001 and 2011, the Iranian government contracted with Turkish developer Kuzu Group to build Pardis City, a "readymade community" designed as a self-contained satellite municipality 50 kilometers east of Tehran in the foothills of Mount Damavand. [[23]](https://www.indiatimes.com/news/world/this-is-pardis-a-city-in-the-middle-of-a-desert-now-a-ghost-town/articleshow/122541048.html) The project encompassed approximately 37,000 housing units with supporting infrastructure: hospitals, schools, parks, public transportation systems, mosques, and bazaars. [[22]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/iran-blog/2014/jan/30/irans-economy-struggles-to-support-ahmadinejads-ill-conceived-housing-vision) The underlying policy framework was the Mehr (meaning "sun") housing scheme, authorized during 2005–2011 under President Mahmoud Ahmadinejad's administration, targeting 1.5 million units nationally across multiple satellite developments. [[22]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/iran-blog/2014/jan/30/irans-economy-struggles-to-support-ahmadinejads-ill-conceived-housing-vision)

## Timeline and Occupancy

Construction progressed through 2001–2011, with initial phases (1–3) achieving partial completion and occupancy by approximately 2014. Approximately 74,000 units were completed or substantially completed across phases 1–3, attracting a resident population comprising Afghan migrant workers (~30%), low-income Iranian families, and approximately 90% middle-class commuters traveling daily to central Tehran due to inadequate intra-city transportation integration. [[23]](https://www.indiatimes.com/news/world/this-is-pardis-a-city-in-the-middle-of-a-desert-now-a-ghost-town/articleshow/122541048.html)

## Critical Infrastructure Failures

By 2013, the Mehr housing scheme had become infamous for systematic defects: faulty sewage systems, inadequate heating in winter, intermittent electrical supply, and chronically insufficient water. [[22]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/iran-blog/2014/jan/30/irans-economy-struggles-to-support-ahmadinejads-ill-conceived-housing-vision) Across the entire Mehr scheme (not just Pardis), approximately 200,000 units lacked reliable utilities. A 2017 earthquake compounded these defects, causing structural damage to completed buildings. Satellite imagery from 2019 onward showed Pardis phases 4+ as complete but uninhabited shells—vast housing complexes containing zero residents, zero vehicles, and no visible human occupation.

## Geographic Connection and Transportation Dysfunction

Pardis occupied arid terrain administratively within Tehran province but intended as a separate satellite city. The location proved strategically isolated: no connection to Tehran's metro system, highway exits terminating in dead ends or military-controlled territory, and no integrated public transit plan. Residents faced daily 2–3 hour commutes by private vehicle or bus. [[23]](https://www.indiatimes.com/news/world/this-is-pardis-a-city-in-the-middle-of-a-desert-now-a-ghost-town/articleshow/122541048.html) This transportation dysfunction made Pardis a "bedroom community" only in name—an economically non-productive appendage to Tehran.

## Why It Ended: Economic Collapse

The project underwent developer withdrawal beginning in 2018. The primary economic cause was escalating inflation: the Ahmadinejad-era monetary expansion (printing currency to finance subsidies and megaprojects) had created persistent double-digit and eventually triple-digit inflation rates. Combined with sanctions pressure following international disputes, the inflation rate rendered the developer's economic model unviable—construction costs outpaced government financing and buyer purchasing power.

The secondary trigger was the 2018 U.S. withdrawal from the Iran nuclear deal and re-imposition of comprehensive sanctions. International bank financing froze entirely. Kuzu Group, reliant on foreign currency flows and international banking, withdrew from Iran in 2018. [[22]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/iran-blog/2014/jan/30/irans-economy-struggles-to-support-ahmadinejads-ill-conceived-housing-vision) [[24]](https://iransofaraway.substack.com/p/irans-three-million-home-lie-inside) The developer's contract was unilaterally voided; Turkish workers departed; construction ceased.

## Current Status and Financial Legacy

As of October 2025, Pardis City remains in a state of partial abandonment with partial occupancy. Phases 1–3 remain inhabited by approximately 74,000 residents despite infrastructure deficits. Phases 4 onward contain approximately 11,000 unfinished units as of 2025, with no active construction documented since 2018. Contemporary satellite photographs show "no cars, no people, no sign of life" in abandoned phases—imagery unchanged between 2019 and 2024. [[23]](https://www.indiatimes.com/news/world/this-is-pardis-a-city-in-the-middle-of-a-desert-now-a-ghost-town/articleshow/122541048.html) [[24]](https://iransofaraway.substack.com/p/irans-three-million-home-lie-inside)

The broader economic reckoning: the Rouhani administration formally recognized the Mehr scheme as responsible for approximately 40% of excess liquidity creation in the economy, directly contributing to persistent inflation. The scheme generated stranded assets for both the state (unrecovered loans) and individual purchasers (properties without utility connections, declining nominal value as currency depreciated).

**Kuzu Group contract value**: Not documented in available sources.

**Bank-e Maskan (Housing Bank) loans**: \$10.2 billion in total Mehr scheme financing as of January 2011.

The Pardis case represents not technical construction failure but economic model collapse: an infrastructure project designed in an era of capital abundance (2001–2011) became economically impossible to complete in an era of capital scarcity and sanctions.
