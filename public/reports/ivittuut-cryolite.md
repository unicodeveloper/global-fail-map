# Ivittuut

## What Was Attempted

On the Arsuk Fjord in southwestern Greenland, approximately 5 kilometers south of the Danish naval base at Kangilinnguit, the settlement of Ivittuut was established to support extraction of cryolite—a rare fluorine-aluminum mineral essential as a flux in aluminum smelting [[28]](https://detectivetiger.com/stories/greenlands-forgotten-mining-town-that-helped-shape-history/). Discovered and initially mined by Inuit communities, Ivittuut became the site of the world's largest cryolite reserve and the center of global cryolite supply for over a century [[28]](https://detectivetiger.com/stories/greenlands-forgotten-mining-town-that-helped-shape-history/).

## Operational Scale and Production

Mining operations began formally in **1854** and continued uninterrupted for **133 years** until **1987** [[31]](https://ecos.au.dk/en/researchconsultancy/themes/mining-and-environment/ivittuut-cryolite-mine). The scale of extraction was enormous: Ivittuut produced approximately **3.7 million tons of crude ore**, from which approximately **2.15 million tons of cryolite** was extracted [[31]](https://ecos.au.dk/en/researchconsultancy/themes/mining-and-environment/ivittuut-cryolite-mine).

Production peaked during two distinct periods:
- **1942 (World War II era)**: 86,000 tons produced in a single year, supporting Allied aluminum production for military aircraft [[29]](https://fluoridealert.org/news/how-this-abandoned-mining-town-in-greenland-helped-win-world-war-ii/)
- **1985 (final year of high production)**: 110,000 tons produced [[29]](https://fluoridealert.org/news/how-this-abandoned-mining-town-in-greenland-helped-win-world-war-ii/)

By the final decade, production fell sharply as the economic case for mining deteriorated.

## Why It Ended

Ivittuut's closure resulted from the convergence of two factors: **resource depletion and technology substitution**. The high-grade cryolite ore accessible through open-pit mining had become depleted by the 1980s [[30]](https://www.amusingplanet.com/2020/01/the-abandoned-cryolite-mining-town-of.html). Simultaneously, synthetic cryolite production from fluorspar became commercially competitive and cheaper than natural mining in the 1960s, fundamentally shifting the economics of Ivittuut [[30]](https://www.amusingplanet.com/2020/01/the-abandoned-cryolite-mining-town-of.html). The combination made continued extraction economically unviable. The mine closed permanently in **1987**, ending 133 years of continuous operation [[31]](https://ecos.au.dk/en/researchconsultancy/themes/mining-and-environment/ivittuut-cryolite-mine).

## Settlement Abandonment and Current Status

The entire mining settlement was abandoned by approximately **2000** [[16]](https://www.muchbetteradventures.com/magazine/greenland-ghost-towns/). Today, Ivittuut stands as a ghost town: approximately **seven buildings remain standing** in varying states of preservation, with most structures deteriorated [[29]](https://fluoridealert.org/news/how-this-abandoned-mining-town-in-greenland-helped-win-world-war-ii/). The open-pit mine itself is flooded. The settlement includes remains of World War II–era military infrastructure, evidence of the site's strategic importance during the conflict [[29]](https://fluoridealert.org/news/how-this-abandoned-mining-town-in-greenland-helped-win-world-war-ii/), and an abandoned cemetery [[30]](https://www.amusingplanet.com/2020/01/the-abandoned-cryolite-mining-town-of.html).

**Importantly, as of 2026, Ivittuut is not permanently abandoned.** Eclipse Metals Ltd. holds an active exploration license (MEL2007-45) for the cryolite deposit, with the license renewed most recently in **September 2025 through 2027** [[31]](https://ecos.au.dk/en/researchconsultancy/themes/mining-and-environment/ivittuut-cryolite-mine). In **October 2025**, Eclipse Metals raised **Australian \$4 million** in capital to fund feasibility studies and environmental/social impact assessments for potential mining resumption [[31]](https://ecos.au.dk/en/researchconsultancy/themes/mining-and-environment/ivittuut-cryolite-mine). Drilling operations were conducted in 2025 to evaluate ore remaining in the deposit and assess modern extraction viability [[31]](https://ecos.au.dk/en/researchconsultancy/themes/mining-and-environment/ivittuut-cryolite-mine).

Thus, Ivittuut represents a case where infrastructure and settlement were abandoned, but the underlying mineral deposit remains under active development consideration. The town itself may not reopen, but the resource exploitation is far from permanently concluded.

## Environmental Legacy

Ivittuut's 133-year operation left behind an environmental footprint. Contamination of the Arsuk Fjord with lead and zinc from mining operations is documented, though measured concentrations are declining at an approximate rate of **4% annually since 1982**, with elevated levels persisting within a **5-kilometer radius** of the former mining site [[31]](https://ecos.au.dk/en/researchconsultancy/themes/mining-and-environment/ivittuut-cryolite-mine).
