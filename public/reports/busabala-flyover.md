# The Busabala Flyover

## What Was Proposed

The Busabala Flyover, formally the Munyonyo Southern Bypass and Busabala junction improvements, was designed to decongest traffic on the southern approaches to Kampala by creating a grade-separated interchange on the route between Kampala's city center and the Entebbe Expressway. The project was contracted to China State Construction Engineering Corporation (CSCEC) as part of the broader road rehabilitation program funded through government budgets and development partnerships.

## Timeline and Geographic Connection

The project's exact start date is not clearly documented in available sources, but construction activity was evident by the early 2020s. The site location is the Busabala junction near Munyonyo in the southern approach to Kampala, a high-traffic corridor connecting the city center to Entebbe International Airport and points south. The incomplete structure—a partially constructed elevated roadway—remains physically visible and unfinished as of September 2026.

## Status and What Ended

**Status: ABANDONED** (contractor withdrawn due to non-payment).

The flyover was abandoned by the contractor following a broader government payment crisis. CSCEC, the contracting firm, demanded outstanding payment of Sh43 billion and eventually ceased work and departed the site. This followed a larger pattern: by August 2026, the Ministry of Works and Transport owed contractors across all road projects cumulative arrears of Sh1.351 trillion, rendering many major contractors unable to continue operations [[1]](https://www.newvision.co.ug/category/news/contractors-abandon-road-works-over-sh1351-tr-NV_210488_082026) [[15]](https://www.newvision.co.ug/category/news/contractors-abandon-road-works-over-sh1351-tr-NV_210488). The Busabala Flyover became a casualty of this systemic cash crisis.

## Why It Mattered, and What Survived

The Busabala Flyover, if completed, would have addressed a critical chokepoint on Kampala's only direct expressway to the airport and southern regions, reducing congestion-related delays and improving commercial throughput for one of East Africa's busiest transport corridors. The incomplete structure itself survived, but in a state of dangerous disrepair. Road surfaces remain unpaved or poorly maintained around the flyover site; road signs and safety barriers have not been installed. This degraded condition contributed directly to a tragedy on **May 3, 2025**, when Rajiv Ruparelia, the 35-year-old managing director of the Ruparelia Group and son of Uganda's billionaire Sudhir Ruparelia, was driving a high-performance Nissan GTR along the Kajjansi–Munyonyo Road near the Busabala Flyover junction [[36]](https://www.billionaires.africa/2025/05/03/rajiv-ruparelia-only-son-of-ugandas-richest-man-dies-in-car-crash/) [[37]](https://africa.businessinsider.com/local/lifestyle/from-exile-to-empire-how-sudhir-ruparelia-built-one-of-africas-billion-dollar-empires/bnqvwgg). According to police reports, Ruparelia lost control of his vehicle after colliding with temporary pavements associated with the incomplete infrastructure works, resulting in a fatal crash [[36]](https://www.billionaires.africa/2025/05/03/rajiv-ruparelia-only-son-of-ugandas-richest-man-dies-in-car-crash/). His death became a tragic emblem of how incomplete infrastructure poses ongoing safety hazards to the public long after abandonment.
