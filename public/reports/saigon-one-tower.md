# Saigon One Tower

Saigon One Tower (later known as IFC One Saigon), located at 34A Tôn Đức Thắng Street in District 1, stands as one of Ho Chi Minh City's most visible monuments to failed ambition. The location is prime: the address sits on the bank of the Saigon River in the central business district, meters from the iconic Bitexco Financial Tower. The structure itself—a 641-foot (approximately 195-meter) reinforced concrete skeleton—has dominated the skyline for over a decade as an incomplete shell.

Development began in 2007–2008, during the real estate boom that preceded the 2008 global financial crisis and Vietnam's subsequent 2011 housing crisis. Saigon M&C Real Estate JSC led the project, with Dong A Bank providing financing estimated at approximately \$228 million. [[4]](https://vietnaminsiders.com/insider-story-unfinished-buildings-in-saigon/) [[18]](https://e.vnexpress.net/photo/news/abandoned-saigon-skyscraper-gets-facelift-after-11-years-4506916.html) The original vision was to create a premium office and residential tower that would rank among the tallest buildings in Vietnam.

Construction halted in 2012, immediately following the onset of Vietnam's severe real estate contraction. However, the cessation of work was not merely a temporary pause. The project faced multiple structural challenges: mismanagement from its inception, unresolved land title and resettlement compensation disputes with residents occupying the site, and entanglement with broader governance failures. Most significantly, the project became connected to fraud allegations and financial misconduct. Dong A Bank itself faced severe distress; the bank's former chairperson, Truong My Lan, was later convicted of large-scale fraud and financial crimes. These governance failures created a complex web of liability that prevented any straightforward resolution.

Between 2012 and 2022, the tower stood as a derelict concrete frame—a ubiquitous landmark in photographs of Ho Chi Minh City's unfinished skyline and featured prominently in internet forums and photography essays documenting urban abandonment. In September 2022, visible work resumed when a contractor installed a distinctive glass facade with a dragon-scale aesthetic pattern. [[18]](https://e.vnexpress.net/photo/news/abandoned-saigon-skyscraper-gets-facelift-after-11-years-4506916.html) This work suggested a partial revival effort and new ownership or financing, potentially under the name IFC One Saigon.

However, by February 2026, the tower remained functionally incomplete. A detailed Reddit comment from February 2, 2026, confirmed that the building had received facade work but housed no operational businesses, tenants, or commercial activity. [[19]](https://www.reddit.com/r/VietNam/comments/1qtlm92/whats_this_building_behind_bitexco_looks/) The interior shell remains unfinished; the building has not achieved occupancy certificates or operational status. The concrete frame and partial glass facade survive as a physical structure, but the project as a development—an income-generating, occupied office and residential building—has not materialized despite fourteen years of elapsed time since construction halted.

The precise current ownership and legal status remain undocumented in the sources reviewed. The partial 2022 facade work may reflect an investor attempting to rehabilitate the asset or may be speculative work to increase land value. No official developer statement or Ho Chi Minh City government announcement regarding final resolution or demolition plans was found in 2026 sources. The tower thus remains in a suspended state: physically present, partially improved, but commercially non-functional and legally unresolved.
