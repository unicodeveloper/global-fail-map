# The Beirut to Damascus Railway

The Beirut-Damascus Railway stands as both achievement and ruin—a piece of infrastructure that succeeded spectacularly for 81 years before abandonment, not due to technical failure but to conflict. The railway was completed in December 1892, with the main port terminus at Beirut operational by that date, and received formal concession in 1893 for the Damascus-to-Al-Bireh line serving the broader Levantine region. [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Lebanon) The line was inaugurated on August 3-4, 1895, as a narrow-gauge railway connecting the Mediterranean port of Beirut with the interior cities of Damascus, Rayak, and beyond. [[14]](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1182854/124-years-ago-the-beirut-damascus-railway-line-was-born.html)

This 147-kilometer network served genuine economic purpose throughout the late Ottoman period and subsequent decades. At its peak, the Rayak station alone employed 2,500 workers managing the junction of multiple branch lines—an extraordinary concentration of railway infrastructure for a regional station. [[13]](https://syriauntold.com/2021/10/29/the-forgotten-railways-of-syria-and-lebanon-tales-of-a-missed-connection/) The railway connected Beirut's Mar Mikhael terminus directly to Damascus, Haifa, Tripoli, and other major commercial centers, enabling regional trade and passenger movement that would have been impossible overland. [[11]](https://www.plus961.com/2025/07/lebanons-forgotten-train-stations-a-journey-through-time/) The network remained operationally viable through most of the twentieth century, surviving the transition from Ottoman to French mandate rule after 1920, Lebanon's independence in 1943, and the early decades of the Lebanese Republic.

However, the Lebanese Civil War (1975-1990) severed the railway's operational existence. The final operational train departed in 1976, carrying a symbolic cargo of 800 goats from Beirut to Rayak under conductor Asaad Namrud—a mundane last journey that marked the end of 81 years of regional connectivity. [[12]](https://www.thedailybeast.com/lebanons-abandoned-train-stations-inside-these-sad-but-beautiful-gems/) [[14]](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1182854/124-years-ago-the-beirut-damascus-railway-line-was-born.html) The Mar Mikhael Railway Station, located in Beirut's port district and serving as the terminus for the entire network, stood among the infrastructure casualties of the port area's vulnerability to both civil conflict and subsequent disasters.

The railway never recovered. As of 2019, Lebanon's 403-404 kilometers of railway tracks lay unused and deteriorating across the country. [[14]](https://today.lorientlejour.com/article/1182854/124-years-ago-the-beirut-damascus-railway-line-was-born.html) [[15]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Lebanon) The line remained closed due to infrastructure decay, the absence of rolling stock, lack of maintenance funding, and the absence of any institutional commitment to restoration. Civil society organizations including Train-Train (founded in 2005) advocated for railway preservation and restoration, but no sustained government investment materialized. [[11]](https://www.plus961.com/2025/07/lebanons-forgotten-train-stations-a-journey-through-time/)

What survives, partially, is the material patrimony. The Mar Mikhael Railway Station building itself endured through the Lebanese Civil War and subsequent conflicts, but sustained damage during the August 4, 2020 Beirut port explosion. On August 4, 2020, ammonium nitrate stored in the port warehouse detonated in a series of massive explosions that killed 130-218 people (casualty figures vary across sources), injured thousands, and devastated port infrastructure and surrounding neighborhoods. [[33]](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/world/massive-explosion-shakes-lebanons-capital-beirut) [[34]](https://maritime-executive.com/article/dozens-dead-in-massive-blast-at-port-of-beirut) [[35]](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-lebanon-security-blast-idUSKCN25021M/) The blast destroyed an estimated 10,000-15,000 buildings and left approximately 300,000 people homeless. [[34]](https://maritime-executive.com/article/dozens-dead-in-massive-blast-at-port-of-beirut)

In response to the 2020 port explosion damage, UNESCO signed a restoration agreement with Lebanese authorities in July 2023, allocating approximately €2 million for rehabilitation of the Mar Mikhael station building. [[10]](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/how-beirut-is-rebuilding-after-explosion) Rehabilitation work commenced in September 2025, representing the first substantial institutional investment in railway heritage since the 1976 closure. [[10]](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/how-beirut-is-rebuilding-after-explosion) However, restoration of the station building does not imply restoration of the railway network itself—the tracks remain disused, ownership and operating authority remain ambiguous, and no credible restoration strategy for functional rail service has emerged from Lebanese authorities.

The railway represents infrastructure that successfully executed its intended function—moving goods and people across 147 kilometers—for eight decades, only to be abandoned not due to technical inadequacy but due to political collapse and the absence of institutional capacity to maintain it during conflict. The technology worked; the society that built it could not sustain it.
