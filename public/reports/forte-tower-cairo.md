# Forte Tower

While ancient and modern settlement design projects occupied the realm of territorial expansion, Cairo's mid-twentieth-century modernization produced a different category of abandoned project: individual monumental structures frozen mid-realization within the existing city.

Forte Tower, begun during Egypt's 1970s Sadat era, represents a specific category of failure in which political succession undermined developer confidence and regulatory protection. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had) The tower occupied Gezira Island in the Zamalek neighborhood of central Cairo—prime real estate in the heart of the capital. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had)

The project was conceived as the first phase of Sadat's ambition to create a "new Manhattan of Egypt," a cluster of skyscrapers on the banks of the Nile that would establish Cairo as a cosmopolitan financial center. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had) Forte Tower itself was designed as a 166-meter cylindrical structure with distinctive Islamic-pointed-arch windows crowning its upper floors—a visual synthesis of modernist verticality and Islamic architectural motifs. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had) The building was planned to function as a 450-room luxury hotel with restaurants, a nightclub, and other high-end commercial spaces. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had)

Construction proceeded to the point of concrete-shell completion, but the project never advanced to interior finishing or mechanical systems. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had) The structure remained "unfinished and abandoned" for approximately thirty years, its concrete skeleton visible as a Cairo landmark. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had) A single occupied space—a bedding and furnishings showroom—represented the minimal commercial use of the site during its decades of abandonment. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had)

The cause of abandonment reflects a specific vulnerability in politically dependent development: President Sadat's assassination in 1981 removed the original source of presidential patronage and developer access. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had) His successor, Hosni Mubarak, brought a different circle of favored developers and businessmen. The original Forte Tower investor lacked standing in the new patronage structure. Regulatory disputes and litigation from competing interests prevented completion. [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had) As explained by one observer of Cairo's development patterns: "A developer gets direct permission from the president, then another president comes in with his own circle of businessmen who want a piece of the cake. The original investor says no. The project is abandoned." [[4]](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2020/jun/23/unfinished-abandoned-demolished-how-cairo-is-losing-architecture-it-never-knew-it-had)

What survived Forte Tower is the concrete structure itself, visible as a symbol of failed development ambition—a monument to incompletion that stands in Cairo as a cautionary artifact. The building's persistent incompleteness illustrates how political succession, absent stable regulatory frameworks, can freeze development mid-construction indefinitely.
