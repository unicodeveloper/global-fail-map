# Credit Suisse

## Founding and Global Stature

Credit Suisse was founded on 5 July 1856 in Zurich and headquartered at Paradeplatz, the city's financial center. [[5]](https://my957.com/2023/03/18/how-credit-suisse-has-evolved-over-167-years/) By the early 21st century, Credit Suisse had evolved into one of the world's 30 globally systemically important banks (GSIB), designated as such by the Financial Stability Board. [[1]](https://fortune.com/2023/03/20/ubs-credit-suisse-turning-point-historic-challenging-day-switzerland-banking-crisis/) The institution was a primary dealer for the U.S. Federal Reserve, managed approximately CHF 1.3 trillion in assets under management as of 2022, and employed more than 50,000 professionals globally, with 17,000 in Switzerland. [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_Suisse) Credit Suisse ranked among the most recognized Swiss institutions worldwide, with a history spanning 167 years and deep ties to Swiss wealth management, investment banking, and corporate finance.

## Decade of Cascading Scandals (2010–2022)

Credit Suisse's final decade was marked by a relentless sequence of regulatory failures, financial losses, and internal misconduct.

**Major Fraud and Loss Events:**
- **Greensill Capital Exposure**: Credit Suisse invested and lent CHF 10 billion to Greensill Capital, a supply chain finance company. When Greensill collapsed in 2021, Credit Suisse faced losses exceeding CHF 3 billion, revealing inadequate due diligence and risk controls.
- **Archegos Capital Management Collapse (March 2021)**: Credit Suisse sustained trading losses of CHF 5.5 billion (approximately USD 5.5 billion) from exposure to Archegos Capital Management, a family office that imploded due to undisclosed leverage. The loss was one of the largest trading losses in modern banking history and indicated severe weaknesses in Credit Suisse's market risk management and client monitoring systems.

**Regulatory and Compliance Failures:**
- **Suisse Secrets Investigation**: In 2021, the Financial Times and Organized Crime and Corruption Reporting Project (OCCRP) published extensive documentation of Credit Suisse's role in facilitating money laundering for corrupt foreign officials, drug traffickers, and human rights violators. Leaked client records (the "Suisse Secrets") revealed that Credit Suisse held accounts for clients with known ties to human rights abuses, corruption, and criminal activity, and that senior management had overlooked or ignored compliance red flags.
- **Multiple Regulatory Fines**: Credit Suisse faced cumulative regulatory fines in excess of USD 5 billion for various violations, including foreign exchange manipulation, sanctions evasion, and anti-money laundering failures.

## Financial Deterioration (2022–2023)

By 2022, Credit Suisse's financial condition had deteriorated sharply:
- Customer outflows reached CHF 111 billion in Q4 2022 alone as clients withdrew deposits in response to reputation damage and perceived financial instability.
- The bank reported a CHF 7.9 billion annual loss in 2022, eliminating the dividend and signaling operational distress.
- Shareholder equity was depleted by accumulated losses and litigation reserves.
- Credit Suisse's credit default swap spreads widened dramatically, indicating that market participants assigned high bankruptcy probability.

## The Emergency Merger (March 19, 2023)

On 19 March 2023, following intervention by the Swiss Federal Department of Finance, the Swiss National Bank (SNB), and the Swiss Financial Market Supervisory Authority (FINMA), UBS Group AG agreed to acquire Credit Suisse in an emergency rescue transaction. [[18]](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/media/display-page-ndp/en-20230319-tree.html) [[19]](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2023/03/20230319-mm-cs-ubs/) [[21]](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/ubs-take-over-credit-suisse-central-bank-2023-03-19/) FINMA approved the merger on 19 March 2023 and deployed extraordinary regulatory measures, including exemptions from standard bankruptcy procedures, to enable the transaction to proceed without formal insolvency proceedings. [[19]](https://www.finma.ch/en/news/2023/03/20230319-mm-cs-ubs/) The Swiss National Bank provided liquidity assistance of CHF 100 billion to UBS to facilitate the acquisition and stabilize the combined entity. [[20]](https://amp.cnn.com/cnn/2023/03/23/investing/credit-suisse-ubs-impact-switzerland)

Under the terms of the merger agreement:
- UBS acquired Credit Suisse's total assets and assumed liabilities up to a specified threshold.
- The Swiss government provided a CHF 100 billion liquidity assistance loan to UBS.
- FINMA authorized UBS to acquire certain of Credit Suisse's debt instruments at nominal value, effectively eliminating CHF 16 billion in Credit Suisse subordinated debt while protecting senior creditors.
- The merged entity became substantially larger than Switzerland's GDP, creating a "systemic cornerstone institution" under Swiss law.

## Formal Dissolution

Credit Suisse Group AG was formally dissolved on 31 May 2024, approximately 14 months after the emergency merger. [[2]](https://everything-pr.com/credit-suisse) The legal entity ceased to exist; all assets, subsidiaries, and ongoing operations were consolidated into UBS or transferred to wind-down entities. The 167-year-old institution had ceased independent operations.

## Geographic Connection

Headquarters: Paradeplatz, Zurich; primary operations and regulation: Zurich, Switzerland; global operations: 50,000+ employees across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific regions.

## What Ended

Credit Suisse's independent corporate governance (19 March 2023); its status as an autonomous global systemically important bank (19 March 2023); its legal existence as a separate entity (31 May 2024); its distinct brand identity and client relationship management system; employment of thousands in Zurich and globally (significant workforce reductions executed by UBS post-acquisition); its proprietary technology infrastructure and back-office systems (integrated into UBS systems).

## What Survived

The asset base of approximately CHF 1.3 trillion (including the CHF 160 billion in assets transferred to a "bad bank" designated for wind-down) transferred to UBS and continued to serve institutional and private clients. The physical headquarters building on Paradeplatz in Zurich remained in use by UBS. Certain business units—particularly investment banking client relationships, wealth management franchises, and trading operations—were integrated into UBS's operating structure. The Swiss financial system benefited from the forced consolidation; UBS became approximately twice the size of Switzerland's GDP and was designated as a "global systemically important bank" by the Financial Stability Board, cementing Switzerland's position as a banking center despite the loss of a second GSIB.

## Causation and Failure Mechanisms

Credit Suisse's collapse resulted from three overlapping causes: (1) **endemic compliance and risk management failures** across client onboarding, market risk monitoring, and regulatory reporting; (2) **massive trading losses and bad loans** that eliminated capital buffers and depleted equity; and (3) **reputational contagion** that triggered deposit flight when clients lost confidence in management's ability to navigate regulatory and market risks. The 2023 collapse was not a "sudden" failure but rather the culmination of a decade of deterioration during which management failed to remedy known control failures or reshape the business model for a post-scandal environment.
