# Zira Island

## Design and Vision

In February 2009, the renowned Danish architectural firm Bjarke Ingels Group (BIG) released a master plan for the development of Zira Island (Böyük Zirə adası), a 1 million square meter landmass in the Caspian Sea within Baku Bay. [[18]](https://en.trend.az/azerbaijan/society/1415155.html) [[19]](https://www.fastcompany.com/90183959/azerbijans-futuristic-eco-island-plans-2) The project promised something unprecedented for the region: a carbon-neutral eco-resort and residential community designed to generate as much renewable energy as it consumed.

The design concept was poetic and ambitious. Buildings were to be shaped as silhouettes of Azerbaijan's seven famous mountains, creating a sculptural landscape that honored national geography while functioning as a self-sufficient energy system. [[17]](https://www.khl.com/news/seven-peaks-of-azerbaijan/31399.article) The masterplan incorporated renewable energy systems combining solar photovoltaic arrays and offshore wind farm generation; advanced waste management including organic waste composting; stormwater capture and recycling systems; and thermal heat pumps for climate control. [[19]](https://www.fastcompany.com/90183959/azerbijans-futuristic-eco-island-plans-2) [[20]](http://www.jetsongreen.com/2009/02/zira-island-planned-as-zero-energy-sustainable-development.html) [[21]](https://inhabitat.com/zira-island-by-big-architects/) The estimated cost was \$3 billion. [[33]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Khazar_Islands)

The project was contextualized within a global movement toward sustainable urbanism. Concurrent zero-carbon city initiatives like Dongtan (China) and Masdar City (United Arab Emirates) were under development. Zira Island positioned Azerbaijan as part of a forward-looking, environmentally conscious development paradigm—a sharp contrast to the country's legacy as the world's first oil-producing region and a center of petrochemical pollution. [[3]](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/article/2024/jul/29/worlds-first-oil-town-baku-fossil-fuel-legacy-cop29)

## Geographic Location

Zira Island is located at approximately 40°17′ North, 49°55′ East, positioned within the Caspian Sea south of Baku's Absheron Peninsula. [[24]](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B6y%C3%BCk_Zir%C9%99_adas%C4%B1) The island lies within Baku Bay, in the immediate vicinity of existing oil and gas extraction infrastructure.

## Abandonment: Land-Use Conflict and Institutional Paralysis

The Zira Island project was never formally cancelled. Instead, it encountered an insurmountable land-use conflict that made development impossible. The island sits atop or immediately adjacent to natural gas extraction infrastructure controlled by SOCAR (the State Oil Company of the Republic of Azerbaijan), the nation's largest energy producer. [[33]](https://grokipedia.com/page/Khazar_Islands) Developing the island as an eco-resort required evacuating or relocating active gas production equipment and associated pipelines—a technically feasible but economically unacceptable proposition given SOCAR's dominance in state energy policy and revenue.

Funding shortages, exacerbated by oil price volatility beginning in 2010, prevented any negotiated resolution. The project received no further government support after the initial announcement. No environmental impact assessments proceeded; no infrastructure construction began; no investor financing materialized.

## Current Status and Archival Survival

As of 2025, Zira Island remains undeveloped. The site appears in no contemporary development reports, government planning documents, or media coverage after 2009. The project exists only in archival form: BIG's published masterplan and architectural renderings, design documentation in professional journals, and conceptual discussions in sustainability literature. [[22]](https://www.detail-online.com/article/zira-island-masterplan-14027/) Bjarke Ingels Group continues to reference the project as a completed design exercise in the firm's professional portfolio, but it has never been constructed.

The silence following the 2009 announcement is the strongest evidence of abandonment. Legitimate projects generate continued discussion even during periods of suspension. Zira Island generated none. What survived is only the architectural concept—a demonstration of what a zero-carbon island community could look like, preserved in digital form and professional memory, but never realized in physical space or economic activity.
