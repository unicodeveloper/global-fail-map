# HVTN 702 Uhambo: The Dossier of a South African HIV Vaccine Trial That Failed with Integrity

In February 2020, after enrolling 5,407 participants across 14 sites in South Africa, the HVTN 702 trial announced that its experimental HIV vaccine had produced zero protective efficacy - the vaccine and placebo showed statistically identical infection rates over three years of surveillance [[7]](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/experimental-hiv-vaccine-regimen-ineffective-preventing-hiv). The vaccine was not unsafe; it was simply ineffective. This distinction between safety and efficacy defined the trial's final chapter and offers specific lessons for builders pursuing high-stakes vaccine development in resource-constrained epidemics.

The trial's stopping point was neither technical failure nor logistical collapse. It was scientific honesty enforced by protocol. For researchers in Cape Town and across southern Africa, the Uhambo experience demonstrates what rigorous failure looks like when institutional infrastructure is sufficient to handle it, and what knowledge survives when ambition meets an honest no.

## The Ambition

HVTN 702 was designed as an act of epidemiological courage. The 1994 RV144 trial in Thailand had achieved 31.2 percent protection against HIV using a canary pox vector prime followed by protein boost with alum adjuvant - modest protection, but the first positive efficacy signal in a human HIV vaccine trial [[10]](https://www.nejm.org/do/10.1056/NEJMdo005972/full/). Thirteen years later, the global HIV vaccine enterprise faced a choice: assume this design would fail in Africa, or test it in the population with the highest burden of HIV risk.

Southern Africa carried 28 percent of global HIV deaths in 2016 despite holding 6 percent of the world's population. The dominant circulating virus subtype was clade C, not the clade B/E mix prevalent in Thailand. The background infection incidence among women in South Africa's poorest communities reached 4.2 percent annually, roughly 14 times higher than in the RV144 trial [[13]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2031499). HVTN 702 asked whether an adapted vaccine - ALVAC-HIV (vCP2438) delivered as prime at months 0, 1, 3, and 6, followed by bivalent subtype C gp120 protein boosts at months 3, 6, 12, and 18, all formulated with MF59 adjuvant instead of alum - could protect people in this exponentially higher-risk setting [[12]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02968849).

The trial enrolled participants starting October 2016 across a distributed network anchored in Cape Town. The Desmond Tutu HIV Centre at the University of Cape Town coordinated the study through Linda-Gail Bekker and the University of Cape Town Clinical Trials Unit. Two sites operated in Cape Town itself: Emavundleni, located in the New Crossroads township south of the city center, and a Khayelitsha site in the township east of the city, both managed by trained research nurses and physicians linked to university infrastructure. The trial's total cost approached \$104 million, funded by NIAID, the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, and the South African Medical Research Council [[6]](https://apnews.com/press-release/pr-newswire/business-health-infectious-diseases-c6b8688f99814eae9dad79d031477aff).

## What Happened

Enrollment proceeded from October 2016 through 2019, reaching 5,407 participants (2,706 vaccinated, 2,701 placebo). Vaccination campaigns ran across 14 South African sites spanning Cape Town, Durban, Johannesburg, Soweto, Tembisa, and smaller cities. The protocol specified that the Data and Safety Monitoring Board would conduct an interim efficacy analysis once 110 infection events had accumulated in either arm.

By January 23, 2020, that threshold had been crossed. The DSMB convened to review the unblinded data against pre-specified futility boundaries - statistical thresholds defining when continued vaccination in the trial would be ethically unjustifiable if efficacy appeared impossible to achieve. The analysis found 138 HIV infections in the vaccinated group versus 133 in the placebo arm, yielding a hazard ratio of 1.02 (95 percent confidence interval 0.81 to 1.3). Zero efficacy. On February 3, 2020, NIAID announced that vaccinations would stop immediately [[6]](https://apnews.com/press-release/pr-newswire/business-health-infectious-diseases-c6b8688f99814eae9dad79d031477aff).

Critically, the DSMB expressed no safety concerns. Vaccine reactogenicity was mild to moderate and comparable to placebo: local reactions (injection site pain, induration, erythema) at 48–58 percent, systemic symptoms (myalgia, headache, fatigue) at 40–70 percent, predominantly grade 1 or 2 (mild-moderate) [[4]](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/49/NCT02968849/Prot_ICF_000.pdf). No vaccine-related serious adverse events occurred. The decision to halt vaccination was not a safety evacuation but a statement that continuing to inject an ineffective vaccine into healthy people violated research ethics.

The distinction proved crucial. Vaccinations stopped. Follow-up continued. All 5,407 participants were unblinded to their treatment assignment and continued in the study for 12 months of post-vaccination safety monitoring and clinical follow-up [[2]](https://www.hvtn.org/content/dam/hvtn/news/hvtn-news-releases/2020/Fact%20Sheet%20-%20Understanding%20the%20702%20Outcome.pdf). The trial achieved administrative completion on November 16, 2021, after this monitoring window closed [[1]](https://desmondtutuhealthfoundation.org.za/admin-dthf/news/hvtn702-hiv-vaccine-trial-discontinued/).

## Why It Ended

The immunological findings amplified the enigma. Predecessor work in HVTN 100 (a phase 1/2 trial in South Africa using the same vaccine) had met all "go/no-go" criteria for advancement. Binding antibody titers were 3.6 to 8.8 times higher than those induced by RV144. V1V2-specific antibody response rates reached 83.6 percent, well above the 63 percent threshold that predictions suggested would predict efficacy. CD4-positive T-cell responses were more frequent than in RV144 (58–63 percent versus 40 percent) [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/PMC7888373).

Yet in HVTN 702, this immunological success did not translate to protection. The vaccine reduced breakthrough infection risk by zero percent. Mechanistic investigations documented the divergence. The V1V2 IgG response rate in HVTN 702 was 67 percent - significantly lower than the 100 percent observed in RV144, despite overall binding antibody titers being higher [[13]](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2031499). The MF59 adjuvant, chosen to amplify immune magnitude, may have altered the balance between mucosal and systemic immunity compared to the alum used in RV144. Preclinical work in non-human primates suggested alum conferred protection in challenge models while MF59 did not [[2]](https://www.hvtn.org/content/dam/hvtn/news/hvtn-news-releases/2020/Fact%20Sheet%20-%20Understanding%20the%20702%20Outcome.pdf).

A second mechanism emerged from viral genetics. The vaccine insert carried the gp120 from a clade C strain, but circulating HIV in South Africa showed extraordinary strain diversity. The critical V1V2 epitope binding sites diverged across the epidemic. Particularly, the position 169 amino acid residue - strongly implicated in RV144 protection - appeared at low frequency in South African isolates [[2]](https://www.hvtn.org/content/dam/hvtn/news/hvtn-news-releases/2020/Fact%20Sheet%20-%20Understanding%20the%20702%20Outcome.pdf).

A third factor was epidemiological. The baseline HIV incidence in HVTN 702 participants (4.2 percent among women) exceeded the vaccine's apparent capacity to suppress. RV144's protection was already modest; pushing it into a population with 14-fold higher acquisition risk may have asked too much of the immunological signal.

## What Survived

The trial's infrastructure remained standing. All 14 sites retained their trained personnel, community relationships, and data management systems. The Emavundleni and Khayelitsha sites in Cape Town stayed operational and engaged. The University of Cape Town Clinical Trials Unit, which had coordinated 6 affiliated South African sites, consolidated its experience and continued recruitment for successor trials.

The biospecimen archive endured. Blood samples, plasma, and other biological materials from the 5,407 participants remained stored and accessible for secondary immunological analysis. This archive enabled post-hoc studies published in 2022 (an analysis in the Journal of Infectious Diseases examining RV144 antibody and T-cell correlates of infection risk in the HVTN 702 cohort) and 2026 (a cross-protocol immunogenicity comparison in Frontiers Immunology examining how different adjuvants - AS01B, MF59, and alum - shaped immune responses across HVTN trials 100, 107, 120, and 702) [[8]](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/immunology/articles/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1768201/full) [[11]](https://academic.oup.com/jid/article-abstract/226/2/246/6618597).

Most valuably, the institution that had managed it survived. Linda-Gail Bekker continued as deputy director of the Desmond Tutu HIV Centre and progressed to leadership roles at the International AIDS Society, the Access to Medicine Foundation, and Harvard University's Center for AIDS Research. The Centre retained its role as a primary site for HVTN 705 (Imbokodo, a mosaic vaccine trial in women) and HVTN 706 (Mosaico, a mosaic vaccine trial in men and transgender persons), demonstrating that the loss of a single trial did not discredit the institution or the investigator team.

## What the Next Attempt Can Learn

HVTN 702 offers six specific lessons for builders pursuing vaccine development in epidemics where both HIV burden and viral diversity are high.

First, immunogenicity is not a valid proxy for efficacy. Meeting thresholds on binding antibody titers, CD4 responses, and cellular markers in early-phase work does not ensure field efficacy. The vaccine generated robust, durable immune responses. Those responses failed to protect. Future trials must acknowledge this uncertainty: meeting immunogenicity gates is a necessary but not sufficient step toward clinical benefit [[10]](https://www.nejm.org/do/10.1056/NEJMdo005972/full/).

Second, viral subtype adaptation requires population-specific validation rather than template transfer. RV144 success in clade B/E communities does not automatically transfer to clade C communities. Circulating strain diversity, epitope frequency, and host genetic factors introduce population-specific challenges that cannot be resolved by analogy [[2]](https://www.hvtn.org/content/dam/hvtn/news/hvtn-news-releases/2020/Fact%20Sheet%20-%20Understanding%20the%20702%20Outcome.pdf).

Third, adjuvant selection fundamentally shapes immune quality, not merely magnitude. MF59 amplified binding antibody titers relative to RV144's alum, yet the protective potential appeared diminished. The balance between mucosal IgA and systemic IgG, the polarization toward Th1 versus Th2 responses, and the durability of memory formation all depend on adjuvant chemistry in ways current models do not fully predict.

Fourth, pre-specified stopping rules enable honest science. HVTN 702's futility criteria were set before data unblinding. When those criteria were met, the trial stopped. No reanalysis, no post-hoc re-specification, no salvage attempt. The trial's credibility rests on this protocol fidelity, and that credibility proved essential to maintaining participant and community trust for successor recruitment [[5]](https://avac.org/blog/hvtn-702-stopped-early-for-non-efficacy/).

Fifth, distributed multi-site trials build sustainable regional capacity. HVTN 702 operated across 14 sites in economically diverse settings. When the trial ended, those sites did not vanish. The infrastructure persisted. Community advisory relationships, trained nurses, data systems, and investigator networks survived the vaccine failure and enabled subsequent research. This durability is itself a form of success [[1]](https://desmondtutuhealthfoundation.org.za/admin-dthf/news/hvtn702-hiv-vaccine-trial-discontinued/).

Sixth, transparent communication about failure preserves institutional credibility. When HVTN 702 leaders announced the halt, they did not minimize the result or speculate hopefully about salvage paths. They reported the data plainly and committed to rigorous post-trial analysis. This honesty - unusual in an era where vaccine failures often trigger defensiveness - appears to have sustained the communities' and regulators' willingness to continue engagement [[3]](https://www.treatmentactiongroup.org/statement/treatment-action-group-statement-on-the-hvtn-702-hiv-vaccine-trial-results/).

The Uhambo trial was named for the Zulu word meaning "the journey." The journey ended at an unambiguous station: a vaccine that did not work, tested fairly, reported fully, and acknowledged as a genuine scientific loss rather than a setback to be spun. For future vaccine developers, particularly those working in resource-limited settings with high-burden epidemics, that clarity - achieved through rigor, distributed capacity, and institutional integrity - is the most durable artifact.
