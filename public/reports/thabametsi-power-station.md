# Thabametsi Power Station

## What Was Proposed

Thabametsi Coal-Fired Power Station was a 630-megawatt independent power producer project proposed to be developed near Lephalale in the Limpopo province of South Africa [[6]](https://www.power-technology.com/projects/thabametsi-coal-power-plant-limpopo-province/). The plant was designed as a dry-cooled coal-fired facility, meaning it would require less water than conventional wet-cooled plants in a water-stressed region. The project was located approximately 22 kilometres west of the town of Lephalale, in the Waterberg District, adjacent to Exxaro's existing Grootegeluk coal mine [[10]](https://exxaro-reports.co.za/reports/integrated-reports2020/cmrr/thabametsi-project.php).

## Timeline and Regulatory Path

The project followed a disciplined regulatory pathway that appeared successful for several years:

- **March 2015**: Environmental Impact Assessment approval issued by the Department of Environmental Affairs
- **October 2016**: Thabametsi project company selected as preferred bidder in the South African Government's Coal IPP Procurement Programme, with a bid of 80 cents per kilowatt-hour
- **2017–2019**: Project moved through detailed engineering and financing phases; major development banks and institutional investors conducted due diligence
- **November 30, 2020**: Project formally cancelled following withdrawal of major financial partners and investor base
- **December 2020**: High Court of South Africa set aside the environmental authorization [[13]](https://cer.org.za/news/final-nail-in-the-coffin-for-proposed-khanyisa-coal-power-station)

## Causes of Cancellation

The collapse of Thabametsi was not a single failure but a convergence of regulatory, financial, and policy obstacles:

**Climate Change Litigation.** The most significant regulatory obstacle was a landmark court judgment that established climate change impacts as a mandatory consideration in environmental authorization for coal projects. A 2017 court ruling confirmed that climate change impacts must be part of the environmental impact assessment process [[16]](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/courts/court-ruling-a-blow-to-khanyisa-coal-project/). This shifted the evidentiary burden on coal projects from routine environmental impact to explicit carbon and climate-change accounting—a novel legal standard that made traditional environmental authorizations legally vulnerable.

Environmental rights organizations, particularly the Centre for Environmental Rights, leveraged this precedent to challenge Thabametsi's authorization. In a Pretoria High Court order dated May 27, 2021, the environmental approval for the planned coal power plant was declared invalid [[13]](https://cer.org.za/news/final-nail-in-the-coffin-for-proposed-khanyisa-coal-power-station) [[14]](https://www.greenbuildingafrica.co.za/south-africa-eia-expires-for-acwa-power-new-build-600mw-khanyisa-coal-fired-plant/). The court found that the original environmental assessment had not adequately considered climate change impacts in violation of South Africa's constitutional and statutory obligations. This judicial intervention was described as "the final nail in the coffin for the proposed coal plant" [[15]](https://allafrica.com/stories/202106030792.html).

**Bank Financing Withdrawal.** Between 2018 and 2019, the major South African development banks that had been expected to finance the project withdrew support. Nedbank, Standard Bank, FirstRand, and Absa all retreated from coal infrastructure financing, reflecting both environmental pressure and shifting capital allocation away from fossil fuels. By 2019, the project faced a financing gap that proved impossible to close with remaining sources.

**Institutional Investor Exits.** In November 2020, two critical institutional investors—the Public Investment Corporation (PIC) and the Industrial Development Corporation (IDC)—withdrew from the project [[8]](https://www.greengrants.org/2020/12/03/south-africa-coal-victory/). This withdrawal occurred days before the formal cancellation and signalled that even state-aligned institutions no longer viewed coal IPPs as viable investments.

**International Partner Policy Shifts.** The project's commercial partners retreated on global policy grounds:
- **KEPCO (Korea Electric Power Corporation)** announced in October 2020 that it would no longer pursue overseas coal projects, directly triggering the Thabametsi withdrawal
- **Marubeni Corporation** of Japan announced in November 2020 a corporate-wide policy against new coal power projects, leading to its exit from Thabametsi
- These policy shifts reflected broader global capital reorientation away from coal infrastructure [[8]](https://www.greengrants.org/2020/12/03/south-africa-coal-victory/)

## What Survived

The Thabametsi power project did not generate electricity, but its legacy endured in other forms:

- **Regulatory Precedent**: The Pretoria High Court decision established a binding legal standard that all future coal projects in South Africa must explicitly account for climate change impacts in environmental authorizations. This precedent has deterred subsequent coal infrastructure proposals and shaped regulatory practice.
- **Technical Documentation**: Engineering feasibility studies, site surveys, and technical specifications remain in archive form, accessible to researchers and future analysts.
- **Financial Analysis**: The project's economic models and risk assessments have informed South African policy discussions about coal phase-out trajectories and energy transition costs.
