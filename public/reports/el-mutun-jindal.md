# The El Mutun steel contract

El Mutum, a mountain in Motacucito, Santa Cruz Department, contains one of the world's largest iron ore reserves—approximately 40 billion tonnes of untapped deposits located 50 kilometers from Puerto Suárez. [[10]](https://www.livemint.com/Companies/V9Uth6i0WZAJ1PkR93x83M/Jindal-Steel-wins-225-million-verdict-on-Bolivia-iron-proj.html) [[12]](https://www.firstpost.com/business/corporate-business/jindal-steel-wins-22-5-million-arbitration-against-bolivian-company-1985563.html) [[17]](https://www.bnamericas.com/en/news/bolivia-plans-us400mn-mutun-steel-plant) In 2007, Jindal Steel and Power, India's largest integrated steel manufacturer, signed a development contract valued at \$2.1 billion to exploit this resource and construct a major steel manufacturing complex. [[11]](https://www.firstpost.com/business/jindal-steel-threatens-to-pull-out-of-bolivias-largest-mining-project-339102.html) [[13]](https://www.firstpost.com/business/jindal-steel-cancels-2-bn-mining-project-in-bolivia-over-supply-woes-380666.html) The planned facility was to include a 1.7-million-tonne-per-year steel plant, sponge-iron factory, pellet unit, and power generation complex. [[17]](https://www.bnamericas.com/en/news/bolivia-plans-us400mn-mutun-steel-plant) By design, the complex was to be served by planned rail infrastructure connecting southward to Puerto Busch—a comprehensive industrial ecosystem anchoring Bolivia's regional development strategy.

Bolivia's contractual obligation was straightforward: supply 10 million cubic meters per day of natural gas to the facility and provide adequate land and access infrastructure. [[11]](https://www.firstpost.com/business/jindal-steel-threatens-to-pull-out-of-bolivias-largest-mining-project-339102.html) By 2010, it became apparent that Bolivia could not fulfill these commitments. Jindal issued notices of non-compliance. Bolivia responded by unilaterally encashing Jindal's performance bonds—\$18 million in 2010 and a second \$18 million in 2012—claiming Jindal had failed to meet development timelines. [[10]](https://www.livemint.com/Companies/V9Uth6i0WZAJ1PkR93x83M/Jindal-Steel-wins-225-million-verdict-on-Bolivia-iron-proj.html) [[14]](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/decision/en-jindal-steel-bolivia-s-a-v-empresa-siderurgica-del-mutun-final-award-wednesday-6th-august-2014) In December 2014, Jindal formally terminated the contract, citing Bolivia's "non-investor friendly attitude" and systematic failure to deliver contracted natural gas supplies and land. [[13]](https://www.firstpost.com/business/jindal-steel-cancels-2-bn-mining-project-in-bolivia-over-supply-woes-380666.html)

The dispute moved to the International Chamber of Commerce in Paris. On August 6, 2014, an ICC tribunal issued a final award in Jindal's favor, ruling that Empresa Siderúrgica del Mutún (ESM), Bolivia's state-owned mining company, had acted unlawfully in encashing the performance guarantees, and ordered Bolivia to repay Jindal \$22.5 million. [[14]](https://jusmundi.com/en/document/decision/en-jindal-steel-bolivia-s-a-v-empresa-siderurgica-del-mutun-final-award-wednesday-6th-august-2014) [[18]](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/companies/jindal-steel-and-power-wins-22-5-mn-arbitration-against-bolivian-firm/) Bolivia announced an appeal of the ruling in late 2014, but sources do not document the appeal's outcome or Bolivia's current legal status as of 2026. [[15]](https://www.bnamericas.com/en/news/bolivia-to-appeal-icc-ruling-on-jindal-iron-ore-mine1) [[16]](https://www.bnamericas.com/en/news/bolivian-court-to-hear-mutuns-appeal-against-icc-ruling)

What survived was the ore deposit itself, which remains in Bolivian state ownership, unexploited. In October 2014, Bolivia announced an alternative strategy: a \$400-million state-owned mini-mill steel facility at El Mutum, designed to process ore domestically without foreign direct investment. [[17]](https://www.bnamericas.com/en/news/bolivia-plans-us400mn-mutun-steel-plant) [[34]](https://www.steelorbis.com/steel-news/latest-news/bolivian-president-announces-national-steel-plan-848124.htm) The implementation status of this alternative project through 2026 is not documented in the sources reviewed. Notably, a Chinese-financed Mutun Steel Complex eventually opened at the El Mutum site in February 2025—a success case rather than a failure, and therefore outside the scope of this investigation of abandoned projects. [[1]](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202503/10/WS67ce403fa310c240449d9c8c.html) [[37]](https://www.mining-technology.com/news/bolivias-china-funded-steel-plant-to-begin-production/) [[38]](https://www.alttower.com/chinese-funding-supports-mut-n-steel-complex-launch-in-bolivia_n150) [[39]](https://www.riotimesonline.com/bolivias-546-million-steel-plant-begins-operations-after-50-year-wait/) [[40]](https://www.mysteel.net/news/5077188-bolivias-china-funded-steel-plant-set-to-begin-operations-after-50-years-of-delays) [[41]](https://business.inquirer.net/508283/bolivia-inaugurates-steel-plant-built-with-chinese-loan) [[42]](https://wallstreetobserver.com/iron-ore-set-for/china-funded-steel-plant-opens-in-bolivia/)

The Jindal episode reveals a structural mismatch between Bolivia's resource wealth and its infrastructure capacity. Bolivia possessed ore; India possessed capital and technical expertise. Yet contractual frameworks that required Bolivia to provide natural gas infrastructure—infrastructure Bolivia did not possess—created an impossible constraint. Jindal withdrew; the resources remained in the ground; and the state-directed alternative (if implemented) took over a decade to materialize.
