# Evergrande

## China Evergrande Group: Asia's Largest Property Developer Implodes

**Founded:** 1996
**Founder:** Xu Jiayin (a former steel worker)
**Headquarters:** Shenzhen
**Peak scale (pre-2021):** Over 1,300 projects across 280+ cities; 1.5 million homebuyers with pre-sale deposits; Xu Jiayin ranked as Asia's richest individual (March 2020) [[13]](https://www.firstpost.com/explainers/chinas-evergrande-files-for-bankruptcy-in-us-rise-and-fall-of-the-real-estate-behemoth-13012642.html)
**Default:** December 2021
**Chapter 15 bankruptcy filing:** August 17–18, 2023 (Manhattan bankruptcy court)
**Founder detention:** September 29, 2023

#### What Was Built and Why It Mattered

Evergrande epitomized China's property-development model: a rapid-growth conglomerate funded by pre-sales to homebuyers, with escalating leverage and dependence on continual sales momentum to service debt. At its peak, Evergrande was the world's most heavily indebted property developer and the poster child for China's real estate expansion [[16]](https://www.reuters.com/legal/china-evergrande-files-chapter-15-bankruptcy-us-court-filing-2023-08-17/). The company's rise was emblematic of Shenzhen's transformation from special economic zone into global financial center—Evergrande's headquarters in Shenzhen was not merely administrative but symbolic of the city's appetite for scale, leverage, and growth.

#### Financial Scale and Collapse Mechanics

In December 2021, when Evergrande first defaulted, the company carried **\$305 billion in total debt**, the largest corporate debt burden globally [[8]](https://www.dw.com/en/evergrande-why-the-chinese-property-giant-is-close-to-collapse/a-59175953). Of this, **1.4 million homebuyers held \$207 billion in pre-sale liabilities**—deposits paid for homes that remained unbuilt [[8]](https://www.dw.com/en/evergrande-why-the-chinese-property-giant-is-close-to-collapse/a-59175953). The company controlled 1,300+ projects across 280+ cities, creating systematic interconnection with China's entire supply chain (steel suppliers, concrete manufacturers, labor contractors, local government finances through land sales revenue).

#### Causation: Beijing's "Three Red Lines" and Market Collapse

In August 2020, China's central government imposed the "three red lines" debt controls, imposing caps on property developers' leverage ratios, net debt-to-equity, and cash-to-debt ratios [[7]](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2023/aug/18/chinas-struggling-property-giant-evergrande-files-for-bankruptcy-protection-in-us) [[14]](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2023/08/18/evergrande-bankruptcy-chapter-15-china/). Evergrande, built on aggressive leverage, immediately became unable to refinance at scale. Simultaneously, negative media coverage about unfinished projects and investor losses triggered a withdrawal of buyer confidence. Pre-sales, which had funded Evergrande's entire operating model, collapsed. The company could not simultaneously complete existing projects and service debt [[15]](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/08/17/business/china-evergrande-bankruptcy.html).

#### What Ended and What Survived

**What ended:**
- Evergrande's viability as an independent real estate developer
- Xu Jiayin's role as CEO (detained by authorities September 29, 2023, on allegations of financial crimes)
- The pre-sale business model that had financed China's property boom for two decades

**Status of restructuring (as of 2023-2026):**
The company filed for Chapter 15 protection in the U.S. bankruptcy court to seek recognition of restructuring efforts in multiple jurisdictions [[11]](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/chinas-evergrande-files-bankruptcy-new-york-court) [[12]](https://www.cnn.com/2023/08/17/business/evergrande-files-for-bankruptcy/index.html). However, as of September 2023, Evergrande's chairman remained detained, and restructuring remained stalled: "The plan [to restructure and get back on its feet] now looks to be in jeopardy" [[17]](https://www.cnn.com/2023/09/29/economy/china-evergrande-chairman-detention-intl-hnk).

**What survived:**
- **Evergrande Property Services Group** — offered as debt-for-equity instrument to creditors, but its viability post-separation is uncertain
- **Evergrande New Energy Vehicle Group** — positioned as alternate revenue stream, but subject to same fundamental constraints (creditor disputes, capital constraints)
- The **800+ unfinished projects** remained partially constructed, with completion status unclear and homebuyer recovery uncertain

#### Geographic Connection and Cascading Impacts

Evergrande's Shenzhen headquarters housed the decision-making apparatus for the company's national operations. The city of Shenzhen itself held substantial claims against Evergrande through land conveyances and unpaid fees. The collapse affected Shenzhen's financial markets (Evergrande was listed on Hong Kong Stock Exchange), employment (construction and supply chain jobs), and investor confidence [[9]](https://www.bbc.com/news/business-58540939).

## Shimao Shenzhen-Hong Kong International Centre: Mega-Project Stalled and Transferred

**Project name:** Shimao Shenzhen-Hong Kong International Centre (sometimes referenced as Shimao Center)
**Location:** Longgang District, Shenzhen (geographically distinct from Qianhai Cooperation Zone in Nanshan)
**Land acquisition:** 2017 for 23.9 billion yuan
**Project type:** 700-meter mixed-use commercial-residential skyscraper; intended to anchor new commercial hub

#### Financial Trajectory and Default

Shimao Property Holdings (parent company) defaulted on debt obligations beginning in January 2022, starting with a trust loan default, followed by a **\$1.02 billion bond principal and interest payment default in July 2022** [[30]](https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3162513/shimao-groups-shanghai-unit-defaults-us101m-project-loan) [[31]](https://www.reuters.com/business/chinese-property-developer-shimao-misses-repayment-1-bln-bond-2022-07-03/). The offshore bond default affected **\$1.7 billion in bonds held by foreign investors and 8.9 billion yuan (\$1.4 billion) in bonds held by Chinese investors**, plus "sizable offshore bank loans" [[32]](https://www.share-talk.com/chinas-real-estate-crisis-deepens-as-big-shanghai-developer-defaults/).

The defaults cascaded across multiple instruments: Moody's downgraded Shimao in February 2022 [[28]](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/24/moodys-downgrades-china-property-developer-shimao-over-debt-troubles.html); on January 11, 2022, the developer announced it was putting all projects up for sale [[29]](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2022/jan/11/chinese-developer-shimao-plans-fire-sale-after-downgrade-and-missed-payment) [[33]](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/modern-land-shares-dive-after-some-bondholders-demand-early-repayment-2022-01-10/).

#### Project Abandonment and State Intervention

The Shenzhen-Hong Kong International Centre project entered a construction hiatus of approximately four years (2022–2026). On **May 7, 2026**, China Resources Land Co., Ltd. (a major state-owned enterprise) acquired the stalled mega-project for **7.05 billion yuan (approximately \$1.03 billion USD)** [[20]](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-05-07/china-resources-buys-stalled-shenzhen-mega-project-for-1-billion-102441747.html).

This transaction represented a **65% reduction in land value** from the 2017 acquisition price (23.9 billion yuan → 7.05 billion yuan). However, the acquisition also included a fundamental reconfiguration of project objectives: while the original 2017 concept emphasized commercial office space and financial services, the 2026 China Resources Land acquisition explicitly shifted emphasis toward **residential mixed-use development**, reflecting changing market demand and the government's push to contain property speculation [[20]](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-05-07/china-resources-buys-stalled-shenzhen-mega-project-for-1-billion-102441747.html).

#### Geographic Connection and Systemic Implications

The project's location in Longgang District (not Qianhai in Nanshan) reflects Shimao's attempt to develop a secondary commercial hub outside the government-designated cooperation zone. The project's failure illustrates the vulnerability of private-sector mega-projects to debt cycles independent of zone designation. Unlike Qianhai (a policy instrument), the Shimao Center was pure commercial real estate, and without continuous sales revenue, the project became unsustainable.

#### What Survived

The project itself survives under new ownership with fundamentally altered objectives. What did **not** survive:
- Shimao Property Holdings' independence (company restructuring ongoing)
- The 2017 commercial-hub vision (reoriented to residential)
- Original creditor claims (transferred to state ownership)
