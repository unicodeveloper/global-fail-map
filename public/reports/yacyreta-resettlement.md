# The Yacyreta reservoir

## The Core Infrastructure Failure

The Yacyretá Dam was hydraulically complete and capable of generating electricity by 1989. However, its design capacity required raising the reservoir level to 83 meters above sea level. This elevation would have flooded approximately 1,500 square kilometers of inhabited land and required relocating between 40,000 and 70,000 people, depending on demographic projections and urban growth assumptions. [[5]](https://www.irn.org/yacyreta/) [[7]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacyret%C3%A1_Dam) [[9]](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/902501468742883404/pdf/24640eng.pdf)

The World Bank—the primary lender and governance voice—imposed a critical condition in 1992: the reservoir level would be held at 76 meters (approximately 60% of design capacity) until the Resettlement and Rehabilitation Action Plan was substantially completed and environmental mitigation measures were in place. [[6]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/chronic-problems-at-yacyret%C3%A1-dam-3839/) [[9]](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/902501468742883404/pdf/24640eng.pdf) This condition was based on the World Bank Inspection Panel's assessment (1991–1994) that EBY's institutional capacity to execute resettlement was grossly inadequate.

For seventeen years—from 1994 to February 25, 2011—the reservoir remained frozen at 76 meters. [[6]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/chronic-problems-at-yacyret%C3%A1-dam-3839/) During this period, the dam operated at reduced capacity, generating approximately 20,000 gigawatt-hours annually instead of its design output of 20,000 GWh at full capacity. [[3]](https://eros.usgs.gov/earthshots/itaipu-dam) [[4]](https://www.rivernet.org/southamerica/parana_basin/yacyreta.htm) More significantly, 40,000 to 70,000 people lived in institutional limbo, occupying incomplete housing, facing contaminated water supplies, and uncertain about whether relocation promises would materialize. [[5]](https://www.irn.org/yacyreta/) [[9]](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/902501468742883404/pdf/24640eng.pdf)

## Why Resettlement Failed

The World Bank Inspection Panel documented systematic failures in EBY's resettlement implementation:

1. **Inadequate housing completion**: By 1999, only 4,266 families had been formally resettled into completed housing, compared to the 40,000–70,000 requiring relocation. [[9]](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/902501468742883404/pdf/24640eng.pdf) The gap persisted for a decade.

2. **Environmental mitigation deficiency**: Promised environmental reserves (19,256–187,000 hectares depending on interpretation) were not adequately demarcated, managed, or legally protected. [[9]](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/902501468742883404/pdf/24640eng.pdf)

3. **Water and sanitation inadequacy**: Potable water systems serving relocated communities were incomplete or contaminated with brick-making byproducts and agricultural runoff from upland areas. [[33]](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/5792125.pdf) [[9]](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/902501468742883404/pdf/24640eng.pdf)

4. **Institutional capacity**: EBY operated as a binational entity with governance disputes between Paraguay and Argentina, creating delays in decision-making and fund allocation.

## Resolution and Verification

The reservoir was raised to design level (83 meters) on February 25, 2011, after 17 years of delay. [[6]](https://riverresourcehub.org/resources/chronic-problems-at-yacyret%C3%A1-dam-3839/) At that point, EBY certified that resettlement had been substantially completed—though housing remained geographically isolated and service-deficient (see Case 3). The delay itself—rather than the eventual completion—constitutes the infrastructure failure. It represents a systemic inability of a major regional institution to implement promised mitigation within contractual timeframes.
