# The Interaction Design Institute Ivrea

## Founding and Institutional Structure

On June 16, 2000, Telecom Italia and Olivetti founded the Interaction Design Institute Ivrea (IDII), a graduate design program dedicated to interaction design—the discipline of designing how humans interact with digital and electronic systems [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea). The institute received its first students in 2001 and operated for approximately five academic years before closure.

**Geographic location:** IDII occupied a former Olivetti facility known as the "Study and Research Centre" (Casa Blu) in Ivrea, with additional residential housing for students at Talponia, a residential complex also in Ivrea [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea). The coordinates of the institute match Ivrea's location: approximately 45°27′30″ North, 7°52′30″ East [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea).

**Institutional leadership and vision:** The institute was founded with prominent design educator Gillian Crampton Smith serving as founding director [[21]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_design). It represented an attempt to revive Ivrea as a center of innovation and creative thinking, building on Olivetti's historical legacy as a design-forward company.

**Financial model:** IDII operated on a five-year endowment funded by Telecom Italia [[16]](https://schoolinformationsolution.blogspot.com/2018/05/interaction-design-institute-ivrea.html) [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea). This time-limited funding structure proved fatal to the institution's long-term viability.

## Significance: Incubation of Arduino and Wiring

Despite its institutional brief existence, IDII became the birthplace of two globally significant open-source technology platforms:

1. **Wiring (2003):** An electronics prototyping environment developed by Hernando Barragán in 2003 as a student project at IDII [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea). Wiring provided a simplified programming language and environment for controlling electronic circuits and sensors, designed to be accessible to designers and artists without formal electrical engineering training [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea).

2. **Arduino (2005):** Building directly on Wiring's foundation, a team of five instructors and students—Massimo Banzi, David Cuartielles, Tom Igoe, Gianluca Martino, and David Mellis—created Arduino in 2005 at IDII [[17]](https://lonelybinary.com/en-us/blogs/tutorial/history-of-arduino). Arduino combined Wiring's software environment with a standardized open-source circuit board design, creating an accessible platform for interactive electronics prototyping that democratized the creation of interactive physical systems [[17]](https://lonelybinary.com/en-us/blogs/tutorial/history-of-arduino) [[20]](https://blog.experientia.com/interaction-ivrea-arduino-and-intels-galileo/).

**Global impact:** Arduino went on to become one of the most widely used electronics prototyping platforms in the world, used in educational settings, hobbyist projects, and professional product development across countless disciplines. The platform is freely available under open-source licensing, and its design has been replicated, adapted, and extended globally [[19]](https://tech.eu/2025/10/13/autumn-and-arduino-bring-out-the-best-of-italy/) [[22]](https://arduinohistory.github.io/).

## Closure and Dissolution (2005–2006/2007)

**When it closed:** The institute's final academic year was 2005–2006 [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea). The institution officially ceased independent operation and merged into Domus Academy in Milan in 2007 [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea).

**Why it closed:** The five-year endowment from Telecom Italia was not renewed [[16]](https://schoolinformationsolution.blogspot.com/2018/05/interaction-design-institute-ivrea.html) [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea). Without continued funding, the institute could not sustain faculty, student programs, or facility operations. The endowment limitation reflected both the precarious finances of Telecom Italia (itself struggling during the early 2000s) and the reality that sustaining an experimental design school in a declining industrial town proved economically unviable [[16]](https://schoolinformationsolution.blogspot.com/2018/05/interaction-design-institute-ivrea.html) [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea).

**Status designation:** IDII is classified as dissolved. It ceased to exist as an independent institution, though the Domus Academy merger preserved some organizational continuity and elements of the pedagogical mission.

## What Survived: Arduino's Unexpected Global Success

The crucial distinction in this case is between the **institution** (dissolved) and the **technology** (which thrived). Arduino emerged from IDII in 2005, just as the institute was entering its final year of operation. Rather than requiring the institute's physical infrastructure or institutional support to succeed, Arduino was specifically designed as an open-source platform that could exist independently of any single institution.

**Post-closure trajectory:** After IDII dissolved in 2006–2007, Arduino continued to develop through distributed global collaboration. The platform became increasingly central to maker culture, educational technology, robotics, and interactive design worldwide [[19]](https://tech.eu/2025/10/13/autumn-and-arduino-bring-out-the-best-of-italy/) [[22]](https://arduinohistory.github.io/). Far from being a failed project that dissolved with the institute, Arduino became one of the most significant technology exports from Italy in the twenty-first century [[19]](https://tech.eu/2025/10/13/autumn-and-arduino-bring-out-the-best-of-italy/).

**Institutional legacy:** In 2007, IDII staff and leadership founded CIID (Copenhagen Institute of Interaction Design) in Copenhagen, preserving the pedagogical approach and intellectual lineage of the Ivrea institute in a new geographic location [[18]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interaction_Design_Institute_Ivrea).

**The historiography note:** Arduino's history carries a persistent historiographic problem: Wiring's precursor role and Hernando Barragán's foundational contribution have been sometimes obscured or underemphasized in popular accounts of Arduino's origins [[22]](https://arduinohistory.github.io/). The archived documentation of Arduino's development at IDII provides crucial evidence for crediting both Wiring and Arduino properly to their respective creators [[22]](https://arduinohistory.github.io/).
