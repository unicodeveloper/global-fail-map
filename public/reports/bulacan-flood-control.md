# The Bulacan flood control projects

## Systemic Failure and Corruption

In 2024–2025, investigations revealed a systematic pattern of "ghost projects"—infrastructure officially marked as completed but either never constructed, abandoned mid-work, or constructed in locations where they serve no flood mitigation purpose. The pattern was not isolated to one project or contractor but appeared endemic across the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and local government units.

## The Sipat Riverbank Case: Specific Documentation

One documented example illustrates the pattern. In Sipat (Plaridel), Bulacan, a riverbank protection structure was officially marked as completed with a budget allocation of P96 million (\$1.7 million USD). Investigation revealed:

- Construction documentation showed a completion date in 2024.
- Physical inspection in August 2025 found construction *beginning* in August 2025.
- By September 16, 2025, the work remained unfinished. [[10]](https://cids.up.edu.ph/flood-control-fiasco-a-policy-reckoning-for-accountability-in-the-philippines/)

This case documents not merely delay but falsified completion status—a structure claimed as finished that had not yet begun.

## Systemic Geographic Mismatch

Bulacan province submitted 668 flood control projects to the national government. Investigation found that 411 of these 668 projects (61.5%) were located *outside* identified flood hazard zones, meaning they provided no mitigation benefit to actual flood-prone populations. [[10]](https://cids.up.edu.ph/flood-control-fiasco-a-policy-reckoning-for-accountability-in-the-philippines/) These projects existed on paper and in budget allocations but addressed no real problem.

## National Scope and Economic Loss

The pattern extended nationwide. In December 2021, the Commission on Audit flagged 17 government agencies for managing delayed, abandoned, and idle infrastructure projects totaling P1.44 trillion (\$26 billion USD), a figure representing approximately 5-10% of annual national government spending. [[1]](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2021/12/23/2149585/17-government-agencies-flagged-over-idle-projects) The audit described projects as idle without clear justification, some for years.

## Causes: Corruption, Lack of Feasibility Studies, Contractor Abandonment

President Marcos attributed the systemic failures to:

1. **Corruption**: Contractors and officials colluding to claim completion without building.
2. **Absence of feasibility studies**: Projects greenlit without engineering analysis of need or site suitability.
3. **Lack of accountability mechanisms**: Weak verification of completion before fund release.
4. **Contractor abandonment**: Companies hired to build projects, receiving advance payments, then failing to complete or maintain works without penalty.

## Government Response and Investigation

In September 2025, President Marcos formed an Independent Commission for Infrastructure (ICI) under Executive Order No. 94 to investigate and prosecute infrastructure fraud. [[12]](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_20/HB05701.pdf) Assets of officials and contractors involved were frozen on September 16, 2025. The Office of the Ombudsman committed to prioritize investigation and prosecution of infrastructure fraud cases. [[12]](https://docs.congress.hrep.online/legisdocs/basic_20/HB05701.pdf)

## Distinction: Systemic Corruption vs. Project-Specific Challenges

Unlike the POC modernization (bureaucratic delay) or subway (technical difficulty), the ghost projects represent deliberate fraud and negligence. The infrastructure was not attempted and failed—it was claimed as complete without being built. This is not a challenge to be engineered around but a governance and institutional failure requiring prosecution.
