## An announced product that never shipped

AirPower was Apple's promised answer to a small daily annoyance: charging an iPhone, Apple Watch and AirPods without arranging separate cables and chargers. Apple announced the mat in September 2017 and said it would be available in 2018. It did not reach customers. On March 29, 2019, Apple confirmed that the project had been cancelled. [[1]](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2017/09/the-future-is-here-iphone-x/) [[2]](https://techcrunch.com/2019/03/29/apple-cancels-airpower-product-citing-inability-to-meet-its-high-standards-for-hardware/)

Its documented status is therefore a product cancelled before launch, not a recall, company failure or discontinued product that had already been sold. The difference is useful: AirPower's public life consisted of an announcement, a missed shipping window and a cancellation.

## What Apple promised

Apple's iPhone X announcement described an optional charging mat for up to three devices, including iPhone 8 or iPhone X, Apple Watch Series 3 and a new wireless charging case for AirPods. The ambition was to let compatible devices share one charging surface. [[1]](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2017/09/the-future-is-here-iphone-x/)

That was more demanding than putting three independent chargers inside one enclosure. A placement-flexible surface needs to detect where a device is, select suitable transmitter coils and control the energy delivered. Apple's published patent work documents several approaches to those problems. It does not document a final, production-ready AirPower specification. [[3]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10491017) [[4]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10840007) [[5]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10897148B2/en)

## The patent trail

One concrete record is **US10897148B2**, *Wireless charging mats with multi-layer transmitter coil arrangements*. Apple filed the US application on September 8, 2017. The application was published as US20180090955A1 on March 29, 2018; the patent was granted on January 19, 2021. It describes overlapping transmitter coils in different layers, with arrangements intended to provide coverage across a charging surface. [[5]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10897148B2/en)

Other Apple patents address multiple power-receiving devices and shielding in multi-coil systems. They demonstrate that Apple disclosed substantial engineering work around this class of charger. A patent can describe several embodiments and can be granted after the associated commercial project ends. Neither fact proves which design, if any, would have appeared in the shipped product. [[3]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10491017) [[4]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10840007)

The distinction prevents a common mistake in an atlas of abandoned inventions: a cancelled product is not automatically an abandoned patent. AirPower's cancellation and the later grant of related charging patents can both be true.

## What ended, and what is actually known

Apple's hardware engineering chief Dan Riccio said the company had concluded that AirPower could not meet its standards. His statement was supplied directly to TechCrunch. Apple did not publish a detailed engineering failure analysis with temperatures, efficiency targets, costs or a definitive description of the unsuccessful design. [[2]](https://techcrunch.com/2019/03/29/apple-cancels-airpower-product-citing-inability-to-meet-its-high-standards-for-hardware/)

Contemporary reports discussed heat and interference, but the official statement does not establish one specific technical cause. The credible conclusion is narrower than the familiar retelling: Apple cancelled the product after failing to achieve its intended standard. It is not established that the underlying physics made every possible implementation impossible.

## What survived

The charging ideas remained visible in published intellectual property. Apple's cancellation statement also said it remained committed to wireless charging. That preserves a distinction between abandoning one product configuration and abandoning an entire technical field. [[2]](https://techcrunch.com/2019/03/29/apple-cancels-airpower-product-citing-inability-to-meet-its-high-standards-for-hardware/) [[5]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10897148B2/en)

The case is especially revealing because the promise was easy to understand. The hidden complexity sat underneath the clean surface. What looked like a single mat depended on coordinated sensing, power transfer, shielding and thermal behaviour across several devices. The patent record makes some of those engineering problems inspectable without pretending to expose Apple's private development history. [[3]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10491017) [[4]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10840007) [[5]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10897148B2/en)

## Location and confidence

The pin represents Apple's developer headquarters in Cupertino, California, not a factory or a known prototype-testing room. Confidence is high in the announcement, cancellation and cited patent publication dates. Confidence is lower in any claim assigning the cancellation to one specific internal failure mode. The available primary evidence supports a carefully bounded ending: announced in 2017, cancelled in 2019, never shipped.
