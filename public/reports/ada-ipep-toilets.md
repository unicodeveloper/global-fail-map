# The Ada Toilets

**Status:** Multiple projects abandoned at various stages of construction (2017–2023)
**Program:** Infrastructure for Poverty Eradication Programme (IPEP), a government-sponsored public works initiative
**Locations:** Ada and Sege constituencies, Greater Accra Region; primary documented sites include Kasseh Market and 17 toilet facility locations
**Timeline:** Contracts awarded 2017; by April 2023, all projects remained incomplete or abandoned

The IPEP initiative represented a national attempt to deliver decentralized public infrastructure—sanitation facilities and market spaces—to underserved communities across Ghana. In Ada West and East districts, the program funded the construction of 10-seater toilet facilities in seventeen separate communities and a primary market facility at Kasseh Market [[22]](https://thefourthestategh.com/2023/04/1million-per-constituency-toilets-abandoned-as-communities-defecate-in-the-open/).

**Financial scope:**
- **Sege constituency:** GHC3.3 million for 10 toilet facilities (GHC327,000 per facility)
- **Ada constituencies:** GHC595,000+ combined for additional 4-7 sites
- **Total known allocation:** Approximately GHC4 million minimum for Ada district sanitation and market infrastructure

**What was contracted:** The projects were designed to address acute public health infrastructure deficits. The seventeen toilet facility communities—Lolonya, Wokumagbe, Goi, Bornikope, Kasseh Market, Big Ada, Ada Foah, Songutsokpa, and others—had no publicly accessible sanitation infrastructure. The Kasseh Market redevelopment was intended to provide formal market trading space [[22]](https://thefourthestategh.com/2023/04/1million-per-constituency-toilets-abandoned-as-communities-defecate-in-the-open/).

**Construction status by April 2023 (six years after 2017 award):**
- Most toilet facilities: Stalled at lintel level (incomplete superstructure; walls built, roof incomplete)
- Lolonya facility: Foundation only
- Kasseh Market: Incomplete, trading space reduced, market traders displaced
- Contractors involved: Bamabani and Brwomnmos Real Estate

**Post-abandonment repurposing:** The uncompleted structures were converted to alternative uses by communities:
- Charcoal storage facilities
- Refuse dumping grounds
- Cannabis growing hideouts
- Playgrounds for children
- Informal shelter spaces

**Why abandonment occurred:** Contractors cited multiple barriers, but the principal cause was cost escalation. The original contract values, set at the 2017 award date, became economically unviable by 2018–2020 as material costs and labor wages increased substantially. Concrete, reinforcing steel, and skilled labor all cost significantly more than the original estimates. Contractors reported that the consultant (the intermediary body responsible for contract management) failed to transfer documentation and approvals in a timely manner, further delaying work and increasing holding costs [[3]](https://thefourthestategh.com/2023/09/ipep-abandoned-projects-contractors-call-for-revaluation-payment/).

The contracting authority—the Community Development Agency (CDA)—did not issue public communications about project suspension, did not adjust contract values, and did not revise timelines. The projects simply stalled, leaving communities with incomplete infrastructure and unfulfilled expectations [[22]](https://thefourthestategh.com/2023/04/1million-per-constituency-toilets-abandoned-as-communities-defecate-in-the-open/).

**Public health impact:** The sanitation failure had quantifiable health consequences. In Ada East District alone, documented diarrhea cases totaled 2,729 between 2020 and 2022, a surge attributed partly to the absence of public sanitation infrastructure [[22]](https://thefourthestategh.com/2023/04/1million-per-constituency-toilets-abandoned-as-communities-defecate-in-the-open/). The failure to complete toilet facilities meant that communities continued open defecation, contaminating groundwater and increasing disease burden.

**Contractor response:** In September 2023, contractors formally petitioned the government for contract revaluation and payment for work already completed. They argued that material and labor costs had escalated beyond the original contract value and that completing the projects under the original terms was economically impossible [[3]](https://thefourthestategh.com/2023/09/ipep-abandoned-projects-contractors-call-for-revaluation-payment/). No resolution was documented in available sources as of 2024.
