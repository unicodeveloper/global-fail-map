# The Hazaribagh tannery relocation

## What Was Built and Why It Mattered

For over a century, the Hazaribagh neighborhood in central Dhaka housed approximately 250 leather tanneries—the heart of Bangladesh's leather industry and a major source of employment. By the early 2000s, the tanneries had become a focal point in environmental and public health debates. Untreated tannery wastewater containing chrome and other heavy metals discharged directly into the Buriganga River, visibly darkening its waters and rendering stretches unsuitable for bathing, fishing, or irrigation. The government identified relocation as the solution: move all tanneries to a modern, centralized facility with wastewater treatment infrastructure and environmental compliance systems. The Bangladesh Small and Cottage Industries Corporation (BSCIC) undertook the relocation, establishing a new tannery estate in Hemayetpur, Savar district, approximately 30 kilometers north of central Dhaka.

## Timeline and Cost

The relocation project ran from 2003 to 2017, with the new Hemayetpur facility commissioned around 2010–2012 after most tanneries had relocated. The total project cost reached **Tk 1,078 crore** [[10]](https://www.tbsnews.net/environment/saving-buriganga-farce), making it one of the largest environmental remediation efforts in Bangladesh at that time.

## Geographic Connection

The original Hazaribagh site occupied a 3.5-hectare footprint in the heart of central Dhaka's commercial and residential zone. The relocation destination was Hemayetpur, Savar Upazila, Dhaka district, approximately 30 kilometers north of the original site. This distance was intended to separate the polluting industry from dense population centers, though it complicated transportation logistics for leather traders who had historically operated in the city center.

## Why It Failed: Scope Mismatch, Not Industry Failure

The critical finding emerged after relocation was largely complete. Environmental analysis determined that **Hazaribagh tanneries contributed only 6% of the total pollution load** entering the Buriganga River [[10]](https://www.tbsnews.net/environment/saving-buriganga-farce). The remaining 94% originated from municipal sewage (40%), pharmaceutical discharge (25%), textile mills (12%), and other sources. By relocating the tanneries at enormous cost, the government addressed approximately one-twentieth of the Buriganga's pollution problem—while doing nothing about the vastly larger contributors that remained.

Moreover, the relocated tannery estate did not solve the pollution problem; it displaced it. The Hemayetpur facility now discharges wastewater into the Dhaleshwari River instead of the Buriganga, transferring rather than eliminating the environmental burden. M. Inamul Haque, chairman of the Institute of Water and Environment at Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET), stated that the project was "fundamentally misdiagnosed." The relocation represented a massive capital deployment addressing a symptom rather than attacking root causes of river pollution.

## What Survived

The relocated tannery industry itself survived and continues to operate. The Hemayetpur estate functions as a working industrial zone, and Bangladesh's leather export industry (worth \$1.2 billion annually) depends substantially on this facility. However, **the environmental objective—cleansing the Buriganga—was not achieved**. The project is therefore best classified as **strategically abandoned**: its founding rationale proved incomplete, and no compensatory action addressed the larger pollution sources.
