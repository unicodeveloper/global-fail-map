# El Hamdania Port

## What Was Attempted

Algeria attempted to build one of Africa's largest deep-water ports at Cherchell, approximately 90 kilometers west of Algiers. [[2]](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/algerias-el-hamdania-port) The Port of El Hamdania was conceived as the country's first deep-water facility, designed to handle container ships and bulk cargo on a scale previously impossible in Algeria's existing harbor infrastructure. [[1]](https://www.agbi.com/infrastructure/2025/03/algeria-cancels-deep-water-port-project-with-china/) The project specifications were ambitious: a planned annual capacity of 25-26 million tonnes, 23 docking quays, and 6.5 million twenty-foot equivalent units (TEU) of container capacity annually. [[2]](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/algerias-el-hamdania-port) [[24]](https://www.portseurope.com/algeria-cancels-el-hamdania-deep-water-port-project/)

## Timeline and Geographic Connection

The project originated from a January 2016 agreement signed between Algerian authorities and two Chinese state enterprises: China State Construction Engineering Corporation (CSCEC) and China Harbour Engineering Company (CHEC). [[26]](https://chinamenanewsletter.substack.com/p/xinyi-glass-investment-in-egypt-algeria) Construction was intended to begin shortly thereafter, positioning the port as a cornerstone of Algeria's Belt and Road Initiative participation. The geographic location at Cherchell—a natural harbor site on Algeria's Mediterranean coast—offered deep-water access without the congestion constraints of the Port of Algiers, which remains the country's primary commercial hub. [[5]](https://turkkraft.com/en/knowledge-base/ports-of-algeria)

## Suspension and Cancellation

The project experienced critical suspension in February 2019, coinciding with Algeria's Hirak pro-democracy uprising. [[25]](https://www.africaintelligence.com/north-africa/2025/03/11/algiers-shelves-plans-for-cherchell-mega-port,110385547-art) Despite the initial 2016 agreement and nine years of planning, construction never commenced. By March 2025, Algerian officials formally shelved the project. [[1]](https://www.agbi.com/infrastructure/2025/03/algeria-cancels-deep-water-port-project-with-china/) [[24]](https://www.portseurope.com/algeria-cancels-el-hamdania-deep-water-port-project/) The decision came after years of documented problems: lack of government project management expertise, internal bureaucratic conflicts, corruption, and political instability had rendered the initiative unviable. [[25]](https://www.africaintelligence.com/north-africa/2025/03/11/algiers-shelves-plans-for-cherchell-mega-port,110385547-art)

## Why It Failed

The specific causes were not technical or engineering-related; detailed preliminary design work had been completed by the international consulting firm Antea Group and Danish engineering firm Ramboll. [[4]](https://int.anteagroup.com/projects/port-centre-el-hamdania-in-cherchell-algeria) Rather, the failure reflected institutional capacity constraints. Multiple independent sources cite management deficiencies, governmental squabbling, and graft as decisive factors. [[25]](https://www.africaintelligence.com/north-africa/2025/03/11/algiers-shelves-plans-for-cherchell-mega-port,110385547-art) The Hirak civil unrest of 2019 accelerated the deterioration of the project's political support. By 2025, with Algeria facing cash constraints and shifting development priorities, the commitment to Beijing evaporated entirely.

## What Survived

The cancellation did not eliminate the planning work. Comprehensive preliminary designs, environmental impact assessments, and technical specifications prepared by Anteta Group remain documented. [[4]](https://int.anteagroup.com/projects/port-centre-el-hamdania-in-cherchell-algeria) However, no physical infrastructure was constructed during the nine-year interval—no docks, cranes, or harbor dredging ever took place at Cherchell. The project represents a pure investment in planning that produced zero built assets.
