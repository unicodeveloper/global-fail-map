## The car outlived the company

The Fisker Ocean reached customers. Its manufacturer did not reach a sustainable business. Production paused in March 2024, and the group began entering Chapter 11 that June. By October, the bankruptcy process had produced a plan for liquidation rather than a return to building cars. [[1]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1720990/000119312524167032/0001193125-24-167032.txt) [[2]](https://www.davispolk.com/experience/fisker-confirms-and-consummates-chapter-11-plan)

The legal timeline matters. Fisker Group Inc. filed on June 17, 2024; Fisker Inc. and other US affiliates followed on June 19. The company's June 24 SEC filing documents both events. This case concerns Fisker Inc., the Ocean manufacturer, not the earlier Fisker Automotive business associated with the Karma. [[1]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1720990/000119312524167032/0001193125-24-167032.txt)

## A manufacturer without its own factory

Fisker's model separated vehicle design and sales from assembly. The Ocean was manufactured by Magna Steyr in Graz, Austria. Fisker expected that relationship to help it scale production without building a conventional car factory of its own. Its 2023 production updates show a company still trying to turn manufacturing capacity into vehicles delivered to paying customers. [[3]](https://investors.fiskerinc.com/news/news-details/2023/Fisker-Ocean-to-Ramp-up-Production-to-an-Expected-300-Units-Per-Day-in-Q4-and-Accelerate-U.S.-Deliveries-in-September-3123-Customer-Vehicles-Built/default.aspx)

That distinction is central to the story. A finished vehicle is not the same as a completed sale, and a sale is not the end of the manufacturer's obligations. Inventory consumes cash while software, spare parts and service have to remain available after delivery. The Ocean's later bankruptcy arrangements show how consequential those continuing obligations became. [[2]](https://www.davispolk.com/experience/fisker-confirms-and-consummates-chapter-11-plan)

## The financing route closed

In March 2024, Fisker announced a six-week production pause while it worked on inventory and financing. Contemporary reporting put that decision alongside a rapidly deteriorating cash position and substantial unsold inventory. The pause was a commercial and liquidity decision, not an announcement that the assembly process could never produce a working vehicle. [[4]](https://www.wired.com/story/fisker-suspends-electric-vehicle-production/) [[5]](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/18/fisker-production-pause-going-concern-cash-raise/)

An SEC filing then recorded that negotiations with a large automaker had ended on March 22. That also prevented Fisker from satisfying a condition of a previously announced financing arrangement. The filing supplies a direct link between the failed transaction and the loss of an available funding route; it does not establish that one negotiation alone explains the entire collapse. [[6]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1720990/000172099024000029/0001720990-24-000029.txt)

## Bankruptcy did not erase the recalls

The company's financial failure and the Ocean's safety recalls are related parts of the history, but they are not interchangeable statuses. A bankruptcy applies to an entity and its debts. A recall concerns a specified defect or compliance problem in a defined vehicle population.

For example, NHTSA recall 24V-865 covered 7,745 potentially affected US Ocean vehicles. The November 2024 report described unintended movement or shifting complaints and an Automatic Vehicle Hold software remedy released earlier that year. This is evidence of a particular safety issue, not evidence that every Ocean was unusable. [[7]](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2024/RCLRPT-24V865-6551.PDF)

## What remained

Fisker's restructuring counsel records an October 16, 2024 order approving its liquidation plan, effective October 17. The plan was supported by a fleet sale of approximately 3,300 vehicles to American Lease, with up to $46.5 million in proceeds. [[2]](https://www.davispolk.com/experience/fisker-confirms-and-consummates-chapter-11-plan)

The settlement also contemplated American Lease operating the Fisker cloud for five years from January 1, 2025, and continued recall-related remediation. Those arrangements preserved a path for connected services after the manufacturer stopped operating. They are not a blanket guarantee that every warranty claim, repair or future software problem would be covered. [[2]](https://www.davispolk.com/experience/fisker-confirms-and-consummates-chapter-11-plan)

## Where the pin belongs

The pin marks La Palma, California: the principal executive offices listed on the face of Fisker's bankruptcy-related SEC filing. The filing's older index metadata still says Manhattan Beach, which is why reading the actual document matters. Graz was the manufacturing site; Delaware was the bankruptcy court's jurisdiction. [[1]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1720990/000119312524167032/0001193125-24-167032.txt)

Confidence is high in the dated production, bankruptcy and liquidation events. The public record supports a financing and execution breakdown, but not a precise allocation of blame among pricing, demand, software, manufacturing and management. The lasting question is how a connected product remains supportable when its original supplier disappears.
