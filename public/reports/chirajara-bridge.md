# The Chirajara bridge

## The Collapse

On January 15, 2018, the Chirajara bridge, a **614-meter cable-stayed structure** under construction on the Bogotá–Villavicencio highway at kilometer 64 in the municipality of Guacheneá (approximately **60 miles southeast of central Bogotá**), experienced a catastrophic partial collapse [[18]](https://www.thestructuralengineer.info/news/colombian-under-construction-bridge-collapsed-probably-due-to-high-loads) [[19]](https://www.enr.com/articles/43848-colombia-launches-probe-into-deadly-bridge-collapse) [[20]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-42701262) [[21]](https://www.constructionbriefing.com/news/construction-workers-killed-in-colombia-bridge-collapse/1131167.article) [[22]](https://www.dw.com/en/bridge-collapse-in-colombia-kills-10-construction-workers-several-missing/a-42159760) [[23]](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/nine-workers-killed-in-colombia-bridge-collapse) [[25]](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/world/at-least-10-construction-workers-killed-after-bridge-collapses-in-colombia-a3741301.html). The bridge was designed as a critical link on the Fourth Generation (4G) highway concession program, which represented Colombia's most ambitious private infrastructure initiative.

The collapse occurred at approximately midday and proved immediately fatal. Initial reports confirmed **9 to 10 construction workers killed** [[20]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-42701262) [[22]](https://www.dw.com/en/bridge-collapse-in-colombia-kills-10-construction-workers-several-missing/a-42159760) [[23]](https://www.sbs.com.au/news/nine-workers-killed-in-colombia-bridge-collapse), with additional workers missing and injured [[19]](https://www.enr.com/articles/43848-colombia-launches-probe-into-deadly-bridge-collapse). One source identified the death toll more precisely as **10 construction workers** [[19]](https://www.enr.com/articles/43848-colombia-launches-probe-into-deadly-bridge-collapse) [[25]](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/world/at-least-10-construction-workers-killed-after-bridge-collapses-in-colombia-a3741301.html). The structure itself—a **446-meter-long bridge** according to some accounts [[22]](https://www.dw.com/en/bridge-collapse-in-colombia-kills-10-construction-workers-several-missing/a-42159760), though other sources cite the longer **614-meter cable-stayed design** [[18]](https://www.thestructuralengineer.info/news/colombian-under-construction-bridge-collapsed-probably-due-to-high-loads)—partially fell into a ravine, creating a scene of twisted steel and concrete that would become emblematic of Colombia's infrastructure challenges. The bridge was originally scheduled to open in **March 2018** as part of the major highway upgrade linking the capital to Colombia's eastern plains [[18]](https://www.thestructuralengineer.info/news/colombian-under-construction-bridge-collapsed-probably-due-to-high-loads) [[22]](https://www.dw.com/en/bridge-collapse-in-colombia-kills-10-construction-workers-several-missing/a-42159760).

## Causal Analysis and Historical Context

The immediate cause of the collapse reflected design inadequacy combined with geotechnical challenges. The Chirajara location sits in **mountainous terrain prone to landslides** [[20]](https://www.bbc.com/news/world-latin-america-42701262), and the site carried historical trauma: **approximately 200 people had been killed in a landslide at the same location in 1974**, establishing a precedent of geological instability [[18]](https://www.thestructuralengineer.info/news/colombian-under-construction-bridge-collapsed-probably-due-to-high-loads). Structural engineers investigating the collapse attributed it to **high loads on unstable terrain and inadequate design provisions** for the specific geotechnical conditions [[18]](https://www.thestructuralengineer.info/news/colombian-under-construction-bridge-collapsed-probably-due-to-high-loads).

The collapse triggered an official investigation by Colombian authorities [[19]](https://www.enr.com/articles/43848-colombia-launches-probe-into-deadly-bridge-collapse). The bridge was later demolished—with the remaining structure eliminated through controlled detonation in **July 2018** [[24]](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/bridge-demolish-explosion-colombia-chirajara-video-kills-a8444936.html).

## Reconstruction and Current Status

Rather than permanently abandoning the crossing, the Colombian National Infrastructure Agency (ANI) and contractor Coviandes committed to replacement. A **new bridge was constructed with a revised design** featuring a **421.2-meter three-span structure**, reflecting lessons learned from the initial failure [[36]](https://www.deltaarquitecturadigital.com/coviandes-finaliza-construccion-del-nuevo-puente-chirajara-anticipadamente-pero-desafios-persisten-en-la-via-bogota-villavicencio/).

Construction of the replacement bridge proceeded and was completed ahead of schedule. Coviandes announced the **completion of the new bridge on January 12, 2024**, with the original 32-month construction timeline compressed through accelerated work [[40]](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/infraestructura/puente-chirajara-finalizo-la-construccion-de-esta-obra-en-la-via-bogota-villavicencio-via-al-llano-595859). The **final completion was achieved on February 4, 2024** [[34]](https://www.eltiempo.com/bogota/puente-chirajara-coviandes-informo-que-fue-terminado-de-manera-anticipada-844067), representing a successful engineering remediation of the initial catastrophe.

However, the bridge's completion did not result in operational service. As of **May 8, 2026**, the new Chirajara viaduct **"remains closed despite being ready since 2024,"** unable to function because **adjacent tunnel infrastructure (Tunnels 12, 13, 14 and related structures) suffered damage**, and **5.6 kilometers of dual carriageway between Bogotá and Villavicencio had been under construction for two years without entering into operation** [[35]](https://subaalternativa.co/puente-chirajara-sigue-cerrado-pese-a-estar-listo-desde-2024/) [[37]](https://revista.costosperu.com/56-kilometros-de-doble-calzada-entre-bogota-y-villavicencio-llevan-dos-anos-construidos-y-sin-entrar-en-operacion/). The report indicates the corridor operates with **only a single lane in the Guayabetal sector**, while **five strategic tunnels remain damaged or incomplete** [[37]](https://revista.costosperu.com/56-kilometros-de-doble-calzada-entre-bogota-y-villavicencio-llevan-dos-anos-construidos-y-sin-entrar-en-operacion/).

The operational blockade reflected a fundamental breakdown in project coordination. Investigation by Colombia's Comptroller General (the national audit authority) revealed that **the ANI had failed to adequately manage completion of supporting infrastructure**, despite the Chirajara bridge itself being finished. On **August 5, 2026**, the Comptroller issued a **fiscal finding against the ANI for \$824,960 million pesos** (approximately \$206 million USD at current exchange rates), specifically citing the ANI's mismanagement in enabling the opening of the completed viaduct [[38]](https://www.elnuevosiglo.com.co/nacion/contraloria-pone-la-lupa-sobre-contrato-4g-de-chirajara) [[42]](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/infraestructura/viaducto-chirajara-sigue-sin-operar-contraloria-abre-hallazgo-fiscal-por-824-960-millones-contra-la-ani-499748).

## Remediation and Future Timeline

Responding to escalating pressure, **the ANI announced on August 6, 2026, that it would allocate \$68 billion Colombian pesos** (approximately \$17 million USD) to stabilize kilometer 58 of the highway corridor and rehabilitate the Chirajara bridge for operation [[41]](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/infraestructura/ani-destinara-68-000-millones-para-estabilizar-kilometro-58-de-la-via-al-llano-y-rehabilitar-el-puente-chirajara-499861). However, this announced funding carried an estimated **two-year completion timeline**, projecting operational service would not commence until **2028** at the earliest—**nearly a decade after the original collapse** [[39]](https://www.eluniversal.com.co/colombia/2026/08/06/chirajara-cerca-de-operar-ani-y-coviandes-avanzan-en-acuerdo-clave/).

## Assessment

The Chirajara case represents a hybrid failure: **successful technical reconstruction overshadowed by systemic institutional failure** in managing the broader corridor. The bridge itself was rebuilt competently and ahead of schedule, yet the wider 4G highway concession collapsed into administrative paralysis, cost overruns, and jurisdictional disputes between the private contractor and government authority. The structure stands complete but inoperative—a monument to Colombian infrastructure's fundamental challenge of integrating multiple projects under fragmented governance.
