# The Greater Kampala Light Rail

## What Was Envisioned

The Greater Kampala Light Rail was conceived as a modern, rapid transit system serving the greater metropolitan area, including connections between Kampala's central business district and surrounding towns: **Port Bell, Namanve, Kyengera, Kawempe, Kira, Wakiso, and Mukono**. A light rail system would reduce dependence on private vehicles and informal bus networks (matatus), improving air quality and reducing congestion. Estimated cost: over **\$1 billion**.

## Historical Timeline: Three Iterations, Eighteen Years of Non-Delivery

**First Planning Cycle (2008–2023):**
The project was first formally proposed in the National Transport Master Plan (2008–2023). This comprehensive blueprint, developed with government and development partner support, allocated resources and identified the light rail as a centerpiece of Kampala's modernization. When the plan's 15-year horizon expired in 2023, no construction had commenced [[11]](https://www.kcca.go.ug/media/docs/Chapter%206%20FINAL%20Transport%20Mobility%20and%20Connectivity%20Strategy.pdf).

**Second Planning Cycle (2016–2021):**
The National Resistance Movement (NRM), Uganda's ruling party, included the light rail in its 2016–2021 election manifesto, re-promising the system to voters. No implementation followed [[7]](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608250124.html).

**Third Planning Cycle (2021–2026):**
The NRM again included light rail in its 2021–2026 manifesto, maintaining the commitment. Again, no tangible progress occurred [[7]](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608250124.html).

**Current Status (March 2026):**
In March 2026, the government signed a **Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with Orascom Construction Company** (an Egyptian construction giant) to conduct an **18-month feasibility study** for the light rail [[9]](https://ubc.go.ug/2026/03/14/uganda-signs-mou-for-feasibility-study-on-kampala-light-rail-system/) [[10]](https://nilepost.co.ug/news/327482/uganda-signs-deal-with-orascom-to-study-light-rail-transit-system-for-greater-kampala). This feasibility study—the same type of preliminary work that typically precedes actual project initiation—represents the most concrete action in 18 years. However, a feasibility study is not construction; it is a planning step that precedes the decision to finance and build. If the study is completed on schedule in September 2027, and if a decision to proceed is made promptly, actual construction could begin in 2028 at the earliest—24 years after the project was first formally proposed.

## The Pilot Project: A Glimpse of What Might Have Been

A narrow pilot was attempted: the **Namanve–Kampala Passenger Rail Service** (2015–2018). This service operated an existing railway line with refurbished rolling stock, offering scheduled passenger service connecting the industrial suburb of Namanve to central Kampala. Over its 3-year run, the service achieved **942,982 passenger trips**, demonstrating genuine demand for rail-based urban mobility [[11]](https://www.kcca.go.ug/media/docs/Chapter%206%20FINAL%20Transport%20Mobility%20and%20Connectivity%20Strategy.pdf). However, the service was suspended in 2018 for rehabilitation. When it resumed in April 2024, it operated with minimal frequency and limited service hours, failing to establish itself as a reliable transit option. The pilot thus served as a proof-of-concept that was never scaled [[8]](https://www.independent.co.ug/passenger-rail-for-kampala-set-to-resume/).

## Why It Was Never Built: Institutional and Political Barriers

The light rail has not been built despite 18 years of repeated political commitments because:

1. **Lack of dedicated financing**: No government budget line item or concessional loan was secured to fund construction.
2. **Competing priorities**: Road-based investments (often via Chinese contracts) took precedence in capital allocation.
3. **Institutional capacity**: The Uganda Railways Corporation and Ministry of Works lacked the institutional experience and staffing to develop and oversee a complex urban transit project.
4. **Political discontinuity**: Electoral cycles and shifting government priorities meant that each administration re-proposed the project without inheriting the momentum from its predecessor [[7]](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608250124.html).
5. **Informal transport dominance**: The matatu (minibus) industry, deeply embedded in Kampala's politics and economy, has no incentive to support formal rail, creating political opposition to the project [[8]](https://www.independent.co.ug/passenger-rail-for-kampala-set-to-resume/).

## Geographic Scope and Population Served

The proposed light rail network would have served all five divisions of Kampala (Central, Nakawa, Lubaga, Makindye, Kawempe) and extended into Wakiso and Mukono districts. The metropolitan area population of approximately 2 million residents—and an estimated 500,000+ daily commuters from surrounding districts—would have benefited from rapid transit connectivity.

## Status: NEVER CONSTRUCTED / PERPETUALLY PLANNED

As of September 2026, no physical infrastructure has been built. The project exists only in strategic plans, manifestos, and now an MOU for a feasibility study. The perpetual replanning without execution suggests a pattern of political posturing rather than genuine implementation intent.
