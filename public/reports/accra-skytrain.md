# The Accra SkyTrain

**Status:** Stalled indefinitely; likely abandoned (status unconfirmed post-August 2020)
**Concession signing date:** November 11, 2019, at Africa Investment Forum, Johannesburg, South Africa
**Concession cost:** \$2.6 billion USD (also reported as \$3 billion in some sources)
**Operator consortium:** Africa Investment SkyTrain Consortium Holdings (South African entities: Wilson Bayly Holmes Ovcon, Bunengi Group, Ai Capital)
**Design specifications:**
- Network: 194 km elevated automated monorail system
- Routes: 5 separate lines
- Capacity: 380,000 passengers per day
- Technology: Air-powered automated system

**Promised timeline (November 2019):**
- Financial close: November 2020
- Construction start: January 2020
- First station delivery: August 2020

The Accra SkyTrain concession was a boldface attempt to modernize Accra's public transport system through a single megaproject. The elevated monorail would have served an estimated 6 million residents of Greater Accra, providing rapid transit across the sprawling metropolitan area [[11]](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/concession-agreement-signed-for-3bn-accra-skytrain-2019-11-11) [[12]](https://www.globalconstructionreview.com/26bn-concession-agreement-signed-ghanas-air-powere/) [[13]](https://africanreview.com/transport-a-logistics/rail/ghana-to-construct-us-2-6bn-accra-skytrain) [[14]](https://www.panafricabd.com/news-articles/ghana-to-construct-us2-6bn-accra-skytrain/) [[15]](https://www.khl.com/international-construction/ghana-to-build-us26-billion-skytrain/141268.article) [[16]](https://www.transportadvancement.com/railway/ghana-signs-concession-agreement-for-2-6bn-high-speed-railway-project/) [[20]](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/ghana-signs-2-3bn-concession-for-skytrain-project.html).

The government signed the concession agreement on November 11, 2019, following more than a year of negotiation with the South African consortium [[11]](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/concession-agreement-signed-for-3bn-accra-skytrain-2019-11-11). The agreement specified that Cabinet and Parliamentary approval would be obtained within 45 days, with construction commencement targeted for January 2020 and the first operational station expected by August 2020 [[17]](https://www.ghanaweb.com/GhanaHomePage/NewsArchive/Accra-to-have-first-sky-train-station-in-August-2020-798391) [[18]](https://www.graphic.com.gh/news/general-news/ghana-signs-agreement-for-accra-sky-train-project.html) [[19]](https://thecophq.org/news/ghana-signs-agreement-for-accra-sky-train-project/).

**What actually happened:** None of the promised milestones were met. By August 2020, less than a year after the concession signing, the Minister of Information confirmed publicly that the SkyTrain project "has not started" [[23]](https://3news.com/accra-sky-train-project-not-started-oppong-nkrumah/). The Financial close did not occur in November 2020. No construction commenced. No station was built.

**Stated reason for stalling:** Government and media sources attributed the delay to COVID-19 pandemic impacts and the South African consortium's reported withdrawal. The precise nature of the consortium's withdrawal is not documented in available sources, but it appears that the South African partners lost interest or financial capacity to proceed post-pandemic [[23]](https://3news.com/accra-sky-train-project-not-started-oppong-nkrumah/).

**Current status (2023–2026):** As of available sources, the SkyTrain project remains in an undefined state. No explicit cancellation has been announced. No resumption timeline has been published. The project exists in a state of indefinite suspension—not formally alive, but not officially dead [[23]](https://3news.com/accra-sky-train-project-not-started-oppong-nkrumah/) [[24]](https://www.ghanabusinessnews.com/2020/07/14/accra-sky-train-project-remains-on-course-ministry/) [[25]](https://www.tremafrica.com/projects/accra-skytrain-ghana/).

**Geographic scope (if it had been built):** The 194 km network would have connected Accra's disparate zones—from Kasoa in the west to Tema in the east, and from Adentan in the northeast to Labadi/coastal areas in the south. It would have been the single largest transport infrastructure undertaking in Ghana's history [[11]](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/concession-agreement-signed-for-3bn-accra-skytrain-2019-11-11) [[12]](https://www.globalconstructionreview.com/26bn-concession-agreement-signed-ghanas-air-powere/) [[13]](https://africanreview.com/transport-a-logistics/rail/ghana-to-construct-us-2-6bn-accra-skytrain) [[14]](https://www.panafricabd.com/news-articles/ghana-to-construct-us2-6bn-accra-skytrain/) [[15]](https://www.khl.com/international-construction/ghana-to-build-us26-billion-skytrain/141268.article) [[16]](https://www.transportadvancement.com/railway/ghana-signs-concession-agreement-for-2-6bn-high-speed-railway-project/).

**Impact of non-realization:** Accra's public transport remains fragmented, reliant on informal minibuses ("tro-tros") and limited commuter rail. The SkyTrain's non-materialization left a gap in the metropolitan area's transport infrastructure that has not been filled by alternative projects.
