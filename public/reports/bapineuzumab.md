## An antibody programme ends

On August 6, 2012, Pfizer and Janssen announced the discontinuation of intravenous bapineuzumab studies in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease. Two completed Phase 3 trials had not met their co-primary clinical endpoints. The decision covered the remaining intravenous studies and their extensions, not every formulation or every anti-amyloid research programme. [[1]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_co_primary_clinical_endpoints_not_met_in_second_phase_3_bapineuzumab_study_in_mild_to_moderate_alzheimer_s_disease_patients_who_do_not_carry_the_apoe4_genotype)

Bapineuzumab was an experimental monoclonal antibody directed against amyloid-beta. The attempt was to turn a biological theory about Alzheimer's disease into a measurable preservation of cognition and everyday function. The result was disappointing precisely because a large clinical programme had been built to test that promise. [[1]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_co_primary_clinical_endpoints_not_met_in_second_phase_3_bapineuzumab_study_in_mild_to_moderate_alzheimer_s_disease_patients_who_do_not_carry_the_apoe4_genotype) [[2]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_topline_results_of_first_of_four_studies_in_bapineuzumab_phase_3_program)

## Four trials, two genetic groups

The Phase 3 programme included four studies. Janssen led Studies 301 and 302, while Pfizer led Studies 3000 and 3001. The completed Janssen studies separated people who carried the APOE4 genetic variant from those who did not. Pfizer's announcement described approximately 1,100 participants in the carrier study and 1,300 in the non-carrier study. [[1]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_co_primary_clinical_endpoints_not_met_in_second_phase_3_bapineuzumab_study_in_mild_to_moderate_alzheimer_s_disease_patients_who_do_not_carry_the_apoe4_genotype)

The corresponding registry records identify the carrier trial as **NCT00575055** and the non-carrier trial as **NCT00574132**. Those records help keep the actual experiments distinct from the larger programme and from later studies with different formulations. [[3]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00575055) [[4]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00574132)

Genetic stratification was a feature of the study design, not evidence that either group was guaranteed to benefit. The relevant question remained whether treatment performed better than placebo on the prespecified clinical measures.

## What the endpoints showed

The studies measured cognition with the Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale and function with the Disability Assessment for Dementia. On July 23, 2012, Pfizer reported that the carrier study had missed its co-primary endpoints. The August announcement reported the same broad outcome in the non-carrier study and the decision to stop the remaining IV programme. [[1]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_co_primary_clinical_endpoints_not_met_in_second_phase_3_bapineuzumab_study_in_mild_to_moderate_alzheimer_s_disease_patients_who_do_not_carry_the_apoe4_genotype) [[2]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_topline_results_of_first_of_four_studies_in_bapineuzumab_phase_3_program)

This does not mean that every biological measurement was unchanged, that every patient had an identical experience or that every imaginable subgroup was ruled out. It means the completed trials did not demonstrate the required benefit on their principal clinical measures. Post-hoc patterns cannot replace that prespecified test.

## Safety and efficacy are different questions

Amyloid-related imaging abnormalities were among the safety issues studied with bapineuzumab. Pfizer's August statement listed ARIA-E, involving edema or effusion, among serious adverse events observed more often in treated participants in Study 301. It also stated that the study had not identified new safety concerns. [[1]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_co_primary_clinical_endpoints_not_met_in_second_phase_3_bapineuzumab_study_in_mild_to_moderate_alzheimer_s_disease_patients_who_do_not_carry_the_apoe4_genotype)

The documented decision was driven by the efficacy results. Calling the programme a safety recall would therefore misstate both the regulatory status and the reason given for ending it. Bapineuzumab was investigational; the intravenous treatment was not a marketed medicine being recalled from routine use. [[1]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_co_primary_clinical_endpoints_not_met_in_second_phase_3_bapineuzumab_study_in_mild_to_moderate_alzheimer_s_disease_patients_who_do_not_carry_the_apoe4_genotype)

## What survived the decision

Participants in the discontinued studies were to receive follow-up evaluations, and the collaborators planned final analyses. Pfizer explicitly said that subgroup and biomarker analyses could still inform understanding of the disease. Ending dosing did not mean discarding the evidence gathered. [[1]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_announces_co_primary_clinical_endpoints_not_met_in_second_phase_3_bapineuzumab_study_in_mild_to_moderate_alzheimer_s_disease_patients_who_do_not_carry_the_apoe4_genotype)

The wider immunotherapy collaboration also contained other work. The August 2012 event should not be stretched into a claim that all amyloid-directed treatment research ended or that one trial programme settled the entire disease mechanism. Its scope was a particular antibody, route of administration, disease stage and clinical programme.

## Why this place is mapped

The pin marks South San Francisco, home to Janssen Alzheimer Immunotherapy R&D LLC, the co-developer unit identified in contemporaneous reporting. It is a corporate research location, not a claim that the thousands of trial participants were treated at one Californian site. [[5]](https://www.bizjournals.com/sanfrancisco/blog/biotech/2012/08/pfizer-janssen-bapineuzumab-alzheimers.html)

Confidence is high in the July and August announcements, the trial identities and the IV discontinuation decision. Confidence is more limited when assigning one mechanism to the absence of clinical benefit. The case remains useful because it shows how a clear negative clinical result can coexist with continuing scientific questions and a body of data worth preserving.
