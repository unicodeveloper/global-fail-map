## A working prototype, a cancelled business

Dyson announced the cancellation of its electric-car programme on October 10, 2019. The project, internally called N526, had produced driveable prototypes, but James Dyson said the company could no longer see a commercially viable way forward. The status is a cancelled vehicle programme, not a claim that the prototype could not move or that Dyson itself closed. [[1]](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/10/10/dyson-scraps-its-electric-car-plans.html) [[2]](https://www.wired.com/story/dyson-electric-car-cancelled-inside-story/) [[3]](https://www.dyson.com/james-dyson/invention-a-life/09)

The distinction is partly a matter of attribution. Dyson described the car as an engineering achievement. That is the developer's assessment, not an independent certification that every requirement of volume production had been satisfied. The public evidence clearly establishes that development had progressed well beyond a sketch and stopped before a customer launch. [[4]](https://www.dyson.com/newsroom/news/corporate/dyson-battery-electric-vehicle)

## Building a car from a different starting point

Dyson's public retrospective describes a vehicle platform with a battery integrated into its structure, bespoke electric drive units, large wheels and three rows of seating. The company wanted to transfer experience in motors, batteries, air treatment and product design into a different industry. [[4]](https://www.dyson.com/newsroom/news/corporate/dyson-battery-electric-vehicle)

Its published images, models and development material make the attempt unusually tangible. This was not simply a brand name placed on an existing car. But designing the vehicle was only part of the task: a customer-facing automotive business also needed manufacturing, distribution, service and a price that could sustain it.

## Why Hullavington is on the map

The automotive team initially worked at Dyson's Malmesbury campus, then moved into the larger former airfield at Hullavington, Wiltshire. Dyson's own account dates the acquisition of the airfield sections to February 27, 2017, and describes restoring the hangars for development work. [[4]](https://www.dyson.com/newsroom/news/corporate/dyson-battery-electric-vehicle)

Hullavington was a development and testing location. The proposed manufacturing operation in Singapore was a different part of the plan. Conflating those roles would turn a map of where an attempt happened into an inaccurate picture of where completed cars were manufactured. [[5]](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/dyson-to-close-automotive-division) [[4]](https://www.dyson.com/newsroom/news/corporate/dyson-battery-electric-vehicle)

## Patents are part of the trail, not a verdict

Vehicle patent drawings became public in May 2019 and attracted attention for the proposed proportions and packaging. Contemporary reporting also relayed Dyson's warning that the drawings were not a complete preview of the final vehicle. They represented disclosed design ideas, not a delivered specification. [[6]](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/05/10/vacuum-maker-dyson-releases-patents-for-new-ev-line-to-debut-in-2021.html) [[7]](https://electrek.co/2019/05/09/dyson-ev-patent-reveal/)

The wider battery work needs equally careful labelling. Dyson acquired Sakti3 in 2015. One of Sakti3's published records, **US9065080B2**, covers an electric-vehicle propulsion system using solid-state rechargeable cells. It was granted on June 23, 2015, and lists Sakti3 as the original assignee. [[8]](https://venturebeat.com/business/dyson-acquires-sakti3-for-90m-to-help-commercialize-breakthrough-solid-state-battery-tech) [[9]](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9065080)

That patent is evidence of related battery research, not proof that the N526 prototypes used its claimed technology. It would be incorrect to infer a solid-state production battery, successful manufacturing scale-up or a specific technical failure merely from the acquisition and the patent's existence. The vehicle programme and the battery patent have distinct histories.

## The commercial decision

In its cancellation statement, Dyson said it had not found a route to commercial viability and had unsuccessfully sought a buyer for the project. The company's later account emphasised the competitive economics of entering the car market as a new manufacturer. These are stated reasons, not a publicly audited breakdown of costs and expected returns. [[1]](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/10/10/dyson-scraps-its-electric-car-plans.html) [[4]](https://www.dyson.com/newsroom/news/corporate/dyson-battery-electric-vehicle)

The evidence does not justify assigning the entire decision to one battery chemistry, one engineering problem or one competitor. Nor does it demonstrate that an alternative strategy would certainly have succeeded. It establishes that the company chose not to carry this programme into production.

## What survived

Dyson retained people, facilities and technical work beyond the car's cancellation. Its retrospective describes the subsequent use of Hullavington and the application of automotive recruits' expertise in other areas of the company. The published models, vehicle images and patent records preserve parts of the attempt for outside inspection. [[4]](https://www.dyson.com/newsroom/news/corporate/dyson-battery-electric-vehicle) [[3]](https://www.dyson.com/james-dyson/invention-a-life/09)

Confidence is high in the cancellation date and Hullavington's development role. Confidence is more limited in reconstructing the private financial calculation or independently judging full production readiness. The pin marks where substantial engineering work happened, while the report keeps technical achievement and commercial survival separate.
