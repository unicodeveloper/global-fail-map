# Omo-Kuraz Sugar

## What Was Planned

The Omo-Kuraz Sugar Development Project (OKSDP) was a massive state-led industrialization scheme comprising six sugar processing factories distributed across three "sugar blocks" with a combined concession area of 150,000–245,000 hectares of irrigated sugarcane cultivation. [[12]](https://africanstudies.web.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/africanstudies/documents/media/executive_summary_introduction.pdf) The project was designed to transform Lower Omo Valley productivity, generate foreign exchange, and produce electricity through bagasse cogeneration. [[12]](https://africanstudies.web.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/africanstudies/documents/media/executive_summary_introduction.pdf)

The project was initiated without published environmental or social impact assessments despite these being legal requirements under Ethiopian law. [[12]](https://africanstudies.web.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/africanstudies/documents/media/executive_summary_introduction.pdf)

## Geographic Connection

The sugar blocks occupied excised portions of three protected areas: Omo National Park and Mago National Park. [[12]](https://africanstudies.web.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/africanstudies/documents/media/executive_summary_introduction.pdf) The irrigation infrastructure was designed to be fed by flows from the Gibe III hydroelectric dam, located upstream on the Omo River. The Lower Omo Valley's flood-recession lands and permanent river access zones were the target geography.

## Timeline and Construction Progress

The project was initiated in 2011. By August 2022, Component VI (one of six planned processing facilities) remained approximately 20% complete. [[11]](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopian-sugar-industry-group-established-to-manage-five-state-owned-sugar-plants/) Components I–III and V achieved operational status but faced severe financial distress. [[11]](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopian-sugar-industry-group-established-to-manage-five-state-owned-sugar-plants/)

## Why Component VI Remained Unfinished

Component VI was explicitly excluded from the Ethiopian government's 2022 sugar industry privatization effort due to documented underperformance by the building contractor. [[11]](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopian-sugar-industry-group-established-to-manage-five-state-owned-sugar-plants/) The ministry announced that privatization would proceed with Components I–V only, leaving Component VI in limbo as a suspended construction project.

## Financial Crisis and Project Failure

Beyond the physical incompleteness of Component VI, the entire OKSDP encountered fundamental financial and operational failure:

- **Debt accumulation**: The Ethiopian Sugar Corporation accumulated 11.3 billion Ethiopian birr (approximately \$219 million USD) in debt by late 2021. [[11]](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopian-sugar-industry-group-established-to-manage-five-state-owned-sugar-plants/)

- **Economic underperformance**: The project "neither met the stated economic purposes" since its 2011 inception, according to government assessment as of 2022. [[11]](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopian-sugar-industry-group-established-to-manage-five-state-owned-sugar-plants/)

- **Resettlement promises unfulfilled**: Communities displaced to make way for sugar blocks reported that promised compensation and replacement services were not delivered. [[16]](https://www.oaklandinstitute.org/report/how-they-tricked-us) Traditional land use systems were dismantled without adequate alternative livelihood provision.

## Geographic Impact on Protected Areas

The sugar blocks occupied former conservation land—excised from Omo and Mago National Parks, which are UNESCO World Heritage sites. [[12]](https://africanstudies.web.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/africanstudies/documents/media/executive_summary_introduction.pdf) The conversion of protected lands to irrigated agriculture represented an irrevocable change in land designation and conservation status.

## What Survived

Unlike the Ethio-Korean farm (completely abandoned), OKSDP left partial physical infrastructure in place: roads, canal systems, cleared agricultural land, and partial processing facilities. [[11]](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/ethiopian-sugar-industry-group-established-to-manage-five-state-owned-sugar-plants/) Components I–III and V continue to operate, though they are financially unviable and dependent on subsidies. The cleared land cannot easily revert to conservation status.

## Status and Outcome

**Formal Status: UNFINISHED/SUSPENDED CONSTRUCTION and OPERATIONAL BUT FINANCIALLY FAILED**

Components I–III and V are operational but financially troubled. Component VI remains 20% complete and formally suspended. The project has not been formally abandoned but has effectively failed to achieve its stated economic objectives and promises to communities.

## Evidence Assessment

**Confidence: HIGH** — The project's status is documented in Ethiopian government documents from 2022: the Ministry of Finance tender announcements for privatization, the Food Business Middle East announcement, and contemporary news reports. The financial crisis, debt figures, and contractor performance issues are drawn from official government statements.

**Data Gaps**: The final outcome of the 2022 privatization effort (sources end at January 2023); whether Component VI has been formally abandoned or remains suspended indefinitely; detailed accounting of compensation provided vs. promised to displaced communities.
