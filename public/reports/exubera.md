## An approved medicine loses its market

Exubera was inhaled human insulin: a dry powder delivered through a specially designed device rather than a conventional insulin injection. It received FDA approval in January 2006 for adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes. Pfizer announced that it was leaving the Exubera business on October 18, 2007, after disappointing commercial uptake. [[1]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pfizer-receives-fda-approval-exubera-first-inhaleable-form) [[2]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000115752307009976/0001157523-07-009976.txt)

This is a commercially discontinued product, not a medicine that never worked at all. It is also not a story in which later safety concerns should be moved backwards in time to explain an earlier decision. Approval, market adoption, withdrawal and subsequent safety updates were separate events. [[1]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pfizer-receives-fda-approval-exubera-first-inhaleable-form) [[3]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nektar-terminates-all-negotiations-potential-partners-inhaled) [[4]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_statement_on_exubera_labeling_update_in_the_united_states)

## What the delivery technology attempted

Nektar Therapeutics developed inhalation technology used in Exubera, while Pfizer handled the product's commercialisation. Their company announcements describe the partnership and the planned US introduction. The San Carlos pin represents Nektar's co-developer headquarters during this period. [[5]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nektar-reports-pfizer-acquire-sanofi-aventis-worldwide-rights) [[6]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nektar-reports-pfizer-introduce-exubera-us-comprehensive)

The attraction was straightforward: some people who needed insulin disliked injections or delayed treatment because of them. Delivering insulin through the lungs offered another route. But a new route also introduced a new device, dose handling, training requirements and pulmonary considerations. It was not simply an injection pen with the needle removed. [[1]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pfizer-receives-fda-approval-exubera-first-inhaleable-form) [[7]](https://diabetesjournals.org/spectrum/article/29/3/180/32800/Commentary-Why-Was-Inhaled-Insulin-a-Failure-in)

## Approval was one milestone

Exubera's regulatory assessments established conditions under which it could be used. The European Medicines Agency's product record preserves the authorisation history and assessment documents, while the contemporary approval announcement describes the US indication. Those records are more informative than a blanket claim that regulators pronounced the product risk-free. [[1]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pfizer-receives-fda-approval-exubera-first-inhaleable-form) [[8]](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/exubera)

Clinical use required attention to patient selection and lung function. Patients still needed an insulin regimen appropriate to their disease; the inhaled product did not make every other aspect of diabetes care disappear. The practical experience of switching therefore mattered alongside the laboratory and trial results. [[1]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/pfizer-receives-fda-approval-exubera-first-inhaleable-form) [[7]](https://diabetesjournals.org/spectrum/article/29/3/180/32800/Commentary-Why-Was-Inhaled-Insulin-a-Failure-in)

## The commercial ending

Pfizer's October 2007 SEC-filed announcement recorded the decision to exit Exubera and a large financial charge associated with doing so. The company said the product had not gained the acceptance it had expected from patients and physicians. That is a direct account of the commercial problem, though it does not independently measure every cause of weak adoption. [[2]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000115752307009976/0001157523-07-009976.txt)

Later analysis discussed the device's size, dosing complexity, cost and the extra clinical work associated with an inhaled therapy. Those are plausible and documented adoption barriers. Their exact individual contributions cannot be reconstructed from a single sales figure or a retrospective narrative. [[7]](https://diabetesjournals.org/spectrum/article/29/3/180/32800/Commentary-Why-Was-Inhaled-Insulin-a-Failure-in)

The contrast is the point: a product can pass an approval process and still fail to fit the routines, incentives and preferences around its use.

## The later safety update

In April 2008, Nektar announced that it was ending negotiations with potential partners after additional lung-cancer cases had been observed in clinical studies. Pfizer separately issued a US labelling update. The number of cases was small, and the statements did not establish a causal relationship between the medicine and cancer. [[3]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nektar-terminates-all-negotiations-potential-partners-inhaled) [[4]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_statement_on_exubera_labeling_update_in_the_united_states)

This development must remain in the chronology. It followed Pfizer's 2007 commercial withdrawal announcement and affected what could happen next. Saying that the original withdrawal was simply a response to a proven cancer risk would merge two different events and overstate the evidence. [[3]](https://ir.nektar.com/news-releases/news-release-details/nektar-terminates-all-negotiations-potential-partners-inhaled) [[4]](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer_statement_on_exubera_labeling_update_in_the_united_states) [[2]](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000115752307009976/0001157523-07-009976.txt)

## What remains to learn

Exubera's regulatory documents and clinical history survived the product. The broader idea of inhaled insulin also continued, so this entry is not a verdict on every future device or formulation. Its boundary is a named product and the decisions made around it. [[7]](https://diabetesjournals.org/spectrum/article/29/3/180/32800/Commentary-Why-Was-Inhaled-Insulin-a-Failure-in) [[8]](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/exubera)

Confidence is high in the approval, withdrawal announcement and later safety-update dates, which have company and regulatory records. Confidence is lower in assigning exact percentages of blame to price, device design, marketing or physician preference. The location role is co-developer headquarters in San Carlos, California, not a manufacturing plant or an individual trial site.
