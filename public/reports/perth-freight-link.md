# The Perth Freight Link

The Perth Freight Link (also known as Roe 8-10 or the Perth-to-Fremantle freight route) represents the most recent and most extensively documented infrastructure failure in Perth. Announced in May 2014 by the federal government, the project aimed to construct 23 kilometres of new and upgraded roadway to divert freight traffic from Perth's city centre and create a direct link between Kewdale (in eastern Perth) and Fremantle port. [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Freight_Link)

## The Project's Scope and Geographic Connection

The route was designed in three stages: Roe Highway Stage 8 (from Leach Highway to Fremantle), termed Roe 8; the proposed new carriageway from Roe Highway to Fremantle Port as Roe 9; and the Port Fremantle upgrade as Roe 10. The most controversial and central component, Roe 8-9, passed directly through the **Beeliar Wetlands**, a 8,000-hectare protected environmental area south of Perth that serves as critical habitat for native fauna and flora. [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Freight_Link) The project would have required excavation through Aboriginal heritage sites and removal of native vegetation across sensitive ecosystems.

## Construction and Suspension

Construction on the project commenced in late 2016, and work proceeded through the first half of 2017. However, on 12 March 2017—less than four months after a change of state government—the newly elected McGowan Labor government suspended the project. The government had campaigned explicitly on opposition to the Freight Link, and incoming Premier Mark McGowan made cancellation a policy priority. ## Causes of Abandonment

The Perth Freight Link was undone by a convergence of obstacles:

**Environmental and Regulatory**: The Environmental Protection Authority (EPA) initially rejected the project's environmental assessment in January 2013, citing unacceptable impacts on the Beeliar Wetlands. While the government successfully appealed this decision and received conditional approval, the environmental concerns remained politically potent. [[5]](https://www.epa.wa.gov.au/proposals/swan-river-pedestrian-bridge-linking-east-perth-foreshore-burswood-peninsula) The Save Beeliar Wetlands action group maintained sustained community opposition, and Indigenous heritage groups raised concerns about the route's impact on Noongar cultural sites.

**Cost Escalation**: The project faced severe budget pressures. Stage 2 (the tunnel component through sensitive areas) was estimated at \$5.8 billion alone—nearly three times the original project budget. [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Freight_Link) These escalations made completion increasingly economically untenable.

**Political Change**: The McGowan government's election in March 2017 created an insurmountable political barrier. The new administration viewed the Freight Link as a failed project from the previous government and prioritized redirecting funds toward other infrastructure priorities, including public transport. [[7]](https://www.fullyloaded.com.au/controversial-perth-project-officially-in-the-can/)

## Current Status and Aftermath

The project was formally cancelled in January 2025 following legislative amendment by the Western Australian government, officially removing it from the infrastructure pipeline. [[7]](https://www.fullyloaded.com.au/controversial-perth-project-officially-in-the-can/) The cancellation was permanent and definitive: the land reserved for stages 8 and 9 remains unreleased, but the political consensus against the project is so complete that resurrection appears impossible. This fate mirrors an earlier instance—the Fremantle Eastern Bypass, cancelled in 2001, which removed critical land corridors that would have supported subsequent road infrastructure.

What survived the Freight Link's failure: the port of Fremantle remains under operational pressure with freight routing through Perth's urban streets, and the state government's commitment to alternative freight solutions (rail-based alternatives and port efficiency improvements) intensified without sufficient funding implementation.
